## Physics concept of the day: sampling rate and the shrinking step

**ML first.** Picture a video model at a higher frame rate. Consecutive frames become nearly identical, so most of the "next frame" is just "this frame plus a bit of motion." The interesting part, the change in motion, becomes a tiny correction on top.

**The physics.** Each orbit is cut into $N$ snapshots, so the gap between snapshots is $\Delta t = P/N$, where $P$ is the orbital period. The curvature of the path shows up in the second difference $x_{t+1} - 2x_t + x_{t-1}$. That is acceleration times $\Delta t^2$, which is `accel_step`:

$$a_{\text{step}} = \left(\tfrac{2\pi}{N}\right)^2 \frac{a^3}{(1+m_2)\,r^2}$$

Here $a$ is the semi-major axis (the orbit's size) and $r$ is the current separation. Doubling $N$ shrinks this term **4×**: the gravity signal per step gets quieter relative to plain constant-velocity extrapolation.

**Tie-back.** Going from N=64 to N=128, the model still encodes `accel_step` well. Its headroom-normalised margin is about 0.72–0.76 at both N. But its linear readout of $0.5/r^2$ drops 0.070–0.14 across four seeds (`f-...f8e8`, `f-...68d6`).

Whether the quieter per-step signal *causes* that loss is an **open hypothesis**, not a lab result. It is still a good lens for reading the next results.

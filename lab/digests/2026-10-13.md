## Physics concept of the day: snapshot rate vs. physical force

**ML intuition.** Picture predicting the next frame of a video. At a high frame rate, each frame barely differs from the last, so "continue the current motion" gets you nearly all the way. The model can succeed by learning *per-frame* change, without knowing the real-world units.

**Physics.** The model sees N snapshots per orbit, so the time between snapshots is $\Delta t = P/N$, where $P$ is the orbital period. The quantity it needs for the next step is

$$a_{\text{step}} = \frac{F}{m_2}\,\Delta t^2 = \left(\tfrac{2\pi}{N}\right)^2 \frac{a^3}{(1+m_2)\,r^2}$$

Here $F$ is the gravitational force, $m_2$ is the secondary's mass, $a$ is the orbit's semi-major axis (its size) and $r$ is the current separation. Physical force $F = m_2/r^2$ lacks the $\Delta t^2$ and $a^3$ factors. To read force out of the model, it would have to "undo" those factors. Going from N=64 to N=256 shrinks $a_{\text{step}}$ by $(64/256)^2 = 1/16$, so the per-step correction becomes a tiny nudge on top of straight-line motion.

**Tonight's numbers.** The headroom-normalised accel_step margin stayed about 0.7 at every N. Force fell from 0.247/0.234 (N=64) to 0.108/0.094 (N=128) to 0.040/0.033 (N=256) on seeds 22–23. The model keeps the step-based quantity and loses the unit-converted one.

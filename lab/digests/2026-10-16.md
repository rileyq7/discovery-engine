## Physics concept of the day: snapshots per orbit

**ML bridge.** Think of the same video recorded at 64 fps versus 128 fps. The content is identical, but frame-to-frame differences are smaller at the higher rate.

**The physics.** Each orbit is sampled $N$ times per period $P$, so the time between snapshots is $\Delta t = P/N$. The model predicts snapshot to snapshot. What it needs is the second difference of position, which works out to the physical acceleration times $\Delta t^2$:

$$a_{\text{step}} = \left(\tfrac{2\pi}{N}\right)^2 \frac{a^3}{(1+m_2)\,r^2}$$

Here $a$ is the semi-major axis (orbit size), $r$ is the current separation, and $m_2$ is the secondary's mass. Doubling $N$ from 64 to 128 shrinks that prefactor fourfold, from about 0.0096 to about 0.0024. The physical force, $m_2/r^2$ with $m_2 = 0.5$ fixed, is *identical* in both datasets. Only the sampling changes.

**Tie-back.** accel_step stays about equally decodable at both N (R² 0.924–0.927 in `f-...fd3b`). Physical force does not: seed 22's gap is 0.143 at d64. So finer sampling seems to make the model represent $1/r^2$ less linearly, even though the underlying physics is the same. Why that happens is an open research question, not established physics.

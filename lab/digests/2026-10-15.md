## Physics concept of the day: snapshot rate and the shrinking step

**ML first.** Think of N as a video's frame rate. Doubling the frame rate halves the motion between frames, so whatever the model must predict per frame gets smaller.

**Physics.** The model predicts the next position from the second difference $x_{t+1} - 2x_t + x_{t-1}$, which is acceleration × $\Delta t^2$. Here $\Delta t = P/N$ is the time between snapshots and $P$ is the orbital period. That quantity is `accel_step`:

$$a_{\text{step}} = \left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\right)^2 \frac{a^3}{(1+m_2)\,r^2}$$

$a$ is the orbit's size (semi-major axis) and $r$ is the current separation. The factor $(2\pi/N)^2$ is about 0.0096 at N=64 and about 0.0024 at N=128. **Doubling N makes the per-step signal four times smaller.**

There is also a subtle difference between the two targets. With $m_2$ fixed, force is $0.5/r^2$, which depends only on $r$. `accel_step` carries an extra $a^3$. The model's task only rewards `accel_step`, so it has no reason to cleanly separate out orbit size.

**Tie-back.** In `f-20261014-080108-12be` the loss ratio dropped about fivefold while force $R^2$ moved only about 0.014. The model got better at its task without moving closer to "physical force", and that is exactly what this picture predicts. This echoes Vafa et al. (2025), where good prediction did not imply a Newtonian internal law.

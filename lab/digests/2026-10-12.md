## What the lab did and why

The lab completed 15 cycles and 101 runs, all finished except one. It used 2.4 of its 6 compute-hours.

**Which gravity quantity does it encode?** Early in the evening the lab spread or fixed $m_2$ so that force and `accel_step` would come apart. The agent's idea was that if the force ceiling came from missing $m_2$ information, fixing $m_2$ should lift it. It didn't.

**Which law?** The fits kept coming out as $a(a/r - c)$ with $c \approx 0.5$. In the agent's words, every high-R² fit "has the form a(a/r - c) with c near 0.5, which is the vis-viva quantity. At e <= 0.7 that form may not be separable from a^3/r^2." The lab tried three remedies:
- more eccentricity
- a bigger model (6 layers, d_model=128)
- longer training (4000 steps)

**When does structure form?** This was most of the night's E5 work: matched-seed pairs at N=64 and N=256, read from per-checkpoint trajectories. The agent moved to $e \le 0.85$ because at $e \le 0.5$ "accel_step's random control sits at 0.71-0.78, so final margins are only 0.12-0.17." The learning-rate halving tested whether "timing tracks optimization progress rather than anything about the data."

## Physics concept of the day: why $r^{-1}$ and $r^{-2}$ look alike

**ML first.** Two features that are almost collinear on your training distribution can't be told apart by any fit. You need data where they diverge.

**Physics.** The true per-snapshot law is $a^3/r^2$, where $a$ is orbit size and $r$ is the current distance. The fits keep finding $a(a/r - \tfrac12)$, which is $\tfrac{a^2}{2}(2/r - 1/a)$. That has the same $r$-dependence as $v^2$ in the vis-viva equation $v^2 = G(M+m_2)(2/r - 1/a)$.

Write $r = a(1+x)$, where $x$ is the fractional distance from the average radius. Then, by my own expansion:
- $a^3/r^2 \approx a(1 - 2x + 3x^2)$
- $2\,a(a/r - \tfrac12) \approx a(1 - 2x + 2x^2)$

They agree to first order. At low eccentricity $x$ stays small, so the two forms are near-collinear. At pericentre with $e = 0.85$ ($x = -0.85$) they differ by about 3.6× (my arithmetic).

That's why $c = 0.499$ and $0.539$ in `f-20261012-002808-0644` is ambiguous. It's also why the lab pushed to high $e$, where the probe then hit its R² ≈ 0.70 ceiling.

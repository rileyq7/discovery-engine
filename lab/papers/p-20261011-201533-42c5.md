# Discovery Engine: running draft

## Results

The lab has five replicated findings. Each rests on two training seeds, and together they cover ten completed runs across seeds 2–9. Three findings support their hypotheses and two refute them. Throughout, a probe counts only if $R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}} \geq 0.1$, where the control is a randomly initialised model with the same architecture. `val_mse_ratio` is model MSE divided by the MSE of constant-velocity extrapolation, so values below 1 beat that baseline.

### Gravity-related structure forms before the model beats the baseline

**Coarse checkpoints (every 250 steps).** In seeds 2 and 3, the gravity-related probes already beat their controls at the first trained checkpoint, step 250 [f-20261011-201533-0653]:

- **`accel_step`** (layer 4), which targets $|a_{\text{step}}| = (2\pi/N)^2\, a^3 / \big((1+m_2)\, r^2\big)$: margins of 0.158 (seed 2) and 0.127 (seed 3).
- **`distance`** (layer 1): margins of 0.597 and 0.681.

At step 250, `val_mse_ratio` was still 0.87. It first fell below 0.1 at step 1500 in both seeds, at 0.0858 and 0.0985, and finished at about 0.011 [f-20261011-201533-0653].

**Dense checkpoints (every 25 steps).** The 250-step spacing could not tell whether structure forms before the model starts to beat the baseline or at the same time. We tested this in seeds 4 and 5, using a different configuration with checkpoints every 25 steps [f-20261011-203659-4e2f]. Both seeds showed the same order of events:

1. **Steps 25–50:** `distance` and `energy` formed at step 25. By step 50 the `distance` margins were 0.65 and 0.63, while `val_mse_ratio` was still between 5 and 83.
2. **Step 100:** the `accel_step` margin first reached the threshold at layer 4 (0.115 for seed 4, 0.108 for seed 5). `val_mse_ratio` was still 1.96 and 1.84.
3. **Step 175:** `val_mse_ratio` is first reported below 1 (0.736 and 0.878). The logged trajectories skip steps 125 and 150, so the actual crossing lies somewhere between steps 125 and 175.
4. **Later or never:** force formed last or not at all (see the next subsection).

So `accel_step` structure could be read at least one checkpoint before the model beat constant-velocity extrapolation [f-20261011-203659-4e2f]. The dense-checkpoint runs ended at `val_mse_ratio` 0.141 (seed 4) and 0.166 (seed 5). They are less well trained than the seed 2 and 3 runs, which ended at about 0.011 [f-20261011-201533-0653, f-20261011-203659-4e2f].

### Force versus accel_step

The lab protocol requires comparing the physical force probe, $|F| = m_2/r^2$, with the per-snapshot acceleration probe `accel_step`. In every one of the ten runs, `accel_step` has the higher raw $R^2$:

- **`accel_step` raw $R^2$:** 0.707–0.935.
- **Force raw $R^2$:** 0.452–0.738.

How the two compare *by margin* depends on the configuration (table below):

- **Dense-checkpoint runs:** `accel_step` has the larger margin.
- **Full-eccentricity training runs:** `accel_step` has the larger margin in both seeds.
- **4000-step runs (default and wide $m_2$):** force ends with the larger margin.
- **OOD training runs:** the seeds split.

The control $R^2$ values also differ a lot between configurations. In the full-eccentricity runs, the `accel_step` controls are 0.35–0.38, against 0.71–0.76 elsewhere. Margins should therefore be compared with care across rows.

**Formation timing.** In every run where formation timing could be resolved, force never formed before `accel_step`:

- **Seed 2:** both formed at step 250 [f-20261011-201533-0653].
- **Seed 3:** force formed at step 750, `accel_step` at step 250 [f-20261011-201533-0653].
- **Seed 4:** force never reached the threshold [f-20261011-203659-4e2f].
- **Seed 5:** force formed at step 450, `accel_step` at step 100 [f-20261011-203659-4e2f].

**Refuted: widening the mass range does not weaken the force probe** [f-20261011-203628-5cd9]. We predicted that widening $m_2$ to $[0.01, 1]$ would pull force apart from `accel_step` and make the force probe fall behind. It did not:

- **Force kept the larger margin** in both seeds: 0.243 and 0.235, against 0.205 and 0.175 for `accel_step`.
- **Force margins did not drop** by $\geq 0.05$ relative to the default-range runs. They were about the same or slightly higher.
- **The `accel_step` probe still worked.** Its margin stayed above 0.1, and it led on raw $R^2$ by 0.18–0.25.
- **Both models still beat the baseline**, at `val_mse_ratio` 0.0114 and 0.0119.

**The margin criterion favours force.** The `accel_step` control $R^2$ there is already 0.71–0.75, which caps its possible margin at roughly 0.25–0.29. Force has about 0.51–0.55 of headroom. Measured as a fraction of the available headroom, $(R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}})/(1 - R^2_{\text{control}})$, `accel_step` leads clearly: 0.70 vs 0.48 in seed 2 and 0.71 vs 0.42 in seed 3 [f-20261011-203628-5cd9]. The refutation therefore shows only that the model keeps some force-specific signal beyond the control. It does not show that force is the model's primary representation.

### Where in the network each quantity is read out

Three probes have the same best layer in all ten runs [f-20261011-201533-0653, f-20261011-203628-5cd9, f-20261011-203659-4e2f, f-20261011-203723-b7de, f-20261011-211551-d718]:

- **`accel_step`:** layer 4.
- **`distance`:** layer 1.
- **`energy`:** layer 1.

The best layer for force varies between runs:

- **Layer 4:** seed 2 (both experiments), seeds 4, 5 and 9.
- **Layer 1:** seed 3 (both experiments), seeds 6, 7 and 8.

`distance` margins are large throughout. For example, final $R^2$ was 0.889 against a control of 0.258 in seed 2, and 0.838 against 0.126 in seed 9 [f-20261011-201533-0653, f-20261011-211551-d718]. Force and `accel_step` both scale as $1/r^2$, so part of the force signal may simply be distance information read at a different layer [f-20261011-203628-5cd9].

### Out-of-distribution transfer: the high-eccentricity failure was a coverage gap

**Initial test (seeds 6 and 7, trained on a restricted eccentricity range)** [f-20261011-203723-b7de]. In-distribution, `val_mse_ratio` was 0.0151 (seed 7) and 0.0157 (seed 6).

- **Low mass ratio ($m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$): transfer passed.** `val_mse_ratio` was 0.057 and 0.055, about 3.6× the in-distribution value. The best force $R^2$ held at 0.75–0.77.
- **High eccentricity ($e \in [0.5, 0.8]$): transfer failed the pre-registered 10× criterion.** `val_mse_ratio` was 0.270 and 0.249, about 16–18× the in-distribution value. The best force $R^2$ fell to 0.40 and 0.34, from 0.74 and 0.69 in-distribution.
- **Every two-body ratio stayed below 1.** On high-$e$ systems the model still did about 4× better than constant-velocity extrapolation.
- **Three-body systems are exploratory only.** Their ratios were 72 and 70.

**Follow-up (seeds 8 and 9, trained on $e \in [0, 0.8]$)** [f-20261011-211551-d718]. Once the training data covers high eccentricities, the prediction gap disappears in both seeds:

- **High $e$ matches in-distribution.** Held-out high-$e$ `val_mse_ratio` was 0.0187 (seed 9) and 0.0189 (seed 8). That is about 1.05× and 1.01× the in-distribution values of 0.0178 and 0.0187, against a pre-registered threshold of 3×.
- **High $e$ is no worse than low $e$.** Held-out low-$e$ systems scored 0.0185 and 0.0215.
- **Probes still beat their controls.** At the last checkpoint every probe margin was at least 0.2 (see the table above).

Taken together, the earlier 16–18× gap reflects which eccentricities were covered in training, not a missing law-like predictor [f-20261011-203723-b7de, f-20261011-211551-d718].

**Prediction recovers, but the force probe does not.** Even with full-eccentricity training, the best force probe $R^2$ on held-out high-$e$ systems is only 0.366–0.377, against 0.729–0.802 on held-out low-$e$ systems. Prediction error is the same on both [f-20261011-211551-d718]. This repeats the drop to 0.34–0.40 seen in the restricted-range runs [f-20261011-203723-b7de]. None of the OOD evaluation sets have random-init controls, so this drop cannot be read as a loss of margin, and it is currently unexplained.

### Null and negative results

- **Two hypotheses refuted.**
  - Widening the mass range did not make force fall behind `accel_step` [f-20261011-203628-5cd9].
  - Transfer from restricted-$e$ training to high eccentricity failed the 10× criterion [f-20261011-203723-b7de]. The follow-up traces this to training coverage [f-20261011-211551-d718].
- **Force sometimes does not form at all.** In seed 4, force never beat its control: final margin 0.068 [f-20261011-203659-4e2f].
- **The probe and prediction disagree at high $e$.** With full-eccentricity training, the force probe $R^2$ on high-$e$ systems stays around half its low-$e$ value, even though prediction error is equal [f-20261011-211551-d718].
- **No symbolic equations recovered.** The `equations` field is empty in all ten evidence runs [f-20261011-201533-0653, f-20261011-203628-5cd9, f-20261011-203659-4e2f, f-20261011-203723-b7de, f-20261011-211551-d718]. We have no evidence yet that an explicit $1/r^2$ law can be extracted from the residual stream.
- **No `velocity` probe results.** No finding so far reports any.
- **"First beats baseline" is not a stable event.** Seed 5's `val_mse_ratio` rose back above 1 at step 225 (1.057) [f-20261011-203659-4e2f].

### Caveats

- **Margins at formation are thin.** `accel_step` margins at formation are only slightly above 0.1: 0.127 and 0.158 at step 250, and 0.108 and 0.115 at step 100 [f-20261011-201533-0653, f-20261011-203659-4e2f]. With a threshold of 0.12, the dense-run crossing would move to about step 175. It would then coincide with `val_mse_ratio` < 1 instead of coming before it [f-20261011-203659-4e2f].
- **Early-forming probes may reflect input geometry.** `distance` and `energy` can be read out while the model is still far worse than the baseline. This may be geometry that is easy to read from the input embedding, not computation related to gravity [f-20261011-201533-0653, f-20261011-203659-4e2f].
- **Trajectories have gaps.**
  - The 250-step runs log no entries at steps 500, 1250, 2000, 2750 or 3500 [f-20261011-201533-0653].
  - The dense runs log nothing at steps 125 or 150 [f-20261011-203659-4e2f].
- **Some runs carry no timing information.** Every probe is recorded as forming at the final checkpoint: step 4000 in the wide-$m_2$ runs, step 3000 in the OOD runs, and step 2500 in the full-$e$ runs [f-20261011-203628-5cd9, f-20261011-203723-b7de, f-20261011-211551-d718]. These runs say nothing about when structure forms.
- **The full-$e$ runs stopped early.** Both stopped at step 2500 of 3000, having used 0.13 of the 0.2 h budget. `val_mse_ratio` was still falling, from 0.034 to 0.018 over the last 500 steps. The reason for the early stop is unknown [f-20261011-211551-d718].
- **Limited sweep.** The full-$e$ result rests on two seeds, one data seed and one architecture ($L = 4$, $d = 64$, $N = 64$) [f-20261011-211551-d718].
- **Correlation in time is not use.** None of these results shows that the model *uses* the probed quantities to make its predictions.

### Open questions

- **Is the force signal distinct from `accel_step` and distance?** A force probe with `accel_step` and `distance` regressed out would settle whether force-specific signal survives. If no margin remains, the wide-mass-range refutation would be overturned [f-20261011-203628-5cd9].
- **Is the ordering robust?** It should be retested with checkpoints every 5–10 steps, a stricter margin, more seeds, and `distance` regressed out of `accel_step` [f-20261011-203659-4e2f].
- **Why does the force probe degrade at high $e$ when prediction does not?** Random-init controls on the OOD evaluation sets are needed before the 0.37 vs 0.73–0.80 gap can be read as a representational difference [f-20261011-211551-d718].
- **Does the high-$e$ result hold at convergence?** The full-$e$ runs should be trained to the full 3000 steps, and with a further seed, to check that no high-$e$ gap above 3× opens [f-20261011-211551-d718].
- **Should the margin criterion be normalised by headroom?** The current criterion is biased against probes with high controls, such as `accel_step` [f-20261011-203628-5cd9].

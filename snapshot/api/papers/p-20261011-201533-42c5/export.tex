\section{Abstract}

We ask whether small transformers trained on simulated two-body orbits ($G = M = 1$) encode the physical force law $|F| = m_2/r^2$ or a substitute that is specific to the task, and when any such structure forms. We fit linear probes to the residual stream and score each one against best-of-several random-model controls. Each probe compares physical force with the per-snapshot acceleration $(F/m_2)\,dt^2$ (\texttt{accel\_step}) that next-step prediction needs. The record holds 21 replicated findings (13 supported, 8 refuted) from 54 evidence runs, 4 of which were cut short by the budget stop. \texttt{accel\_step} beats its control in every setting tested, usually at the last layer. At $N = 64$, force mostly fails its control, with final margins of 0.000–0.080 [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}]. The \texttt{accel\_step} code forms in a narrow window of roughly 50–140 training steps that barely moves with $N$. Halving the learning rate delayed it by only 10–30 steps [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}, \texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}, \texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}]. Late in training, linear decodability stops tracking prediction: \texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ rose by at most 0.025 while \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} fell 5.5–6.8× [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}]. The main negative result is that symbolic fits to the probe direction give $r^{-1}$, not $r^{-2}$, in all four seeds fitted [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}, \texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}]. An artefact of the fitter has not yet been ruled out.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data generation}

All orbits are simulated with REBOUND in units where $G = 1$ and the primary mass is $M = 1$. The gravitational force on body 2 is therefore $|F| = m_2/r^2$, where $m_2$ is the mass ratio. Varying $M$ would add nothing. Positions are sampled at fixed phase intervals, so absolute mass and time scale cannot be identified from them.

Each orbit is sampled at a fixed number of snapshots per orbit, $N$ (\texttt{steps\_per\_orbit}). The time between snapshots is therefore $\Delta t = P/N$, which grows with orbit size ($\Delta t^2 \propto a^3$). The model never sees physical time.

Three-body systems are hierarchical: the third body sits at 3.5–6× the inner semi-major axis. They are used only as an evaluation set for models trained on two-body systems.

The configuration schema adds these fields to the specification:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{data.eccentricity\_range}
\item \texttt{data.data\_seed}
\item \texttt{probes.control\_margin}
\item \texttt{probes.patching}
\item \texttt{probes.symbolic}
\item \texttt{eval\_sets}, for out-of-distribution evaluation
\end{itemize}

Unknown fields are rejected. The training ranges differ between the cited experiments:

\begin{itemize}
\item Some runs train on the default range, which reaches $e \leq 0.5$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item Some runs widen the mass ratio to $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item Some runs widen eccentricity to $e \in [0, 0.8]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\item Some runs widen both, to $e \in [0, 0.9]$ and $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. Both of these runs share data seed 0.
\end{itemize}

The held-out evaluation sets are:

\begin{itemize}
\item low mass ratio, $m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$
\item high eccentricity, $e \in [0.5, 0.8]$
\item three-body systems [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]
\item held-out low-$e$ and high-$e$ splits, used for the wide-eccentricity runs [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tokens, model and prediction target}

Each token is one time step. It holds the $xy$ positions of up to three bodies (6 numbers) in the centre-of-mass frame, with absent bodies set to zero. Because every system uses the same token format, a model trained on two bodies can be evaluated on three-body sets.

The model predicts the normalised displacement to the next step. All evidence runs use one transformer architecture: $L = 4$ layers, width $d = 64$, and $N = 64$ snapshots per orbit [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Prediction quality is reported as \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio}. This is the model's MSE divided by the MSE of constant-velocity extrapolation, $\hat{x}_{t+1} = 2x_t - x_{t-1}$. Both are computed in physical position units over steps $t \geq 1$ on the same held-out systems. A value below 1 means the model beats the baseline.

\subsection{Training}

The lab's default first-experiment (E1) configuration trains for 5k steps at batch size 128 and learning rate $10^{-3}$. Training runs on a laptop GPU (MPS) and is kept CPU-friendly.

Checkpoint spacing varies between experiments. One formation experiment probes every 250 steps [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}]. A dense-checkpoint experiment probes every 25 steps early in training [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].

Every finding is replicated on two training seeds.

\textbf{Budget hard stop.} A runaway guard stops a run once it reaches 2× its seed's share of \texttt{budget.max\_compute\_hours}. The clock starts at the first training step, so dataset generation does not count. A stopped run is checkpointed and probed at its stop step. It records \texttt{steps\_completed} and \texttt{steps\_planned}, and its \texttt{completion} is the fraction of planned steps it trained. Runs below 80\% completion do not count toward replication.

Two of the 12 evidence runs were cut short by an earlier version of this guard. That version stopped at 1.2× the budget share and counted dataset generation against it. Both runs are in [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]: seed 9 stopped at completion 0.867 (step 2640 of 3000) and seed 8 at 0.9 (step 2714). Under that version the probes were taken at the last regular checkpoint, step 2500, not at the stop step. Their \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still falling when training stopped.

\subsection{Probe targets}

Linear probes read five quantities from the residual stream at every layer:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{distance}
\item relative \texttt{velocity}
\item \texttt{energy}, which is constant per system
\item physical \texttt{force}, $|F| = m_2/r^2$
\item the per-snapshot acceleration \texttt{accel\_step}:
\end{itemize}

\[|a_{\text{step}}| = \frac{F}{m_2}\,\Delta t^2 = \left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\right)^2 \frac{a^3}{(1+m_2)\,r^2}.\]

Next-step prediction needs \texttt{accel\_step}, not the physical force. A weak \texttt{force} probe on its own could therefore mean the model learned a per-snapshot version of gravity, not that it learned no gravity. For this reason \texttt{force} and \texttt{accel\_step} are always compared.

A unit test checks two properties of \texttt{accel\_step}:

\begin{itemize}
\item It equals the second difference of positions, to within 1\%.
\item It is not proportional to the physical force across orbit sizes.
\end{itemize}

\texttt{velocity} probe results do not appear in any cited finding.

\subsection{Probe protocol and random-model controls}

Probes run on 300 held-out systems, at every other time step from $t = 8$. Systems are split 70/30 into probe-train and probe-test sets, so rows from the same orbit never appear on both sides.

Each probe is a ridge regression (RidgeCV over $\alpha \in [10^{-3}, 10^{4}]$) on standardised inputs and outputs. $R^2$ is averaged across output dimensions.

Every probe has a control: the same probe fitted on a randomly initialised model of the same architecture. There is one control per layer, because a random model does not change across checkpoints. A probe result counts only if

\[R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}} \geq 0.1 \quad (\texttt{probes.control\_margin}).\]

For each target, the lab reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the best layer
\item its $R^2$ and control $R^2$
\item \texttt{formed\_at\_step}, the first checkpoint at which the margin reaches the threshold (null if it never does)
\end{itemize}

The control $R^2$ for \texttt{accel\_step} is high under the default ranges (0.71–0.75). This caps the margin \texttt{accel\_step} can reach. One finding therefore also reports a post hoc, headroom-normalised score, $(R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}})/(1 - R^2_{\text{control}})$, but its pre-registered criterion uses the raw margin [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].

Probes on the out-of-distribution evaluation sets have no random controls. Their $R^2$ values therefore cannot be read as margins [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}, \texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Only the final checkpoint's activations are saved to disk, as float16. Activations for earlier checkpoints are recomputed from the saved weights when needed.

\subsection{Symbolic regression}

When \texttt{probes.symbolic} is enabled, PySR fits a closed-form expression to the best-layer probe direction for \texttt{force} and for \texttt{accel\_step}. The \texttt{accel\_step} fit uses the inputs $r$, $m_2$ and $a$. Each fit reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the equation
\item whether the true law was recovered (\texttt{law\_recovered})
\item \texttt{fit\_error\_vs\_true\_law}
\end{itemize}

Symbolic fits were run at the final checkpoint only, and only in the runs behind [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. These are the only evidence runs with fitted equations.

\subsection{Activation patching}

The pipeline also supports activation patching. Heads are ranked by a force probe on each head's contribution to the residual stream. The top three heads, plus the lowest-ranked head as a control, are each patched with that head's output on a different system, and the relative increase in next-step MSE is recorded. None of the cited findings report patching results.

\section{Results}

The lab has 21 citable findings, all replicated across seeds. Of these, 13 are supported and 8 are refuted. They rest on 54 evidence runs. The budget hard stop cut 4 of those runs short, and we flag them wherever their numbers are used. The evidence for the earliest findings lists no \texttt{control\_spread}, which means those runs were judged against a single random control. Later runs use best-of-3 or best-of-5 controls.

\subsection{Per-snapshot acceleration is decodable in every run; physical force depends on the run}

The \texttt{accel\_step} probe beat its random-model control in every evidence run. Its target is the per-snapshot acceleration $(F/m_2)\,dt^2 = (2\pi/N)^2 a^3/((1+m_2) r^2)$.

The physical-force probe, $|F| = m_2/r^2$, behaved differently:

\begin{itemize}
\item In runs of 2500–4000 steps it usually beat its control, sometimes by a larger raw margin than \texttt{accel\_step}.
\item In 300–600-step runs with dense checkpoints it usually failed.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Raw margin understates \texttt{accel\_step}.} The \texttt{accel\_step} random controls are often high, which caps its raw margin. A headroom-normalised margin, $(R^2 - \text{control})/(1 - \text{control})$, corrects for this:

\begin{itemize}
\item With $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$, force had the larger raw margin, but on the normalised margin \texttt{accel\_step} led: 0.70 vs 0.48 and 0.71 vs 0.42 [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item In the $L = 2$, $d = 32$ cell, force led on raw margin in all three seeds (counting seed 0). On the normalised margin the ordering split:
\item \texttt{accel\_step} led in seed 1 (0.46 vs 0.36).
\item Force led in seed 2 (0.42 vs 0.37) [\texttt{f-20261013-000220-ab2d}].
\item In the 6-layer model, force's normalised margin was 0.41 in both seeds, against 0.66 and 0.61 for \texttt{accel\_step} [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}].
\item At $N = 16$ with 3000 steps, the \texttt{accel\_step} control reached 0.80–0.81, the highest the lab has seen [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Force signal in short runs.}

\begin{itemize}
\item At $N = 64$ with seeds 62–64, force never beat its control, while \texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ reached 0.890–0.902 against controls of 0.724–0.746 [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}].
\item The two seeds in the 65–69 replication that did clear the bar did so only just: margins of 0.103 and 0.105 against a 0.1 bar, with control spread 0.034–0.048 [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Early force transient.} A brief force signal appears early in training and then fades:

\begin{itemize}
\item 0.166–0.189 at step 10 under best-of-5 controls, decaying to about 0.00–0.02 by steps 40–50 [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}];
\item 0.148–0.159 at step 10 in seeds 62–64 [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}];
\item 0.106–0.127 at $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}];
\item 0.12–0.19 at steps 10–25 in seeds 65–69, falling to 0.002–0.10 by steps 40–55 [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Force over longer training.}

\begin{itemize}
\item In the $L = 2$ cell, the force margin kept rising from step 500 to step 3000: 0.112 to 0.221, and 0.136 to 0.255. The \texttt{accel\_step} margin levelled off at 0.13–0.15 by step 1500 [\texttt{f-20261013-000220-ab2d}].
\item At $e \in [0, 0.8]$, force formed late (steps 250–400) and was still rising at step 600 [\texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Possible confound.} Force and \texttt{accel\_step} are collinear through $1/r^2$, so part of the force signal may be distance information [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}]. In the $L = 2$ cell, force peaks at layer 1, the same layer where distance is decoded at $R^2$ 0.84–0.85 [\texttt{f-20261013-000220-ab2d}]. No run has yet regressed distance out of the force target.

\textbf{Where each quantity is read out:}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\texttt{accel\_step}} usually peaks at the last layer: layer 4 in most 4-layer models, and layer 6 in the 6-layer model [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}, \texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}]. The exceptions are:
\item seed 67, which peaked at layer 2 [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}];
\item the $N = 16$, 3000-step runs, which peaked at layer 2 [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}];
\item the 2-layer model, which peaked at layer 2, its last layer [\texttt{f-20261013-000220-ab2d}].
\item \textbf{\texttt{distance}} peaks at layer 1 or 2 [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}, \texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}].
\item \textbf{Force} has no stable best layer. It ranges from layer 0 to layer 4 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}, \texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}] and drifts across layers 1–6 in the 6-layer model [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{When the \texttt{accel\_step} code forms}

The \texttt{accel\_step} code forms within a fairly narrow window of training steps. The loss at that point varies a lot between settings.

\textbf{Early runs.}

\begin{itemize}
\item The code was present at the first checkpoint (step 250), 1250 steps before \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} fell below 0.1 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}].
\item With 25-step checkpoints, it formed at step 100 in both seeds, while \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still 1.96 and 1.84 [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{$N = 64$, $e \le 0.5$.} The persistent 0.1-bar crossing came at:

\begin{itemize}
\item steps 140, 140 and 100 [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}];
\item steps 85–90 [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}];
\item steps 90, 100 and 100, with half-final-margin steps of 80, 60 and 90 [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}].
\end{itemize}

In each case, the crossing came 40–90 steps before the model beat constant-velocity extrapolation.

\textbf{Changing $N$ barely moves the formation step.}

\begin{itemize}
\item At $N = 16$, formation came at steps 90, 75 and 80, within 5–15 steps of the seed-matched $N = 64$ runs. But \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} dropped below 1 at step 20, so the "forms before beating the baseline" ordering reverses [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].
\item At $N = 256$ and $e \in [0, 0.85]$, formation came at steps 60, 70 and 60, even though the model never beat the baseline (final \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} 2.03–2.37) [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}].
\item The matched $N = 64$ arm formed at steps 60, 60 and 70 [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Changing eccentricity.}

\begin{itemize}
\item At $e \in [0, 0.7]$, formation came at steps 60, 60 and 70 [\texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}].
\item At $e \in [0, 0.8]$, it came at steps 50–100 [\texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Comparing against the baseline is unreliable across $N$.} "Beats the baseline" depends on how strong the constant-velocity baseline is at each $N$, so it should not be used to time formation across $N$ [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].

\textbf{The loss level at formation is not fixed.} Formation came at:

\begin{itemize}
\item 2.0–2.9\% of the step-0 value at $N = 64$, $e \le 0.5$ [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}];
\item 8–10\% at $e \in [0, 0.85]$ [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}];
\item about 15–18\% at $N = 256$ [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}].
\end{itemize}

Formation timing therefore tracks a window of training steps, not a fixed fraction of the loss reduction.

\textbf{A recurring reporting lag.} \texttt{formed\_at\_step} sometimes trails the trajectory by one checkpoint. In seed 68 it reports 80, while the margin holds from step 65 [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}]. In the $N = 16$, 3000-step runs it reports 1000, while the trajectory first crosses 0.1 at step 500 [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}]. Where the two disagree, the findings use trajectory values.

\textbf{Three timing hypotheses were refuted:}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Stricter bar at $N = 64$} [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].
\item In 2 of 3 seeds, the \texttt{accel\_step} margin never reached a 0.15 bar; it levelled off at 0.134–0.135.
\item The early force spike survived best-of-5 controls.
\item \textbf{Halving the learning rate to 0.0005} [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}].
\item Formation moved from steps 60/60/70 to 70/80/100. That is +10/+20/+30 steps, not the roughly 2× delay that proportional scaling predicts.
\item Training did slow: final \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was about 0.22, against about 0.13 at lr 0.001.
\item \textbf{Pre-registered $N = 64$ window} [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\item Only 2 of 5 fresh seeds (65–69) fell inside the window; at least 4 of 5 were required.
\item Seeds 67 and 68 formed for good at step 65, and seeds 65, 66 and 69 at step 90. Half-final steps ranged from 40 to 70.
\item These runs used 5-step checkpoints, against 10-step checkpoints earlier, so part of the shift to earlier steps may be resolution.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Eccentricity sets the control level, not decoding quality}

The layer-4 \texttt{accel\_step} random-control $R^2$ falls as the eccentricity range widens:

\begin{itemize}
\item 0.71–0.77 at $e \le 0.5$;
\item 0.543, 0.553 and 0.559 at $e \le 0.7$ [\texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}];
\item 0.372, 0.396 and 0.375 at $e \le 0.8$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}];
\item 0.25–0.35 at $e \le 0.85$ [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}].
\end{itemize}

Raw \texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ falls with it:

\begin{itemize}
\item 0.80–0.81 at $e \le 0.7$ [\texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}];
\item 0.670–0.672 at $e \le 0.8$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}];
\item 0.51–0.59 at $e \le 0.85$ [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}].
\end{itemize}

The larger raw margins at high $e$ therefore reflect more headroom, not a stronger representation.

The headroom-normalised margin also declines:

\begin{itemize}
\item 0.475, 0.455 and 0.475 at $e \le 0.8$. All three seeds fall in the pre-registered [0.44, 0.53] band, between the cited \textasciitilde{}0.55–0.58 at $e \le 0.7$ and \textasciitilde{}0.38–0.41 at $e \le 0.85$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}].
\item 0.34–0.40 at $e \le 0.85$, by that finding's own measure [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}].
\item Force reaches only 0.15–0.21 at $e \le 0.8$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}] and about 0.16–0.22 at $e \le 0.7$ [\texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}].
\end{itemize}

Three ceiling values cannot tell a gradual decline from one that steepens; per unit of $e$, the drop looks steeper above 0.8. The reference points also come from separate experiments that share one data seed [\texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}, \texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}].

\subsection{Coarser sampling does not strengthen the \texttt{accel\_step} code}

\textbf{The prediction.} At $N = 16$ trained for 3000 steps, the \texttt{accel\_step} margin would rise above the $N = 64$ reference [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}].

\textbf{The result: refuted in both seeds.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ was 0.941 vs control 0.810 (margin 0.131) and 0.936 vs 0.795 (margin 0.140).
\item The normalised margins of 0.69 and 0.68 fall below the predicted 0.8 and level with the \textasciitilde{}0.70 at $N = 64$.
\item Force led on raw margin: 0.236 and 0.275.
\item Both models beat the baseline by a wide margin (\texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} 0.0007).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Caveat.} The $N = 64$ reference runs used single controls and may differ in training length, so a matched arm is still needed [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}].

\subsection{Refuted hypotheses about the force probe}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A wider mass range did not make force fall behind \texttt{accel\_step} on raw margin.} The normalised margin still favoured \texttt{accel\_step} (see above) [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item \textbf{Fixing $m_2 = 0.5$ did not lift force $R^2$ to the predicted 0.85} [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}].
\item Force $R^2$ was 0.750 and 0.732, while \texttt{distance} was decoded at 0.87–0.89.
\item Both runs were cut short by the budget hard stop. Seed 14 reached 0.55 completion, below the 80\% replication bar, so this finding effectively rests on seed 15 (completion 0.917).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Linear decodability stops tracking prediction quality}

The 6-layer, $d_{\text{model}} = 128$ model was trained at $e \in [0, 0.85]$ [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}]:

\begin{itemize}
\item After step 2000, layer-6 \texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ rose only from 0.710 to 0.735 (seed 56) and from 0.683 to 0.705 (seed 57).
\item Over the same steps, \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} fell 6.8× and 5.5×, to 0.0037 and 0.0032.
\end{itemize}

The 2-layer model showed a related pattern: its \texttt{accel\_step} margin plateaued by step 1500 while its force margin kept rising [\texttt{f-20261013-000220-ab2d}].

\subsection{Out-of-distribution transfer}

\textbf{A model trained on a restricted $e$ range did not stay within 10× of its in-distribution error on high-$e$ systems} [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}].

\begin{itemize}
\item On high-$e$ systems, \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was 0.270 and 0.249, about 16–18× the in-distribution value. It still stayed below 1.
\item Transfer to low mass ratios held, at about 3.6× in-distribution.
\item Three-body ratios of 72 and 70 are exploratory only.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Training on $e \in [0, 0.8]$ closed the prediction gap} [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

\begin{itemize}
\item The high-$e$ to in-distribution ratio was about 1.05× and 1.01×.
\item Both runs were cut short by the budget hard stop at step 2500 (completion 0.867 and 0.9), with \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} still falling.
\item A probe gap remained: force $R^2$ was 0.366–0.377 on held-out high-$e$ systems, against 0.729–0.802 on low-$e$ systems. The out-of-distribution probes have no random controls.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Symbolic regression has not recovered $1/r^2$}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{At $e \in [0, 0.9]$:} the fits to the \texttt{accel\_step} direction were $0.0103\,a^2/r$ and $0.00277\,((a+0.685)^3/r - r)$, with fit errors 0.71 and 0.62. These rest on weak probes ($R^2$ 0.395 and 0.503) [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{With stronger probes ($R^2$ 0.831 and 0.881):} the fits were still $r^{-1}$, of the form $a^2/r - c\,a$ with $c \approx 0.55$ (fit errors 0.245 and 0.201). The force fits were $0.81/r$ and $0.80/r$ [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}].
\item \textbf{Open check:} the fitting pipeline has not yet been run on the true \texttt{accel\_step} target. Until it is, an artefact of the fitter cannot be ruled out [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Null and negative results, and open gaps}

\textbf{Eight hypotheses were refuted:}

\begin{itemize}
\item a wider $m_2$ range would separate force from \texttt{accel\_step} [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}];
\item restricted-$e$ training would transfer to high $e$ [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}];
\item fixing $m_2$ would lift force [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}];
\item wide-$e$ training would yield $r^{-2}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}];
\item at $N = 64$, \texttt{accel\_step} would clear a 0.15 bar and the early force spike would not survive best-of-5 controls [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}];
\item formation would scale with the learning rate [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}];
\item $N = 64$ formation would fall in a narrow window [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}];
\item coarser sampling ($N = 16$) would raise the \texttt{accel\_step} margin [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Not yet reported:}

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{velocity} probe results;
\item activation-patching results;
\item any test of whether the \texttt{accel\_step} code is causally used.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Limits throughout:}

\begin{itemize}
\item All probes are linear.
\item Where \texttt{accel\_step} controls are high ($e \le 0.5$, $N = 16$), its margins sit close to the 0.1 bar.
\item Most results share data seed 0 and one 4-layer architecture.
\end{itemize}

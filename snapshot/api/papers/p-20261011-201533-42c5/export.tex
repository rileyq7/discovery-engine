\section{Abstract}

We ask whether small transformers trained to predict simulated two-body orbits (REBOUND, $G = M = 1$) learn the gravitational law or heuristics that happen to work, and when any such structure forms. Linear probes read the residual stream for force $m_2/r^2$, per-snapshot acceleration (\texttt{accel\_step}) $(2\pi/N)^2 a^3/((1+m_2)r^2)$, distance and energy. Each probe is compared against a randomly initialised control, and symbolic regression is fitted to the probe directions. All 9 findings replicated (4 supported, 5 refuted) across 18 evidence runs. The budget hard stop cut 4 of those runs short. Models beat constant-velocity extrapolation, reaching \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} of about 0.011 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}]. \texttt{accel\_step} beats its control by step 100, while \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} is still 1.96 and 1.84 [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}]. Force forms later or never, and the timing did not shift at $N = 256$ [\texttt{f-20261011-231210-478d}]. Restricted-eccentricity training left a 16–18× high-$e$ gap [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]. Training on $e \in [0, 0.8]$ closed it, in runs stopped early at completion 0.867 and 0.9 [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]. The main negative result is that no $1/r^2$ law was recovered. All four symbolic fits of \texttt{accel\_step} give $r^{-1}$ forms [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}, \texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}].

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data generation}

All orbits are simulated with REBOUND in units where $G = 1$ and the primary mass is $M = 1$. The gravitational force on body 2 is therefore $|F| = m_2/r^2$, where $m_2$ is the mass ratio. Varying $M$ would add nothing. Positions are sampled at fixed phase intervals, so absolute mass and time scale cannot be identified from them.

Each orbit is sampled at a fixed number of snapshots per orbit, $N$ (\texttt{steps\_per\_orbit}). The time between snapshots is therefore $\Delta t = P/N$, which grows with orbit size ($\Delta t^2 \propto a^3$). The model never sees physical time.

Three-body systems are hierarchical: the third body sits at 3.5–6× the inner semi-major axis. They are used only as an evaluation set for models trained on two-body systems.

The configuration schema adds these fields to the specification:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{data.eccentricity\_range}
\item \texttt{data.data\_seed}
\item \texttt{probes.control\_margin}
\item \texttt{probes.patching}
\item \texttt{probes.symbolic}
\item \texttt{eval\_sets}, for out-of-distribution evaluation
\end{itemize}

Unknown fields are rejected. The training ranges differ between the cited experiments:

\begin{itemize}
\item Some runs train on the default range, which reaches $e \leq 0.5$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item Some runs widen the mass ratio to $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item Some runs widen eccentricity to $e \in [0, 0.8]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\item Some runs widen both, to $e \in [0, 0.9]$ and $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. Both of these runs share data seed 0.
\end{itemize}

The held-out evaluation sets are:

\begin{itemize}
\item low mass ratio, $m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$
\item high eccentricity, $e \in [0.5, 0.8]$
\item three-body systems [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]
\item held-out low-$e$ and high-$e$ splits, used for the wide-eccentricity runs [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tokens, model and prediction target}

Each token is one time step. It holds the $xy$ positions of up to three bodies (6 numbers) in the centre-of-mass frame, with absent bodies set to zero. Because every system uses the same token format, a model trained on two bodies can be evaluated on three-body sets.

The model predicts the normalised displacement to the next step. All evidence runs use one transformer architecture: $L = 4$ layers, width $d = 64$, and $N = 64$ snapshots per orbit [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Prediction quality is reported as \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio}. This is the model's MSE divided by the MSE of constant-velocity extrapolation, $\hat{x}_{t+1} = 2x_t - x_{t-1}$. Both are computed in physical position units over steps $t \geq 1$ on the same held-out systems. A value below 1 means the model beats the baseline.

\subsection{Training}

The lab's default first-experiment (E1) configuration trains for 5k steps at batch size 128 and learning rate $10^{-3}$. Training runs on a laptop GPU (MPS) and is kept CPU-friendly.

Checkpoint spacing varies between experiments. One formation experiment probes every 250 steps [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}]. A dense-checkpoint experiment probes every 25 steps early in training [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].

Every finding is replicated on two training seeds.

\textbf{Budget hard stop.} A runaway guard stops a run once it reaches 2× its seed's share of \texttt{budget.max\_compute\_hours}. The clock starts at the first training step, so dataset generation does not count. A stopped run is checkpointed and probed at its stop step. It records \texttt{steps\_completed} and \texttt{steps\_planned}, and its \texttt{completion} is the fraction of planned steps it trained. Runs below 80\% completion do not count toward replication.

Two of the 12 evidence runs were cut short by an earlier version of this guard. That version stopped at 1.2× the budget share and counted dataset generation against it. Both runs are in [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]: seed 9 stopped at completion 0.867 (step 2640 of 3000) and seed 8 at 0.9 (step 2714). Under that version the probes were taken at the last regular checkpoint, step 2500, not at the stop step. Their \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still falling when training stopped.

\subsection{Probe targets}

Linear probes read five quantities from the residual stream at every layer:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{distance}
\item relative \texttt{velocity}
\item \texttt{energy}, which is constant per system
\item physical \texttt{force}, $|F| = m_2/r^2$
\item the per-snapshot acceleration \texttt{accel\_step}:
\end{itemize}

\[|a_{\text{step}}| = \frac{F}{m_2}\,\Delta t^2 = \left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\right)^2 \frac{a^3}{(1+m_2)\,r^2}.\]

Next-step prediction needs \texttt{accel\_step}, not the physical force. A weak \texttt{force} probe on its own could therefore mean the model learned a per-snapshot version of gravity, not that it learned no gravity. For this reason \texttt{force} and \texttt{accel\_step} are always compared.

A unit test checks two properties of \texttt{accel\_step}:

\begin{itemize}
\item It equals the second difference of positions, to within 1\%.
\item It is not proportional to the physical force across orbit sizes.
\end{itemize}

\texttt{velocity} probe results do not appear in any cited finding.

\subsection{Probe protocol and random-model controls}

Probes run on 300 held-out systems, at every other time step from $t = 8$. Systems are split 70/30 into probe-train and probe-test sets, so rows from the same orbit never appear on both sides.

Each probe is a ridge regression (RidgeCV over $\alpha \in [10^{-3}, 10^{4}]$) on standardised inputs and outputs. $R^2$ is averaged across output dimensions.

Every probe has a control: the same probe fitted on a randomly initialised model of the same architecture. There is one control per layer, because a random model does not change across checkpoints. A probe result counts only if

\[R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}} \geq 0.1 \quad (\texttt{probes.control\_margin}).\]

For each target, the lab reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the best layer
\item its $R^2$ and control $R^2$
\item \texttt{formed\_at\_step}, the first checkpoint at which the margin reaches the threshold (null if it never does)
\end{itemize}

The control $R^2$ for \texttt{accel\_step} is high under the default ranges (0.71–0.75). This caps the margin \texttt{accel\_step} can reach. One finding therefore also reports a post hoc, headroom-normalised score, $(R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}})/(1 - R^2_{\text{control}})$, but its pre-registered criterion uses the raw margin [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].

Probes on the out-of-distribution evaluation sets have no random controls. Their $R^2$ values therefore cannot be read as margins [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}, \texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Only the final checkpoint's activations are saved to disk, as float16. Activations for earlier checkpoints are recomputed from the saved weights when needed.

\subsection{Symbolic regression}

When \texttt{probes.symbolic} is enabled, PySR fits a closed-form expression to the best-layer probe direction for \texttt{force} and for \texttt{accel\_step}. The \texttt{accel\_step} fit uses the inputs $r$, $m_2$ and $a$. Each fit reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the equation
\item whether the true law was recovered (\texttt{law\_recovered})
\item \texttt{fit\_error\_vs\_true\_law}
\end{itemize}

Symbolic fits were run at the final checkpoint only, and only in the runs behind [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. These are the only evidence runs with fitted equations.

\subsection{Activation patching}

The pipeline also supports activation patching. Heads are ranked by a force probe on each head's contribution to the residual stream. The top three heads, plus the lowest-ranked head as a control, are each patched with that head's output on a different system, and the relative increase in next-step MSE is recorded. None of the cited findings report patching results.

\section{Results}

The lab has nine citable findings. All nine are replicated across two seeds: four are supported and five are refuted. They rest on 18 evidence runs, and the budget hard stop cut 4 of those runs short. A probe result counts only when its $R^2$ beats a random-init control of the same architecture by at least 0.1. Most evidence runs use $L = 4$, $d = 64$ and $N = 64$ snapshots per orbit [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]. One experiment raised $N$ to 256 [\texttt{f-20261011-231210-478d}].

\subsection{Prediction quality}

Almost every trained model beat constant-velocity extrapolation on held-out two-body systems. The exception is the $N = 256$ runs.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Final in-distribution \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio}:}
\item About 0.011 in the default-range runs [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}].
\item 0.0114 and 0.0119 with $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item 0.0151 and 0.0157 in the restricted-eccentricity transfer runs [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}].
\item 0.0178 and 0.0187 with $e \in [0, 0.8]$. Both runs were stopped early, at completion 0.867 and 0.9 [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\item 0.0137 and 0.0128 with $e \in [0, 0.9]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item 0.0135 and 0.0115 in the symbolic-replication runs (seeds 24 and 26) [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}].
\item 0.063 and 0.0145 with $m_2$ fixed at 0.5. Both runs were stopped early: seed 14 at completion 0.55 and seed 15 at 0.917 [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}].
\item \textbf{The dense-checkpoint runs ended higher,} at 0.141 and 0.166 [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item \textbf{The $N = 256$ runs did not beat the baseline.} Their \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was 3.12–3.20 after all 600 planned steps [\texttt{f-20261011-231210-478d}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{When structure forms}

\textbf{Supported: gravity-related structure forms before the model beats the baseline} [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}, \texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{250-step checkpoints (seeds 2 and 3)} [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}]:
\item \texttt{accel\_step} already beat its control at step 250, with margins of 0.158 and 0.127 at layer 4.
\item \texttt{distance} margins at step 250 were 0.597 and 0.681.
\item \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} first fell below 0.1 only at step 1500.
\item \textbf{25-step checkpoints (seeds 4 and 5)} [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}]:
\item The \texttt{accel\_step} margin first reached $\geq 0.1$ at step 100 (0.115 and 0.108). At that step \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still 1.96 and 1.84.
\item \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was first reported below 1 at step 175 (0.736 and 0.878).
\item \texttt{distance} and \texttt{energy} formed at step 25, while \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still between 5 and 83.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Refuted: formation timing does not scale with steps per orbit} [\texttt{f-20261011-231210-478d}].

\begin{itemize}
\item At $N = 256$ the \texttt{accel\_step} margin first reached $\geq 0.1$ at step 100 in both seeds (0.116 and 0.104). At step 50 it had been 0.045–0.049. Step 100 is the same crossing step as at $N = 64$.
\item \texttt{distance} and \texttt{energy} again formed by step 25.
\item Force never beat its control. Its final margin was 0.012–0.032, after a brief peak of 0.09 at step 50.
\item These models never learned the task (\texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} 3.12–3.20). That makes it hard to read the timing as set by task demand.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Force never formed before \texttt{accel\_step}.}

\begin{itemize}
\item Seed 3: force formed at step 750, \texttt{accel\_step} at step 250. Seed 2: both formed at step 250 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}].
\item Seed 5: force formed at step 450, \texttt{accel\_step} at step 100. Seed 4: force never formed [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item At $N = 256$, force never formed in either seed [\texttt{f-20261011-231210-478d}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Caveats on timing.}

\begin{itemize}
\item The crossing margins sit just above the threshold, at 0.104–0.158. With a 0.12 threshold, the dense-run crossing would move to about step 175 and coincide with \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} falling below 1 [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}, \texttt{f-20261011-231210-478d}].
\item Seed 5's \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} rose back to 1.057 at step 225 [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item Early \texttt{distance} and \texttt{energy} signal may be input geometry rather than learned dynamics [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item Forming early is a correlation in time. It does not show that the model uses this structure to predict [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Refuted: widening the mass range makes force fall behind \texttt{accel\_step}} [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Result:} with $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$, force kept the larger margin in both seeds.
\item \textbf{The criterion is biased.} \texttt{accel\_step}'s control $R^2$ of 0.71–0.75 leaves it little room for a margin. Measured as a fraction of the available headroom, $(R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}})/(1 - R^2_{\text{control}})$, \texttt{accel\_step} leads: 0.70 vs 0.48 and 0.71 vs 0.42.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Refuted: fixing $m_2$ lifts the force probe} [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Setup:} with $m_2 = 0.5$, the force is exactly $0.5/r^2$.
\item \textbf{Prediction:} force $R^2$ would reach 0.85.
\item \textbf{Result:} force $R^2$ reached only 0.750 and 0.732, while \texttt{distance} was decoded at 0.87–0.89. The force ceiling therefore comes from the $1/r^2$ nonlinearity or how the layers encode it, not from missing $m_2$ information.
\item \textbf{Both runs were cut short by the budget hard stop.} Seed 15 reached completion 0.917. Seed 14 reached only 0.55 and stopped at step 1500, with \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} 0.063. That is below the lab's 80\% completion bar, so under the protocol seed 14 does not count as an agreeing seed. Treat this finding's replication with that in mind. The better-trained seed 15 had the slightly lower force $R^2$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Where each quantity is read out.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{energy} is best at layer 1 in every evidence run.
\item \texttt{distance} is best at layer 1 in every run except seed 16, where it is best at layer 2 [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \texttt{accel\_step} is best at layer 4 in every run except the $N = 256$ runs, where it is best at layer 3 [\texttt{f-20261011-231210-478d}].
\item Force has no stable best layer: layer 1, 2 or 4 depending on seed [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}, \texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}].
\item Force and \texttt{accel\_step} are collinear through $1/r^2$, so part of the force signal may be distance information [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Out-of-distribution transfer}

\textbf{Refuted: training on restricted eccentricity transfers to high eccentricity} [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Low mass ratio ($m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$): passed.} \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was 0.057 and 0.055, about 3.6× in-distribution.
\item \textbf{High eccentricity ($e \in [0.5, 0.8]$): failed the pre-registered 10× criterion.} \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was 0.270 and 0.249, about 16–18× in-distribution. Force $R^2$ fell to 0.40 and 0.34.
\item \textbf{All two-body ratios stayed below 1.}
\item \textbf{Three-body ratios of 72 and 70 are exploratory only.}
\item \textbf{OOD probes have no random controls.}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Supported: covering $e \in [0, 0.8]$ in training removes the gap} [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Prediction gap closed:} held-out high-$e$ \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was 0.0187 and 0.0189, about 1.05× and 1.01× in-distribution. The pre-registered threshold was 3×.
\item \textbf{Early stop:} both runs were cut short by the budget hard stop at step 2500 of 3000 (completion 0.867 and 0.9), while \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still falling. Both are above the 80\% bar.
\item \textbf{Probe gap remained:} the best force probe on high-$e$ systems reached only 0.366–0.377, against 0.729–0.802 on low-$e$ systems, even though prediction error was equal on both.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Symbolic recovery of the law}

\textbf{Refuted: wide eccentricity coverage yields $r^{-2}$ from the probe directions} [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Setup:} $e \in [0, 0.9]$, $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$.
\item \textbf{\texttt{accel\_step} fits:}
\item Seed 16: $0.0103\,a^2/r$, with error 0.71.
\item Seed 17: $0.00277\,\big((a+0.685)^3/r - r\big)$, with error 0.62.
\item \textbf{Force fits:} $m_2/r + 0.40$ and $(m_2 + 0.60)/r$.
\item \textbf{Weak probes:} \texttt{accel\_step} raw $R^2$ was only 0.395 and 0.503, so these fits describe a weak linear approximation.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Supported: the linearly decodable gravity direction has an $r^{-1}$ orbit-scale form} [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Better probes, same result:} seeds 24 and 26 reached \texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ of 0.831 and 0.881, and their fits were still $r^{-1}$.
\item Seed 24: $0.0134\,r\,(0.275 - a/r)^2$, which expands to $a^2/r - 0.55a + 0.076r$, with error 0.245.
\item Seed 26: $0.0136\,a\,(a/r - 0.562) + 0.0015$, with error 0.201.
\item \textbf{Force fits} were $0.81/r$ and $0.80/r$, with errors 0.43–0.46.
\item \textbf{Agreement:} together with an earlier seed, the pattern holds in 3 of 3 seeds.
\item \textbf{Strong codes alongside:} \texttt{distance} ($R^2$ 0.83–0.85) and \texttt{energy} ($R^2$ 0.92–0.93) at layer 1.
\item \textbf{Caveats:}
\item Seed 26 only just clears the fit-error criterion.
\item The fitted shape $a^2/r - c\,a$ with $c \approx 0.55$ is close to the vis-viva combination $(v\,dt)^2/a$, but this has not been established.
\item The symbolic-regression pipeline has not yet been checked on the true \texttt{accel\_step} target. If it also returns $r^{-1}$ there, the result describes the fitter, not the model.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Summary of null and negative results}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Five hypotheses were refuted:}
\item Wider $m_2$ did not make force fall behind \texttt{accel\_step} [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item Restricted-$e$ training did not transfer to high $e$ within 10× [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]. This was later traced to training coverage [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\item Fixing $m_2$ did not lift force $R^2$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}].
\item Wide-$e$ training did not yield an $r^{-2}$ fit [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item Formation timing did not shift with $N = 256$ [\texttt{f-20261011-231210-478d}].
\item \textbf{No explicit $1/r^2$ law has been extracted.} All four runs with symbolic fits give $r^{-1}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}, \texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}].

\section{Abstract}

We ask whether small transformers trained to predict simulated two-body orbits encode the physical force $|F| = m_2/r^2$ or the per-snapshot acceleration $(F/m_2)\,dt^2$ that next-step prediction actually needs, and when either forms during training. Linear probes on the residual stream count only if they beat the best of several random-init controls by the required margin. Across 46 replicated findings (33 supported, 13 refuted; 109 evidence runs, 4 cut short by the budget hard stop), \texttt{accel\_step} beats its control in every setting we tested. At $N = 64$ snapshots per orbit it forms by steps 85–140, before the model beats constant-velocity extrapolation [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}]. Its headroom-normalised margin stays at about 0.63–0.75 from $N = 16$ to $N = 256$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}, \texttt{f-20261013-051807-f3f1}, \texttt{f-20261013-063631-c4ed}]. Force is decodable at $N = 64$ (margins 0.216–0.255) [\texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}]. At $N = 128$ it loses 0.098–0.128 of trained $R^2$ while the controls stay flat [\texttt{f-20261013-075824-35f0}]. It fails its control at $N = 256$ [\texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}], and the drop persists with $m_2$ fixed [\texttt{f-20261013-234552-f8e8}]. The main negative result is that symbolic fits to the probe directions give $r^{-1}$, not $r^{-2}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}, \texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. Transfer to high eccentricity also failed, with error 16–18× in-distribution [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}].

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data generation}

All orbits are simulated with REBOUND in units where $G = 1$ and the primary mass is $M = 1$. The gravitational force on body 2 is therefore $|F| = m_2/r^2$, where $m_2$ is the mass ratio. Varying $M$ would add nothing. Positions are sampled at fixed phase intervals, so absolute mass and time scale cannot be identified from them.

Each orbit is sampled at a fixed number of snapshots per orbit, $N$ (\texttt{steps\_per\_orbit}). The time between snapshots is therefore $\Delta t = P/N$, which grows with orbit size ($\Delta t^2 \propto a^3$). The model never sees physical time.

Three-body systems are hierarchical: the third body sits at 3.5–6× the inner semi-major axis. They are used only as an evaluation set for models trained on two-body systems.

The configuration schema adds these fields to the specification:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{data.eccentricity\_range}
\item \texttt{data.data\_seed}
\item \texttt{probes.control\_margin}
\item \texttt{probes.patching}
\item \texttt{probes.symbolic}
\item \texttt{eval\_sets}, for out-of-distribution evaluation
\end{itemize}

Unknown fields are rejected. The training ranges differ between the cited experiments:

\begin{itemize}
\item Some runs train on the default range, which reaches $e \leq 0.5$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item Some runs widen the mass ratio to $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item Some runs widen eccentricity to $e \in [0, 0.8]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\item Some runs widen both, to $e \in [0, 0.9]$ and $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. Both of these runs share data seed 0.
\end{itemize}

The held-out evaluation sets are:

\begin{itemize}
\item low mass ratio, $m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$
\item high eccentricity, $e \in [0.5, 0.8]$
\item three-body systems [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]
\item held-out low-$e$ and high-$e$ splits, used for the wide-eccentricity runs [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tokens, model and prediction target}

Each token is one time step. It holds the $xy$ positions of up to three bodies (6 numbers) in the centre-of-mass frame, with absent bodies set to zero. Because every system uses the same token format, a model trained on two bodies can be evaluated on three-body sets.

The model predicts the normalised displacement to the next step. All evidence runs use one transformer architecture: $L = 4$ layers, width $d = 64$, and $N = 64$ snapshots per orbit [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Prediction quality is reported as \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio}. This is the model's MSE divided by the MSE of constant-velocity extrapolation, $\hat{x}_{t+1} = 2x_t - x_{t-1}$. Both are computed in physical position units over steps $t \geq 1$ on the same held-out systems. A value below 1 means the model beats the baseline.

\subsection{Training}

The lab's default first-experiment (E1) configuration trains for 5k steps at batch size 128 and learning rate $10^{-3}$. Training runs on a laptop GPU (MPS) and is kept CPU-friendly.

Checkpoint spacing varies between experiments. One formation experiment probes every 250 steps [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}]. A dense-checkpoint experiment probes every 25 steps early in training [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].

Every finding is replicated on two training seeds.

\textbf{Budget hard stop.} A runaway guard stops a run once it reaches 2× its seed's share of \texttt{budget.max\_compute\_hours}. The clock starts at the first training step, so dataset generation does not count. A stopped run is checkpointed and probed at its stop step. It records \texttt{steps\_completed} and \texttt{steps\_planned}, and its \texttt{completion} is the fraction of planned steps it trained. Runs below 80\% completion do not count toward replication.

Two of the 12 evidence runs were cut short by an earlier version of this guard. That version stopped at 1.2× the budget share and counted dataset generation against it. Both runs are in [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]: seed 9 stopped at completion 0.867 (step 2640 of 3000) and seed 8 at 0.9 (step 2714). Under that version the probes were taken at the last regular checkpoint, step 2500, not at the stop step. Their \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still falling when training stopped.

\subsection{Probe targets}

Linear probes read five quantities from the residual stream at every layer:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{distance}
\item relative \texttt{velocity}
\item \texttt{energy}, which is constant per system
\item physical \texttt{force}, $|F| = m_2/r^2$
\item the per-snapshot acceleration \texttt{accel\_step}:
\end{itemize}

\[|a_{\text{step}}| = \frac{F}{m_2}\,\Delta t^2 = \left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\right)^2 \frac{a^3}{(1+m_2)\,r^2}.\]

Next-step prediction needs \texttt{accel\_step}, not the physical force. A weak \texttt{force} probe on its own could therefore mean the model learned a per-snapshot version of gravity, not that it learned no gravity. For this reason \texttt{force} and \texttt{accel\_step} are always compared.

A unit test checks two properties of \texttt{accel\_step}:

\begin{itemize}
\item It equals the second difference of positions, to within 1\%.
\item It is not proportional to the physical force across orbit sizes.
\end{itemize}

\texttt{velocity} probe results do not appear in any cited finding.

\subsection{Probe protocol and random-model controls}

Probes run on 300 held-out systems, at every other time step from $t = 8$. Systems are split 70/30 into probe-train and probe-test sets, so rows from the same orbit never appear on both sides.

Each probe is a ridge regression (RidgeCV over $\alpha \in [10^{-3}, 10^{4}]$) on standardised inputs and outputs. $R^2$ is averaged across output dimensions.

Every probe has a control: the same probe fitted on a randomly initialised model of the same architecture. There is one control per layer, because a random model does not change across checkpoints. A probe result counts only if

\[R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}} \geq 0.1 \quad (\texttt{probes.control\_margin}).\]

For each target, the lab reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the best layer
\item its $R^2$ and control $R^2$
\item \texttt{formed\_at\_step}, the first checkpoint at which the margin reaches the threshold (null if it never does)
\end{itemize}

The control $R^2$ for \texttt{accel\_step} is high under the default ranges (0.71–0.75). This caps the margin \texttt{accel\_step} can reach. One finding therefore also reports a post hoc, headroom-normalised score, $(R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}})/(1 - R^2_{\text{control}})$, but its pre-registered criterion uses the raw margin [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].

Probes on the out-of-distribution evaluation sets have no random controls. Their $R^2$ values therefore cannot be read as margins [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}, \texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Only the final checkpoint's activations are saved to disk, as float16. Activations for earlier checkpoints are recomputed from the saved weights when needed.

\subsection{Symbolic regression}

When \texttt{probes.symbolic} is enabled, PySR fits a closed-form expression to the best-layer probe direction for \texttt{force} and for \texttt{accel\_step}. The \texttt{accel\_step} fit uses the inputs $r$, $m_2$ and $a$. Each fit reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the equation
\item whether the true law was recovered (\texttt{law\_recovered})
\item \texttt{fit\_error\_vs\_true\_law}
\end{itemize}

Symbolic fits were run at the final checkpoint only, and only in the runs behind [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. These are the only evidence runs with fitted equations.

\subsection{Activation patching}

The pipeline also supports activation patching. Heads are ranked by a force probe on each head's contribution to the residual stream. The top three heads, plus the lowest-ranked head as a control, are each patched with that head's output on a different system, and the relative increase in next-step MSE is recorded. None of the cited findings report patching results.

\section{Results}

The lab currently has 46 citable findings, all with status \texttt{replicated}. Of these, 33 are \texttt{supported} and 13 are \texttt{refuted}. Together they draw on 109 evidence runs. The budget hard stop cut 4 of those runs short, and we flag them wherever their numbers are used. Unless noted, the model has 4 layers and $d_\text{model} = 64$. All results come from linear probes. A probe "beats its control" when $R^2 - \text{control}\ R^2 \ge \max(0.1,\ \text{control spread})$.

\subsection{1. What the residual stream encodes: per-snapshot acceleration, and force only weakly}

\textbf{\texttt{accel\_step} beats its random control at the final checkpoint in every run reported in the findings.} It usually peaks at layer 4. The target is
\[|a_\text{step}| = \left(\tfrac{2\pi}{N}\right)^2 \frac{a^3}{(1+m_2)\,r^2}.\]
Its random controls are high, typically 0.65–0.81 at $e \le 0.5$. This caps the raw margin, so we also report the headroom-normalised margin $(R^2 - c)/(1 - c)$, where $c$ is the control $R^2$.

\textbf{The physical force $|F| = m_2/r^2$ is much less reliable.} It fails its control in short runs, at fine sampling, and in several fixed-mass conditions (§3–§5). When it does pass, it usually peaks at layer 1, the same layer where \texttt{distance} is decoded. The two targets rank differently depending on the metric:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Raw margin} often favours force. Examples: 0.228 / 0.255 / 0.216 for force against 0.207 / 0.178 / 0.156 for \texttt{accel\_step} [\texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}]; see also [\texttt{f-20261013-000220-ab2d}, \texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}].
\item \textbf{Normalised margin} favours \texttt{accel\_step} in every comparison reported. Examples: 0.71 / 0.69 / 0.68 for \texttt{accel\_step} against about 0.43–0.48 for force [\texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}]; 0.86 / 0.83 against 0.49 / 0.53 in a $d_\text{model} = 128$ model [\texttt{f-20261013-020028-8792}].
\item \textbf{The one exception to the normalised ordering} is the $L = 2$, $d_\text{model} = 32$ sweep cell. There, force leads on normalised margin in one of two seeds [\texttt{f-20261013-000220-ab2d}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Widening $m_2$ to $[0.01, 1]$ did not push force behind \texttt{accel\_step} on raw margin (refuted).} Force margins were 0.243 / 0.235 against 0.205 / 0.175 for \texttt{accel\_step} [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].

\textbf{\texttt{distance} and \texttt{energy} form very early.} They form at steps 10–50 in the short-run experiments, while the model is still far worse than the baseline [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}, \texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}]. Their controls disagree by up to about 0.25, so their margins carry less weight than those for \texttt{accel\_step}.

\subsection{2. When structure forms}

\textbf{\texttt{accel\_step} forms before the model beats constant-velocity extrapolation at $N = 64$, but only just.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{250-step checkpoints, single control.} In the first test, the margin was already 0.158 / 0.127 at step 250, the first trained checkpoint. \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} first fell below 0.1 only at step 1500 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}].
\item \textbf{25-step checkpoints.} The crossing came at step 100. At that point \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still 1.96 / 1.84, and it first fell below 1 at step 175 [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item \textbf{10-step checkpoints, best-of-5 controls.} Crossings came at steps 140 / 140 / 100. That is 40–90 steps before \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} < 1. Force did not beat its control in any seed [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}].
\item \textbf{Margin size.} The margins at crossing are thin (0.100–0.12).
\end{itemize}

!\href{figure:formation/run-20261011-234250-a7ca}{Formation trajectory, N = 64, seed 46}

\textbf{Two parts of the follow-up hypothesis failed (refuted)} [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}]:

\begin{itemize}
\item The \texttt{accel\_step} margin never reached 0.15 in 2 of 3 seeds.
\item An early force spike survived best-of-5 controls in all 3 seeds. The force margin was 0.166–0.189 at step 10, then decayed to about 0.00–0.02 by steps 40–50.
\end{itemize}

The same brief force transient appears in other short runs [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}, \texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}, \texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].

\textbf{The formation window at $N = 64$ is wider than first thought.}

\begin{itemize}
\item Seeds 62–64 fell inside the pre-registered windows. Persistent formation came at steps 90–100, and half the final margin was reached at steps 60–90 [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}].
\item Five further seeds at 5-step resolution formed at steps 65–90. Three of the five fell outside the window, so that prediction was refuted [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Formation follows the training step, not the loss.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$N = 16$.} \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} drops below 1 at step 20, well before \texttt{accel\_step} forms at steps 75–90. The formation steps are within 5–15 steps of the seed-matched $N = 64$ runs. The ordering against the baseline therefore flips only because the baseline is weaker at $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].
\item \textbf{$e \le 0.85$.} \texttt{accel\_step} forms at steps 60–70 at $N = 64$ [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}]. At $N = 256$ it also forms at steps 60–70, even though that model never beats the baseline (final \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} 2.03–2.37) [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}].
\item \textbf{Learning rate.} Halving it delayed formation by only +10 / +20 / +30 steps, not about 2×. The hypothesis that formation scales with learning rate was refuted [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Force forms later, and only with longer training.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{600-step runs at $N = 64$.} Force did not beat its control [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}].
\item \textbf{1500-step runs on data seed 1.} Force formed persistently at steps 400–600 in all 3 seeds, ending at margins of 0.167–0.196. This happened only after its best layer moved to layer 1 [\texttt{f-20261013-025812-b9ee}].
\item \textbf{$d_\text{model} = 128$ model.} Force formed 250–300 steps after \texttt{accel\_step}. It gained +0.074 / +0.081 between steps 500 and 5000, while \texttt{accel\_step} gained +0.025 / +0.022 [\texttt{f-20261013-020028-8792}].
\item \textbf{$L = 2$, $d_\text{model} = 32$.} The force margin kept rising through step 3000, while \texttt{accel\_step} levelled off at 0.13–0.15 by step 1500 [\texttt{f-20261013-000220-ab2d}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{$N = 16$ and $N = 64$ give the same force level.} The hypothesis that force declines gradually starting from $N = 16$ was refuted [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}].

\textbf{From $N = 64$ to $N = 128$, force is lost in the trained model, not in the controls.}

\begin{itemize}
\item Data seed 0, seeds 24–26: trained force $R^2$ fell by 0.098–0.128, while controls moved by +0.002 to +0.009 [\texttt{f-20261013-075824-35f0}].
\item Data seed 1, seeds 22/23: trained force $R^2$ fell by 0.095–0.097 [\texttt{f-20261013-224908-8b46}].
\item Data seed 1, seeds 20/21: trained force $R^2$ fell by 0.064–0.072, again with flat controls [\texttt{f-20261013-054542-23e1}].
\item \texttt{distance} $R^2$ does not fall over the same step [\texttt{f-20261013-075824-35f0}, \texttt{f-20261013-224908-8b46}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{From $N = 128$ to $N = 256$, the cause depends on the dataset.}

\begin{itemize}
\item On data seed 1, the drop is entirely a control effect. Trained force $R^2$ stays at about 0.66–0.67, while the force controls rise to 0.60–0.63 [\texttt{f-20261013-063631-c4ed}].
\item On data seed 0, trained $R^2$ also falls, and the controls account for only about 35–50\% of the margin loss. The controls-only hypothesis was refuted [\texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The $N = 256$ shortfall on data seed 0 is not just slower convergence.}

\begin{itemize}
\item 1000 extra steps added only about 0.011–0.014 of trained force $R^2$. The hypothesis that extra training would close the gap was refuted [\texttt{f-20261013-083108-2efd}].
\item Matched on \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio}, $N = 128$ still decodes force better: 0.641 vs 0.557 and 0.630 vs 0.569 [\texttt{f-20261013-093210-2e03}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{At 12000 steps the $N = 64$ vs $N = 128$ gap remains}, at 0.062 / 0.069 in force margin. Force gained only 0.019 / 0.007 between steps 6000 and 12000 [\texttt{f-20261013-051738-2a37}].

\textbf{The normalised \texttt{accel\_step} margin is roughly flat across $N$:}

\begin{itemize}
\item 0.68–0.69 at $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}]
\item 0.68–0.74 at $N = 64$ [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}, \texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}, \texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}]
\item 0.63–0.72 at $N = 128$ [\texttt{f-20261013-051807-f3f1}, \texttt{f-20261013-075824-35f0}, \texttt{f-20261013-054542-23e1}, \texttt{f-20261013-224908-8b46}]
\item 0.66–0.75 at $N = 256$ [\texttt{f-20261013-063603-95b5}, \texttt{f-20261013-063631-c4ed}, \texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}]
\end{itemize}

The prediction that $N = 16$ would raise this margin to about 0.8 was refuted [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}].

\subsection{4. Fixing the mass ratio at $m_2 = 0.5$}

\textbf{The first test was refuted.} Fixing $m_2$ did not lift force $R^2$ to 0.85. Force reached 0.750 / 0.732, against \texttt{accel\_step} at 0.924 / 0.946 [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}]. The budget hard stop cut both runs short:

\begin{itemize}
\item seed 14 stopped at step 1500 (completion 0.55), below the 80\% needed to count as an agreeing seed;
\item seed 15 stopped at step 2500 (completion 0.917).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Complete 3000-step runs at $N = 64$:}

\begin{itemize}
\item Trained force $R^2$ is 0.789 / 0.763 on data seed 0 [\texttt{f-20261013-234439-d851}] and 0.795 / 0.790 on data seed 1 [\texttt{f-20261014-015644-a80b}].
\item These values are higher than with a wide $m_2$ range, but the margins are smaller (0.133–0.195). Fixing $m_2$ raises the force control to about 0.59–0.64.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Fixing $m_2$ does not remove the $N = 64 \to 128$ drop (refuted)} [\texttt{f-20261013-234552-f8e8}]. Trained force $R^2$ still falls with near-flat controls:

\begin{itemize}
\item 0.070 / 0.105 for seeds 20/21 on data seed 0 [\texttt{f-20261013-234552-f8e8}];
\item about 0.11 / 0.14 for seeds 22/23 on data seed 0 [\texttt{f-20261014-004426-68d6}];
\item about 0.065 / 0.088 on data seed 1 [\texttt{f-20261014-015607-930a}].
\end{itemize}

At $N = 128$ and 3000 steps, force fails its control in 5 of these 6 seeds. What is lost appears to be the readout of $1/r^2$, not of the $m_2$ factor.

\textbf{Longer training narrows the gap but has not closed it.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{6000 steps.} $N = 64$ reaches force $R^2$ 0.8235 / 0.7684. That is 0.143 / 0.072 above the $N = 128$ 6000-step references (0.681 / 0.696). The seed-22 comparison is cross-layer, and the per-seed gaps reported span 0.011–0.143 [\texttt{f-20261014-040037-f9bb}].
\item \textbf{12000 steps.} $N = 128$ ends at 0.6894 (seed 22, layer 3) and 0.7197 (seed 23, layer 1). That is still 0.078 / 0.050 below the 3000-step $N = 64$ values [\texttt{f-20261014-050044-cd2f}].
\item Seed 22's force margin of 0.094 does not beat its control.
\item From step 6000 to 12000, force gained only 0.008 / 0.029, while \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} fell about 6–10×.
\item Seed 22 has levelled off. Seed 23 is still rising slowly (about 0.002 per 1000 steps), so its lower ceiling is not yet shown to be a plateau.
\item \texttt{accel\_step} levelled off at normalised margins of 0.773 / 0.787.
\end{itemize}

| $\le 0.8$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}] | 0.372–0.396 | 0.670–0.672 | 0.455–0.475 |
| $\le 0.85$ [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}] | 0.248–0.333 | 0.507–0.594 | 0.34–0.40 |

\textbf{The decline steepens above $e \le 0.75$} [\texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}].

\textbf{Force is weaker at every eccentricity ceiling.} Its normalised margins are about 0.11–0.22, and it forms late, at steps 250–590 [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}, \texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}, \texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}, \texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}].

\textbf{Linear decodability stops tracking prediction quality in a larger model} (6 layers, $d_\text{model} = 128$, $e \le 0.85$). Linear \texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ levels off at about 0.70–0.74 after step 2000. Over the same steps, \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} keeps falling 5.5–6.8× [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}].

\subsection{6. Out-of-distribution transfer}

\textbf{Trained on $e \le 0.5$} [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Low mass ratios} ($m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$): error is about 3.6× the in-distribution error.
\item \textbf{High eccentricity} ($e \in [0.5, 0.8]$): error is 16–18× in-distribution, which fails the pre-registered 10× criterion (refuted). The models still beat the baseline, and force $R^2$ falls to 0.40 / 0.34.
\item \textbf{Three-body sets:} ratios of 72 and 70 are exploratory only.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Trained on $e \in [0, 0.8]$, the high-$e$ gap closes} to 1.01–1.05× in-distribution [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

\begin{itemize}
\item The budget hard stop ended both runs at step 2500 (completion 0.867 and 0.9), so their probes describe the step-2500 weights.
\item Even with matched prediction error, force $R^2$ on held-out high-$e$ systems is only 0.366–0.377, against 0.729–0.802 on low-$e$ systems. These out-of-distribution probes have no random controls.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{7. Symbolic regression has not recovered $r^{-2}$}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Strong \texttt{accel\_step} probes} ($R^2$ 0.831 / 0.881): the fitted direction has the form $a^2/r - c\,a$ with $c \approx 0.55$, not $a^3/r^2$. Fit errors are 0.245 / 0.201. Force fits as about $0.8/r$. The finding reports the same $r^{-1}$ form in 3 of 3 seeds [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}].
\item \textbf{Training at $e \le 0.9$} (\texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ 0.395 / 0.503): the fits are again $r^{-1}$. The hypothesis that wider eccentricity would yield $r^{-2}$ was refuted [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{Possible fitter artefact:} the fitter has not yet been run on the true \texttt{accel\_step} target, so we cannot rule out that the $r^{-1}$ form comes from the fitter rather than the model.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{8. Reproducibility}

\textbf{The original $N = 256$ reference margins did not reproduce on an exact rerun (refuted).} The rerun gave force margins of 0.113 / 0.003. Its step-3000 values agree with 6000- and 12000-step runs to within about 0.025 [\texttt{f-20261013-050513-2c7a}].

\textbf{The current pipeline is deterministic for a fixed seed.} A second identical rerun matched the first to 3 decimals at every checkpoint [\texttt{f-20261013-054603-d6cc}]. Two consequences follow:

\begin{itemize}
\item The 0.11 force gap between seeds 20 and 21 at $N = 256$ is genuine seed variance.
\item Why the original runs differ is still unexplained.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{9. Null and negative results}

The 13 refuted hypotheses:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Widening $m_2$ would put force behind \texttt{accel\_step}.} It did not [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item \textbf{$e \le 0.5$ training would transfer to high $e$ within 10×.} The error was 16–18× [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}].
\item \textbf{Fixing $m_2$ would lift force $R^2$ to 0.85.} It did not; one of the two runs was below 80\% completion [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}].
\item \textbf{Wide-$e$ training would yield an $r^{-2}$ fit.} The fits were $r^{-1}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{\texttt{accel\_step} would clear a 0.15 margin, and the early force spike would vanish under best-of-5 controls.} Neither happened [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].
\item \textbf{Formation would scale in proportion to the learning rate.} The shift was sub-proportional [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}].
\item \textbf{$N = 64$ formation would fall in a narrow window.} Only 2 of 5 seeds did [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\item \textbf{$N = 16$ would raise the normalised \texttt{accel\_step} margin.} It was level with $N = 64$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}].
\item \textbf{Force would decline gradually from $N = 16$.} $N = 64$ matched $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}].
\item \textbf{An exact rerun would reproduce the original $N = 256$ margins.} It did not [\texttt{f-20261013-050513-2c7a}].
\item \textbf{The $N = 128 \to 256$ force drop is entirely a control effect.} On data seed 0, trained $R^2$ also fell [\texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}].
\item \textbf{1000 extra steps would close the $N = 256$ gap.} They did not [\texttt{f-20261013-083108-2efd}].
\item \textbf{Fixing $m_2$ would remove the $N = 64 \to 128$ drop.} The drop persisted [\texttt{f-20261013-234552-f8e8}].
\end{enumerate}

Several supported findings are also nulls for force, in that force fails its control:

\begin{itemize}
\item short $N = 64$ runs [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}];
\item $N = 256$ in most seeds [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}, \texttt{f-20261013-063603-95b5}, \texttt{f-20261013-063631-c4ed}];
\item $m_2$-fixed runs at $N = 128$ [\texttt{f-20261014-004426-68d6}, \texttt{f-20261014-015607-930a}], including seed 22 after 12000 steps [\texttt{f-20261014-050044-cd2f}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{10. Not yet tested, and limits}

No finding yet reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{velocity} probe results;
\item activation patching or any other causal test;
\item a force probe with \texttt{distance} and \texttt{accel\_step} regressed out;
\item a random-control pool shared across $N$;
\item nonlinear or log-target probes;
\item symbolic regression on the true \texttt{accel\_step} target.
\end{itemize}

Limits that apply throughout:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Probes.} All probes are linear.
\item \textbf{Single controls.} The earliest findings used a single random control [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}, \texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}, \texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item \textbf{Formation steps.} \texttt{formed\_at\_step} sometimes lags the trajectory by one checkpoint [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}, \texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}, \texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}].
\item \textbf{Rounded values.} Several paired comparisons use 3-decimal trajectory values whose $R^2$ field may be a maximum over layers [\texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}, \texttt{f-20261014-004426-68d6}, \texttt{f-20261014-015607-930a}].
\item \textbf{Training length.} Force was still rising at the end of most runs.
\item \textbf{Datasets and architecture.} Only two data seeds have been tested, and most results come from one architecture.
\end{itemize}

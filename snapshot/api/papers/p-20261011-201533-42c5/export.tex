\section{Abstract}

We ask whether small transformers trained to predict simulated orbits ($G = M = 1$) encode the physical law $|F| = m_2/r^2$ or a substitute that works only for the task, and when any such structure forms. We fit linear probes to the residual stream and compare each against best-of-several random-model controls. The probes target both physical force and the per-snapshot acceleration $(F/m_2)\,dt^2$ (\texttt{accel\_step}), which is what next-step prediction actually needs. We report 31 replicated findings (21 supported, 10 refuted) from 76 evidence runs; the budget hard stop cut 4 of those runs short. At $N = 64$, \texttt{accel\_step} clears the 0.1 margin at steps 85–140, before \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} falls below 1 [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}]. It leads force on headroom-normalised margin in all multi-seed comparisons but one, for example 0.725/0.740 against 0.44/0.45 [\texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}]. Force forms later and peaks at the \texttt{distance} layer [\texttt{f-20261013-020028-8792}]. It also weakens with denser sampling: on data seed 1 the margin is 0.209 at $N = 64$ and 0.137/0.145 at $N = 128$ [\texttt{f-20261013-054542-23e1}]. The main negative result is that symbolic fits to the probe directions give $r^{-1}$, not $r^{-2}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}, \texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data generation}

All orbits are simulated with REBOUND in units where $G = 1$ and the primary mass is $M = 1$. The gravitational force on body 2 is therefore $|F| = m_2/r^2$, where $m_2$ is the mass ratio. Varying $M$ would add nothing. Positions are sampled at fixed phase intervals, so absolute mass and time scale cannot be identified from them.

Each orbit is sampled at a fixed number of snapshots per orbit, $N$ (\texttt{steps\_per\_orbit}). The time between snapshots is therefore $\Delta t = P/N$, which grows with orbit size ($\Delta t^2 \propto a^3$). The model never sees physical time.

Three-body systems are hierarchical: the third body sits at 3.5–6× the inner semi-major axis. They are used only as an evaluation set for models trained on two-body systems.

The configuration schema adds these fields to the specification:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{data.eccentricity\_range}
\item \texttt{data.data\_seed}
\item \texttt{probes.control\_margin}
\item \texttt{probes.patching}
\item \texttt{probes.symbolic}
\item \texttt{eval\_sets}, for out-of-distribution evaluation
\end{itemize}

Unknown fields are rejected. The training ranges differ between the cited experiments:

\begin{itemize}
\item Some runs train on the default range, which reaches $e \leq 0.5$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item Some runs widen the mass ratio to $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item Some runs widen eccentricity to $e \in [0, 0.8]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\item Some runs widen both, to $e \in [0, 0.9]$ and $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. Both of these runs share data seed 0.
\end{itemize}

The held-out evaluation sets are:

\begin{itemize}
\item low mass ratio, $m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$
\item high eccentricity, $e \in [0.5, 0.8]$
\item three-body systems [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]
\item held-out low-$e$ and high-$e$ splits, used for the wide-eccentricity runs [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tokens, model and prediction target}

Each token is one time step. It holds the $xy$ positions of up to three bodies (6 numbers) in the centre-of-mass frame, with absent bodies set to zero. Because every system uses the same token format, a model trained on two bodies can be evaluated on three-body sets.

The model predicts the normalised displacement to the next step. All evidence runs use one transformer architecture: $L = 4$ layers, width $d = 64$, and $N = 64$ snapshots per orbit [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Prediction quality is reported as \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio}. This is the model's MSE divided by the MSE of constant-velocity extrapolation, $\hat{x}_{t+1} = 2x_t - x_{t-1}$. Both are computed in physical position units over steps $t \geq 1$ on the same held-out systems. A value below 1 means the model beats the baseline.

\subsection{Training}

The lab's default first-experiment (E1) configuration trains for 5k steps at batch size 128 and learning rate $10^{-3}$. Training runs on a laptop GPU (MPS) and is kept CPU-friendly.

Checkpoint spacing varies between experiments. One formation experiment probes every 250 steps [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}]. A dense-checkpoint experiment probes every 25 steps early in training [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].

Every finding is replicated on two training seeds.

\textbf{Budget hard stop.} A runaway guard stops a run once it reaches 2× its seed's share of \texttt{budget.max\_compute\_hours}. The clock starts at the first training step, so dataset generation does not count. A stopped run is checkpointed and probed at its stop step. It records \texttt{steps\_completed} and \texttt{steps\_planned}, and its \texttt{completion} is the fraction of planned steps it trained. Runs below 80\% completion do not count toward replication.

Two of the 12 evidence runs were cut short by an earlier version of this guard. That version stopped at 1.2× the budget share and counted dataset generation against it. Both runs are in [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]: seed 9 stopped at completion 0.867 (step 2640 of 3000) and seed 8 at 0.9 (step 2714). Under that version the probes were taken at the last regular checkpoint, step 2500, not at the stop step. Their \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still falling when training stopped.

\subsection{Probe targets}

Linear probes read five quantities from the residual stream at every layer:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{distance}
\item relative \texttt{velocity}
\item \texttt{energy}, which is constant per system
\item physical \texttt{force}, $|F| = m_2/r^2$
\item the per-snapshot acceleration \texttt{accel\_step}:
\end{itemize}

\[|a_{\text{step}}| = \frac{F}{m_2}\,\Delta t^2 = \left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\right)^2 \frac{a^3}{(1+m_2)\,r^2}.\]

Next-step prediction needs \texttt{accel\_step}, not the physical force. A weak \texttt{force} probe on its own could therefore mean the model learned a per-snapshot version of gravity, not that it learned no gravity. For this reason \texttt{force} and \texttt{accel\_step} are always compared.

A unit test checks two properties of \texttt{accel\_step}:

\begin{itemize}
\item It equals the second difference of positions, to within 1\%.
\item It is not proportional to the physical force across orbit sizes.
\end{itemize}

\texttt{velocity} probe results do not appear in any cited finding.

\subsection{Probe protocol and random-model controls}

Probes run on 300 held-out systems, at every other time step from $t = 8$. Systems are split 70/30 into probe-train and probe-test sets, so rows from the same orbit never appear on both sides.

Each probe is a ridge regression (RidgeCV over $\alpha \in [10^{-3}, 10^{4}]$) on standardised inputs and outputs. $R^2$ is averaged across output dimensions.

Every probe has a control: the same probe fitted on a randomly initialised model of the same architecture. There is one control per layer, because a random model does not change across checkpoints. A probe result counts only if

\[R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}} \geq 0.1 \quad (\texttt{probes.control\_margin}).\]

For each target, the lab reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the best layer
\item its $R^2$ and control $R^2$
\item \texttt{formed\_at\_step}, the first checkpoint at which the margin reaches the threshold (null if it never does)
\end{itemize}

The control $R^2$ for \texttt{accel\_step} is high under the default ranges (0.71–0.75). This caps the margin \texttt{accel\_step} can reach. One finding therefore also reports a post hoc, headroom-normalised score, $(R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}})/(1 - R^2_{\text{control}})$, but its pre-registered criterion uses the raw margin [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].

Probes on the out-of-distribution evaluation sets have no random controls. Their $R^2$ values therefore cannot be read as margins [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}, \texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Only the final checkpoint's activations are saved to disk, as float16. Activations for earlier checkpoints are recomputed from the saved weights when needed.

\subsection{Symbolic regression}

When \texttt{probes.symbolic} is enabled, PySR fits a closed-form expression to the best-layer probe direction for \texttt{force} and for \texttt{accel\_step}. The \texttt{accel\_step} fit uses the inputs $r$, $m_2$ and $a$. Each fit reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the equation
\item whether the true law was recovered (\texttt{law\_recovered})
\item \texttt{fit\_error\_vs\_true\_law}
\end{itemize}

Symbolic fits were run at the final checkpoint only, and only in the runs behind [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. These are the only evidence runs with fitted equations.

\subsection{Activation patching}

The pipeline also supports activation patching. Heads are ranked by a force probe on each head's contribution to the residual stream. The top three heads, plus the lowest-ranked head as a control, are each patched with that head's output on a different system, and the relative increase in next-step MSE is recorded. None of the cited findings report patching results.

\section{Results}

The lab has 31 citable findings, all replicated. Of these, 21 support their hypothesis and 10 refute it. They rest on 76 evidence runs, and the budget hard stop cut 4 of those runs short. Every probe result below is a margin over the best of several randomly initialised controls, unless a finding says otherwise.

\subsection{Prediction quality}

The trained models beat constant-velocity extrapolation by a wide margin:

\begin{itemize}
\item The seeded E1 setup ended at a \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} of about 0.011 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}].
\item At $N = 16$ with 3000 steps it reached 0.0007 [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}].
\item The 6-layer, $d_{\text{model}} = 128$ model reached 0.0037 and 0.0032 [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}].
\item At $N = 64$ with 12000 steps it reached 0.0030 and 0.0031 [\texttt{f-20261013-051738-2a37}].
\end{itemize}

The one exception is dense sampling with short training. At $N = 256$, $e \le 0.85$ and 600 steps, the models never beat the baseline: final \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was 2.03–2.37 [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}]. At $N = 256$ with 3000 steps they did, at 0.237 and 0.282 [\texttt{f-20261013-054603-d6cc}].

\subsection{Per-snapshot acceleration forms early and at a stable training step}

\textbf{\texttt{accel\_step} is linearly decodable above its control in every configuration tested.} It forms early, usually before the model beats the baseline:

| Setting | \texttt{accel\_step} forms (step) | \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} first < 1 (step) |
|---|---|---|
| E1, 250-step checkpoints | 250, 250 | 1500, 1500 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}] |
| $N = 64$, 25-step checkpoints | 100, 100 | 175 [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}] |
| $N = 64$, 10-step checkpoints | 140, 140, 100 | 180, 180, 190 [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}] |
| $N = 64$, best-of-5 controls | 85–90 | 135–165 [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}] |
| $N = 64$, seeds 62–64 | 90, 100, 100 | 140–160 [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}] |
| $N = 64$, 5-step checkpoints, 5 seeds | 65–90 | — [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}] |

\texttt{distance} and \texttt{energy} form earlier still, around steps 5–40, while the model is far worse than the baseline [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}, \texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].

\textbf{The formation step depends on training steps, not on loss level.} Several results point the same way:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$N = 16$.} \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} falls below 1 at step 20, but \texttt{accel\_step} forms at steps 75–90, within 5–15 steps of the seed-matched $N = 64$ runs. The ordering flips because the baseline is weaker at coarse sampling; the formation step barely moves [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].
\item \textbf{$N = 256$ at $e \le 0.85$.} \texttt{accel\_step} forms at steps 60–70 [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}], the same window as the paired $N = 64$ arm (60, 60, 70) [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}], even though these models never beat the baseline.
\item \textbf{Learning rate.} Halving it delays formation only to steps 70, 80 and 100 [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}].
\end{itemize}

!\href{figure:formation/run-20261012-015504-ae8c}{Formation timeline, N = 16, seed 47: val\_mse\_ratio drops below 1 before accel\_step forms}

!\href{figure:heatmap/run-20261012-043022-3319/accel_step}{accel\_step probe heatmap, N = 64, seed 62}

\textbf{Caveat on thin margins.} At $e \le 0.5$ the \texttt{accel\_step} control is high (0.71–0.79), so final margins sit close to the 0.1 bar: 0.109–0.158 [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}]. In two of three seeds the margin never reached 0.15 [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}]. Formation steps therefore depend on the threshold.

\subsection{Physical force: absent in short runs, late and growing in long ones}

\textbf{In short runs, force mostly did not beat its control.} Final force margins were:

\begin{itemize}
\item 0.040, 0.000 and 0.062 [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}];
\item 0.039–0.059 [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}];
\item 0.033–0.080 [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}];
\item 0.065–0.078 at half the learning rate [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}];
\item 0.004–0.031 at $N = 256$ [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}].
\end{itemize}

There were a few borderline exceptions: 0.103 and 0.105 in 2 of 5 seeds [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}], and 0.123 in one $N = 16$ seed [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].

\textbf{Every short run also showed a brief early force spike.} The margin peaked at about 0.11–0.19 around steps 10–25, then fell to about 0 by steps 40–50 [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}, \texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}, \texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}, \texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}]. Best-of-5 controls did not remove it [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].

\textbf{With longer training, a force code appears.} Final force margins after 1500–12000 steps:

\begin{itemize}
\item 0.223 and 0.230 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}];
\item 0.243 and 0.235 with $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}];
\item 0.247 and 0.234 at $N = 64$, 3000 steps [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}];
\item 0.209 and 0.209 at $N = 64$, 3000 steps, data seed 1 [\texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}];
\item 0.255 and 0.266 at $N = 64$, 12000 steps, gaining only 0.019 and 0.007 between steps 6000 and 12000 [\texttt{f-20261013-051738-2a37}];
\item 0.236 and 0.275 at $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}];
\item 0.221 and 0.255 in a 2-layer, $d = 32$ model [\texttt{f-20261013-000220-ab2d}];
\item 0.300 and 0.333 in the 6-layer, $d_{\text{model}} = 128$ model [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}];
\item 0.187 and 0.208 at $d_{\text{model}} = 128$, data seed 1 [\texttt{f-20261013-020028-8792}];
\item 0.196, 0.193 and 0.167 at 1500 steps, data seed 1 [\texttt{f-20261013-025812-b9ee}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Force forms well after \texttt{accel\_step}.}

\begin{itemize}
\item At $d_{\text{model}} = 128$, force formed persistently at steps 400 and 350, against step 100 for \texttt{accel\_step}. From step 500 to 5000, force gained +0.074 and +0.081 in margin, while \texttt{accel\_step} gained +0.025 and +0.022 [\texttt{f-20261013-020028-8792}].
\item At $d_{\text{model}} = 64$ on data seed 1, force first held the bar at steps 400, 450 and 600. \texttt{accel\_step} gained only 0.006–0.018 after step 600 [\texttt{f-20261013-025812-b9ee}]. That finding concludes the short-run "no force code" null is partly an artefact of where training stopped or of the learning-rate schedule horizon.
\item At $e \le 0.8$ and $e \le 0.75$, force formed at steps 250–500 and was still rising at step 600 [\texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}, \texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}].
\end{itemize}

!\href{figure:formation/run-20261013-013406-a8e7}{Formation timeline, d\_model = 128, data seed 1, seed 80: force forms about 250 steps after accel\_step}

!\href{figure:heatmap/run-20261013-043205-0031/force}{Force probe heatmap, N = 64, 12000 steps, seed 20}

\textbf{Which code leads depends on the metric.} Force often has the larger raw margin, because the \texttt{accel\_step} control is high. Measured as a fraction of the available headroom, $(R^2 - c)/(1 - c)$, \texttt{accel\_step} leads in every multi-seed comparison except the 2-layer model:

| Setting | \texttt{accel\_step} | force |
|---|---|---|
| Wide $m_2$ range [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}] | 0.70, 0.71 | 0.48, 0.42 |
| 6-layer model [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}] | 0.66, 0.61 | 0.41 |
| $N = 64$, 3000 steps [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}] | 0.68, 0.70 | 0.45, 0.43 |
| $N = 64$, data seed 1 [\texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}] | 0.725, 0.740 | 0.44, 0.45 |
| $N = 128$, data seed 0 [\texttt{f-20261013-051807-f3f1}] | 0.67, 0.63 | ≈0.20, 0.18 |
| $N = 128$, data seed 1 [\texttt{f-20261013-054542-23e1}] | 0.70, 0.72 | 0.29, 0.30 |
| $d_{\text{model}} = 128$, data seed 1 [\texttt{f-20261013-020028-8792}] | 0.86, 0.83 | 0.49, 0.53 |
| $d_{\text{model}} = 64$, 1500 steps, data seed 1 [\texttt{f-20261013-025812-b9ee}] | 0.66–0.71 | 0.35–0.43 |

In the 2-layer model the comparison splits, one seed each way [\texttt{f-20261013-000220-ab2d}].

\textbf{Confound: force may partly be a read of distance.} The layer where force decodes best is unstable:

\begin{itemize}
\item it ranges from layer 0 to layer 4 [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}];
\item it drifts across layers 1–6 in the 6-layer model [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}];
\item it moved 4 → 1 → 4 during training in one seed [\texttt{f-20261013-020028-8792}];
\item it differs between paired seeds (layer 1 vs 3) [\texttt{f-20261013-054542-23e1}].
\end{itemize}

In the 1500-step runs, force cleared the bar only after its best layer moved from 4 to 1, the layer where \texttt{distance} decodes best. Layer-4 force never exceeded about 0.085 there [\texttt{f-20261013-025812-b9ee}]. In most long runs force also peaks at layer 1, the \texttt{distance} layer [\texttt{f-20261013-000220-ab2d}, \texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}, \texttt{f-20261013-051738-2a37}, \texttt{f-20261013-051807-f3f1}]. Force is also collinear with \texttt{distance} and \texttt{accel\_step} through $1/r^2$. Until a residualised probe is run, the force margin should be read as force decodability, not as evidence of a separate $m_2$-weighted force representation.

\textbf{Fixing the mass ratio did not help.} With $m_2 = 0.5$, the force law is exactly $|F| = 0.5/r^2$. Even so, force $R^2$ stayed at 0.750 and 0.732, while \texttt{distance} decoded at 0.87–0.89 [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}]. The budget hard stop cut both runs short. Seed 14 reached only 0.55 completion, below the 80\% replication bar, so the result rests mainly on seed 15 (0.917 completion).

\subsection{Sampling density}

\textbf{Force decodability falls as sampling gets denser; normalised \texttt{accel\_step} does not.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$N = 16$ vs $N = 64$.} At 3000 steps the force margins match: 0.236–0.275 at $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}] against 0.247 and 0.234 at $N = 64$ [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}].
\item \textbf{$N = 128$, data seed 0, fresh seeds 22–23.} Force margins were 0.108 and 0.094. That is 0.139–0.140 below the matched $N = 64$ seeds, and seed 23 never reached the bar. Across the four $N = 128$ seeds the range is 0.094–0.150 [\texttt{f-20261013-051807-f3f1}]. The drop comes from lower trained $R^2$ (0.56–0.57 vs 0.68–0.70), not from higher controls.
\item \textbf{$N = 128$, data seed 1.} Force margins were 0.137 and 0.145, against 0.209 at $N = 64$. That is a drop of 0.072 and 0.064, smaller than the roughly 0.10–0.14 drop on data seed 0 [\texttt{f-20261013-054542-23e1}, \texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}]. The direction replicates; the size depends on the dataset.
\item \textbf{12000 steps.} The $N = 64$ force margin (0.255, 0.266) stays 0.062 and 0.069 above $N = 128$ (0.193, 0.197). In seed 21 the gap shrank from 0.091 at step 6000, so longer training might narrow it further [\texttt{f-20261013-051738-2a37}].
\end{itemize}

!\href{figure:heatmap/run-20261013-050519-2b49/force}{Force probe heatmap, N = 128, seed 23: force never beats its control}

\textbf{The original $N = 256$ reference did not reproduce, but the reruns are deterministic.} An earlier finding had concluded that force drops sharply between $N = 64$ and $N = 256$ [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}]. That rested on original $N = 256$ runs with force margins of 0.033–0.040. An exact rerun of the $N = 256$, 3000-step config gave different results [\texttt{f-20261013-050513-2c7a}]:

| Seed | force margin | \texttt{accel\_step} margin |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | 0.113 ($R^2$ 0.644 vs control 0.531) | 0.162 |
| 21 | 0.003 (does not beat control) | 0.198 |

The original runs had \texttt{accel\_step} margins of 0.231 and 0.245.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Schedule horizon ruled out.} At step 3000 the rerun matches 6000-step and 12000-step runs to within about 0.025, so the original runs are the outlier [\texttt{f-20261013-050513-2c7a}].
\item \textbf{Second rerun identical.} A second identical rerun matched the first to 3 decimals at all 7 checkpoints, for all four probes. Under the current pipeline, training and probing are therefore deterministic for a fixed seed [\texttt{f-20261013-054603-d6cc}].
\item \textbf{What follows.} The 0.11 force gap between seeds 20 and 21 at $N = 256$ is real seed-to-seed variance. The up-to-0.025 differences between run lengths come from the learning-rate schedule horizon [\texttt{f-20261013-054603-d6cc}].
\item \textbf{Open question.} Why the original runs differ is unresolved. A higher \texttt{accel\_step} control in the reruns (0.71–0.76 against 0.65–0.68) is suggested, but only inferred from summary numbers [\texttt{f-20261013-050513-2c7a}].
\end{itemize}

Taken together, the $N = 128$ arms now place force between $N = 64$ and $N = 256$ on both data seeds. The "sharp step" reading is no longer supported, although $N = 256$ force varies a lot between seeds.

!\href{figure:heatmap/run-20261013-042340-4a86/force}{Force probe heatmap, N = 256 exact rerun, seed 21: force never beats its control}

\textbf{Normalised \texttt{accel\_step} margin across $N$:}

\begin{itemize}
\item 0.68–0.69 at $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}];
\item 0.68–0.70 at $N = 64$ [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}];
\item 0.725–0.740 at $N = 64$, data seed 1 [\texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}];
\item 0.76 and 0.65 at $N = 64$, 12000 steps [\texttt{f-20261013-051738-2a37}];

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Eccentricity sets the control level}

As the training eccentricity range widens, both the control $R^2$ and the trained $R^2$ for \texttt{accel\_step} fall:

| $e$ range | control $R^2$ | trained $R^2$ | normalised margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\le 0.5$ | 0.724–0.746 | 0.890–0.902 | — |
| $\le 0.7$ | 0.543–0.559 | 0.80–0.81 | ≈0.55 |
| $\le 0.75$ | 0.463–0.487 | 0.746–0.753 | 0.51–0.54 |
| $\le 0.8$ | 0.372–0.396 | 0.670–0.672 | 0.455–0.475 |
| $\le 0.85$ | 0.248–0.333 | 0.507–0.594 | 0.34–0.40 |

Sources: [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}, \texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}, \texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}, \texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}, \texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}].

The decline is uneven. About 0.05 of $R^2$ is lost from $e \le 0.7$ to $e \le 0.75$, then about 0.19 from $e \le 0.75$ to $e \le 0.85$ [\texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}]. All of these arms share data seed 0. Force stays much weaker than \texttt{accel\_step} at every eccentricity ceiling, with normalised force margins of 0.16–0.22 at $e \le 0.7$–$0.8$ [\texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}, \texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}, \texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}].

\subsection{Linear decodability stops tracking prediction quality}

In the 6-layer model at $e \le 0.85$, after step 2000 [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}]:

\begin{itemize}
\item layer-6 \texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ rose only from 0.710 to 0.735 and from 0.683 to 0.705;
\item over the same steps, \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} fell 6.8× and 5.5×.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Out-of-distribution transfer}

\textbf{Trained on $e \le 0.5$} [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]:

\begin{itemize}
\item On high-$e$ systems, error was 16–18× the in-distribution error (\texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} 0.270 and 0.249). This fails the pre-registered 10× criterion, although the model still beats the baseline.
\item Transfer to low mass ratios held at about 3.6× the in-distribution error.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Trained on $e \in [0, 0.8]$,} the high-$e$ gap closed to about 1.05× and 1.01× [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]:

\begin{itemize}
\item The budget hard stop cut both runs short at step 2500 (completion 0.867 and 0.9), while \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still falling.
\item Force probe $R^2$ on held-out high-$e$ systems stayed low: 0.366–0.377, against 0.729–0.802 on low-$e$ systems. These out-of-distribution probes have no random controls.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Symbolic regression has not recovered $r^{-2}$}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{With strong probes} ($R^2$ 0.831 and 0.881), the fits to the \texttt{accel\_step} direction were $r^{-1}$, of the form $a^2/r - c\,a$ with $c \approx 0.55$ (fit errors 0.245 and 0.201). The force fits were $0.81/r$ and $0.80/r$ [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}].
\item \textbf{At $e \le 0.9$, with weak probes} ($R^2$ 0.395 and 0.503), the fits were also $r^{-1}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{Open check:} the fitter has not been run on the true \texttt{accel\_step} target, so a fitting artefact cannot be ruled out [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Null and negative results}

Ten hypotheses were refuted:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A wider $m_2$ range would make force fall behind \texttt{accel\_step} on margin.} Force kept the larger raw margin [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item \textbf{A model trained on $e \le 0.5$ would transfer to high $e$ within 10×.} The gap was 16–18× [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}].
\item \textbf{Fixing $m_2 = 0.5$ would lift force $R^2$ to 0.85.} It reached 0.750 and 0.732, and the budget stop cut both runs short [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}].
\item \textbf{Wide-$e$ training would yield an $r^{-2}$ symbolic fit.} The fits were $r^{-1}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{At $N = 64$, \texttt{accel\_step} would clear a 0.15 bar and the early force spike would vanish under best-of-5 controls.} Neither held [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].
\item \textbf{Formation would scale in proportion to the learning rate.} The shift was sub-proportional [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}].
\item \textbf{$N = 64$ formation would fall in a narrow pre-registered window.} Only 2 of 5 seeds did, against the 4 required. Finer checkpoint spacing may explain part of the shift [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\item \textbf{Coarser sampling ($N = 16$) would raise the normalised \texttt{accel\_step} margin.} It was 0.69 and 0.68 [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}], level with $N = 64$ [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}].
\item \textbf{Force margin would decline gradually from $N = 16$ to $N = 256$.} It was refuted because $N = 64$ sits at the $N = 16$ level [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}]. That verdict relied on the non-reproducing $N = 256$ runs. The later $N = 128$ arms show an intermediate force level [\texttt{f-20261013-051807-f3f1}, \texttt{f-20261013-054542-23e1}].
\item \textbf{An exact rerun of the $N = 256$, 3000-step config would reproduce the original margins within 0.02.} It did not, and the schedule-horizon explanation was also refuted [\texttt{f-20261013-050513-2c7a}]. A second rerun then reproduced the first exactly [\texttt{f-20261013-054603-d6cc}].
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Not yet tested, and limits}

\textbf{Not yet reported:}

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{velocity} probe results;
\item activation patching, or any other causal test of whether the \texttt{accel\_step} or force codes are used;
\item any force probe with \texttt{distance} and \texttt{accel\_step} regressed out of the target;
\item a check of the original $N = 256$ runs' per-layer $R^2$ to explain why they did not reproduce [\texttt{f-20261013-050513-2c7a}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Limits throughout:}

\begin{itemize}
\item All probes are linear.
\item \texttt{formed\_at\_step} sometimes lags the trajectory by one checkpoint. Where the two disagree, the findings use the trajectory value [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}, \texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}, \texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}].
\item Four findings use data seed 1 [\texttt{f-20261013-020028-8792}, \texttt{f-20261013-025812-b9ee}, \texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}, \texttt{f-20261013-054542-23e1}]. All others use data seed 0.
\item Determinism has been shown for one config on one machine [\texttt{f-20261013-054603-d6cc}].
\item Most results come from a single 4-layer architecture.
\end{itemize}

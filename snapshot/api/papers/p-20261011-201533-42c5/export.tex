\section{Abstract}

We ask whether small transformers that predict orbits well encode Newtonian gravity or a substitute that is specific to the task. We train them on REBOUND two-body orbits sampled at $N$ snapshots per orbit. We then fit linear probes, each judged against best-of-several random-model controls, for the physical force $|F| = m_2/r^2$ and for the per-snapshot acceleration \texttt{accel\_step} $\propto a^3/r^2$. The lab has 38 replicated findings (26 supported, 12 refuted) from 93 runs. At $N = 64$, \texttt{accel\_step} forms at steps 85–140, before the model beats constant-velocity extrapolation [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}]. Its headroom-normalised margin stays at 0.63–0.75 from $N = 16$ to $N = 256$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}, \texttt{f-20261013-075824-35f0}, \texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}]. Force decodability falls as $N$ grows. In seeds 24–26 the force margin is 0.216–0.255 at $N = 64$, 0.079–0.143 at $N = 128$ and 0.026–0.045 at $N = 256$ [\texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}, \texttt{f-20261013-075824-35f0}, \texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}]. The $N = 256$ shortfall remains when runs are matched on \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} [\texttt{f-20261013-093210-2e03}]. The main negative result is that symbolic fits to the probe directions give $r^{-1}$, never $r^{-2}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}, \texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. Force also peaks at the layer that encodes distance, so it may partly be a read of distance [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}].

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data generation}

All orbits are simulated with REBOUND in units where $G = 1$ and the primary mass is $M = 1$. The gravitational force on body 2 is therefore $|F| = m_2/r^2$, where $m_2$ is the mass ratio. Varying $M$ would add nothing. Positions are sampled at fixed phase intervals, so absolute mass and time scale cannot be identified from them.

Each orbit is sampled at a fixed number of snapshots per orbit, $N$ (\texttt{steps\_per\_orbit}). The time between snapshots is therefore $\Delta t = P/N$, which grows with orbit size ($\Delta t^2 \propto a^3$). The model never sees physical time.

Three-body systems are hierarchical: the third body sits at 3.5–6× the inner semi-major axis. They are used only as an evaluation set for models trained on two-body systems.

The configuration schema adds these fields to the specification:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{data.eccentricity\_range}
\item \texttt{data.data\_seed}
\item \texttt{probes.control\_margin}
\item \texttt{probes.patching}
\item \texttt{probes.symbolic}
\item \texttt{eval\_sets}, for out-of-distribution evaluation
\end{itemize}

Unknown fields are rejected. The training ranges differ between the cited experiments:

\begin{itemize}
\item Some runs train on the default range, which reaches $e \leq 0.5$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item Some runs widen the mass ratio to $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item Some runs widen eccentricity to $e \in [0, 0.8]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\item Some runs widen both, to $e \in [0, 0.9]$ and $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. Both of these runs share data seed 0.
\end{itemize}

The held-out evaluation sets are:

\begin{itemize}
\item low mass ratio, $m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$
\item high eccentricity, $e \in [0.5, 0.8]$
\item three-body systems [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]
\item held-out low-$e$ and high-$e$ splits, used for the wide-eccentricity runs [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tokens, model and prediction target}

Each token is one time step. It holds the $xy$ positions of up to three bodies (6 numbers) in the centre-of-mass frame, with absent bodies set to zero. Because every system uses the same token format, a model trained on two bodies can be evaluated on three-body sets.

The model predicts the normalised displacement to the next step. All evidence runs use one transformer architecture: $L = 4$ layers, width $d = 64$, and $N = 64$ snapshots per orbit [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Prediction quality is reported as \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio}. This is the model's MSE divided by the MSE of constant-velocity extrapolation, $\hat{x}_{t+1} = 2x_t - x_{t-1}$. Both are computed in physical position units over steps $t \geq 1$ on the same held-out systems. A value below 1 means the model beats the baseline.

\subsection{Training}

The lab's default first-experiment (E1) configuration trains for 5k steps at batch size 128 and learning rate $10^{-3}$. Training runs on a laptop GPU (MPS) and is kept CPU-friendly.

Checkpoint spacing varies between experiments. One formation experiment probes every 250 steps [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}]. A dense-checkpoint experiment probes every 25 steps early in training [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].

Every finding is replicated on two training seeds.

\textbf{Budget hard stop.} A runaway guard stops a run once it reaches 2× its seed's share of \texttt{budget.max\_compute\_hours}. The clock starts at the first training step, so dataset generation does not count. A stopped run is checkpointed and probed at its stop step. It records \texttt{steps\_completed} and \texttt{steps\_planned}, and its \texttt{completion} is the fraction of planned steps it trained. Runs below 80\% completion do not count toward replication.

Two of the 12 evidence runs were cut short by an earlier version of this guard. That version stopped at 1.2× the budget share and counted dataset generation against it. Both runs are in [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]: seed 9 stopped at completion 0.867 (step 2640 of 3000) and seed 8 at 0.9 (step 2714). Under that version the probes were taken at the last regular checkpoint, step 2500, not at the stop step. Their \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still falling when training stopped.

\subsection{Probe targets}

Linear probes read five quantities from the residual stream at every layer:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{distance}
\item relative \texttt{velocity}
\item \texttt{energy}, which is constant per system
\item physical \texttt{force}, $|F| = m_2/r^2$
\item the per-snapshot acceleration \texttt{accel\_step}:
\end{itemize}

\[|a_{\text{step}}| = \frac{F}{m_2}\,\Delta t^2 = \left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\right)^2 \frac{a^3}{(1+m_2)\,r^2}.\]

Next-step prediction needs \texttt{accel\_step}, not the physical force. A weak \texttt{force} probe on its own could therefore mean the model learned a per-snapshot version of gravity, not that it learned no gravity. For this reason \texttt{force} and \texttt{accel\_step} are always compared.

A unit test checks two properties of \texttt{accel\_step}:

\begin{itemize}
\item It equals the second difference of positions, to within 1\%.
\item It is not proportional to the physical force across orbit sizes.
\end{itemize}

\texttt{velocity} probe results do not appear in any cited finding.

\subsection{Probe protocol and random-model controls}

Probes run on 300 held-out systems, at every other time step from $t = 8$. Systems are split 70/30 into probe-train and probe-test sets, so rows from the same orbit never appear on both sides.

Each probe is a ridge regression (RidgeCV over $\alpha \in [10^{-3}, 10^{4}]$) on standardised inputs and outputs. $R^2$ is averaged across output dimensions.

Every probe has a control: the same probe fitted on a randomly initialised model of the same architecture. There is one control per layer, because a random model does not change across checkpoints. A probe result counts only if

\[R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}} \geq 0.1 \quad (\texttt{probes.control\_margin}).\]

For each target, the lab reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the best layer
\item its $R^2$ and control $R^2$
\item \texttt{formed\_at\_step}, the first checkpoint at which the margin reaches the threshold (null if it never does)
\end{itemize}

The control $R^2$ for \texttt{accel\_step} is high under the default ranges (0.71–0.75). This caps the margin \texttt{accel\_step} can reach. One finding therefore also reports a post hoc, headroom-normalised score, $(R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}})/(1 - R^2_{\text{control}})$, but its pre-registered criterion uses the raw margin [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].

Probes on the out-of-distribution evaluation sets have no random controls. Their $R^2$ values therefore cannot be read as margins [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}, \texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Only the final checkpoint's activations are saved to disk, as float16. Activations for earlier checkpoints are recomputed from the saved weights when needed.

\subsection{Symbolic regression}

When \texttt{probes.symbolic} is enabled, PySR fits a closed-form expression to the best-layer probe direction for \texttt{force} and for \texttt{accel\_step}. The \texttt{accel\_step} fit uses the inputs $r$, $m_2$ and $a$. Each fit reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the equation
\item whether the true law was recovered (\texttt{law\_recovered})
\item \texttt{fit\_error\_vs\_true\_law}
\end{itemize}

Symbolic fits were run at the final checkpoint only, and only in the runs behind [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. These are the only evidence runs with fitted equations.

\subsection{Activation patching}

The pipeline also supports activation patching. Heads are ranked by a force probe on each head's contribution to the residual stream. The top three heads, plus the lowest-ranked head as a control, are each patched with that head's output on a different system, and the relative increase in next-step MSE is recorded. None of the cited findings report patching results.

\section{Results}

The lab has 38 citable findings. All 38 are replicated: 26 supported the hypothesis and 12 refuted it. They rest on 93 evidence runs. The lab's budget hard stop cut 4 of those runs short, and we flag them wherever their numbers appear. Every probe result below is judged against randomly initialised controls of the same architecture. Unless we say otherwise, "margin" means $R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}}$ at the best layer. "Normalised margin" means $(R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}})/(1 - R^2_{\text{control}})$.

\subsection{Prediction quality}

The models beat constant-velocity extrapolation across most configurations:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Default runs.} The 4-layer, $d_{\text{model}} = 64$ models reach a final \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} of about 0.011 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}].
\item \textbf{Longer or larger runs.} 12000 steps at $N = 64$ reach 0.0030 and 0.0031 [\texttt{f-20261013-051738-2a37}]. A 6-layer, $d_{\text{model}} = 128$ model reaches 0.0037 and 0.0032 [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}]. A $d_{\text{model}} = 128$ model trained for 5000 steps reaches 0.0059 and 0.0053 [\texttt{f-20261013-020028-8792}].
\item \textbf{$N = 16$.} The ratio reaches 0.0007 [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}]. The constant-velocity baseline is weaker at $N = 16$, though, so \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} is not comparable across $N$ [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].
\item \textbf{Fine sampling, short training.} At $N = 256$ with $e \le 0.85$ and only 600 steps, the models never beat the baseline (final 2.03–2.37) [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}]. With 3000 steps at $e \le 0.5$, $N = 256$ ends at 0.24–0.29 [\texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{When structure forms}

\textbf{\texttt{accel\_step} becomes decodable early and stays decodable.} At $N = 64$ and $e \le 0.5$, the \texttt{accel\_step} margin crosses the 0.1 bar before the model first beats the baseline:

\begin{itemize}
\item With 25-step checkpoints it crossed at step 100 in both seeds, while \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still 1.96 and 1.84. The ratio was first reported below 1 at step 175 [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item With 10-step checkpoints it crossed at steps 140, 140 and 100. That is 40–90 steps before \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} < 1 [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}].
\item In a second set of seeds it crossed at steps 85–90, which is 45–75 steps before \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} < 1 [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].
\item In a third set it held the bar for good from steps 90, 100 and 100. At that point \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still 1.7–2.1 [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}].
\end{itemize}

\texttt{distance} and \texttt{energy} form earlier still, at steps 10–30, when the model is far worse than the baseline [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}, \texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}].

!\href{figure:formation/run-20261011-234250-a7ca}{Probe formation, N = 64, seed 46, 10-step checkpoints}

\textbf{The ordering depends on the baseline, not on when \texttt{accel\_step} forms.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$N = 16$.} \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} drops below 1 at step 20. \texttt{accel\_step} forms at steps 75–90, within 5–15 steps of the seed-matched $N = 64$ runs. So "forms before beating the baseline" flips, while the formation step itself barely moves [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].
\item \textbf{$N = 256$, $e \le 0.85$.} \texttt{accel\_step} forms at steps 60–70 even though the model never beats the baseline [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}].
\item \textbf{Wide eccentricity.} At $e \le 0.85$ and $e \le 0.7$, formation also falls at steps 60–70 [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}, \texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}].
\item \textbf{Learning rate.} Halving the learning rate delayed formation by only 10, 20 and 30 steps, not the roughly 2× that proportional scaling predicts [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}].
\end{itemize}

Formation therefore tracks a window of training steps more closely than it tracks a fixed loss level.

\textbf{These timings are sensitive to the threshold.} The \texttt{accel\_step} margins at the crossing are often only just above 0.1. In 2 of 3 seeds the margin never reached a 0.15 bar [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}], and at $N = 16$ no seed reached it [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}]. A pre-registered $N = 64$ formation window failed in 3 of 5 seeds with 5-step checkpoints. Persistent formation came at steps 65–90, roughly 10–20 steps earlier than the window, and part of that shift may come from the finer checkpoint resolution [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].

\textbf{An early, transient force spike.} In short runs the force margin rises briefly near step 10 and then collapses:

\begin{itemize}
\item 0.166–0.189 at step 10, decaying to about 0.00–0.02 by steps 40–50. The spike survives best-of-5 controls [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].
\item 0.148–0.159 at step 10, falling to about 0 by step 40 [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}].
\item 0.106–0.127 at step 10 at $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].
\item 0.12–0.19 at steps 10–25 [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Force versus \texttt{accel\_step}}

\textbf{Short runs.} In 300-step runs at $N = 64$, force does not beat its control at the end [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}]. In 2 of 5 seeds it scraped past the bar, with margins of 0.103 and 0.105 [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].

\textbf{Longer runs.} With more training, a force code forms late and keeps strengthening:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{1500 steps (data seed 1).} Force formed at steps 400–600. The earlier "no force code" null was therefore partly an artefact of where training stopped [\texttt{f-20261013-025812-b9ee}].
\item \textbf{$d_{\text{model}} = 128$.} Force formed at steps 400 and 350, against step 100 for \texttt{accel\_step} [\texttt{f-20261013-020028-8792}].
\item \textbf{3000 steps at $N = 64$.} Final force margins were 0.247 and 0.234 [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}]. On fresh seeds 24–26 they were 0.228, 0.255 and 0.216 [\texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}].
\item \textbf{12000 steps.} Final force margins were 0.255 and 0.266 [\texttt{f-20261013-051738-2a37}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Which code leads depends on the metric.} Force often has the larger raw margin, because the \texttt{accel\_step} control is high (often 0.70–0.80 at $e \le 0.5$). On normalised margin, \texttt{accel\_step} leads in every multi-seed comparison below:

| Setting | \texttt{accel\_step} | force |
|---|---|---|
| Wide $m_2$ range [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}] | 0.70, 0.71 | 0.48, 0.42 |
| 6-layer model [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}] | 0.66, 0.61 | 0.41 |
| $N = 64$, seeds 22/23 [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}] | 0.68, 0.70 | 0.45, 0.43 |
| $N = 64$, seeds 24–26 [\texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}] | 0.71, 0.69, 0.68 | ≈0.43–0.48 |
| $N = 64$, data seed 1 [\texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}] | 0.725, 0.740 | 0.44, 0.45 |
| $d_{\text{model}} = 128$, data seed 1 [\texttt{f-20261013-020028-8792}] | 0.86, 0.83 | 0.49, 0.53 |

The 2-layer, $d_{\text{model}} = 32$ model is the exception: on normalised margin, one seed favours each code [\texttt{f-20261013-000220-ab2d}].

\textbf{Confound: force may partly be a read of distance.} At $N \le 128$, force usually peaks at layer 1, the same layer as \texttt{distance} [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}, \texttt{f-20261013-051738-2a37}, \texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}]. In the 1500-step runs, layer-4 force never exceeded about 0.085 [\texttt{f-20261013-025812-b9ee}]. No probe with \texttt{distance} regressed out has been run yet. The force margin should therefore be read as force \emph{decodability}, not as evidence of a separate $m_2$-weighted representation.

\textbf{Fixing the mass ratio did not lift force.} With $m_2 = 0.5$, so that $|F| = 0.5/r^2$ exactly, force $R^2$ stayed at 0.750 and 0.732, while \texttt{distance} decoded at 0.87–0.89 [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}]. The budget stop cut both runs short. Seed 14 completed only 0.55 of its planned steps, below the 80\% replication bar, so the result rests mainly on seed 15 (0.917 completion).

\subsection{Sampling density: force decodability falls with $N$, \texttt{accel\_step} does not}

Final force margins at 3000 steps:

| | $N = 16$ | $N = 64$ | $N = 128$ | $N = 256$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Data seed 0, seeds 24/25/26 | — | 0.228 / 0.255 / 0.216 [\texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}] | 0.128 / 0.143 / 0.079 [\texttt{f-20261013-075824-35f0}] | 0.026 / 0.045 / 0.029 [\texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}] |
| Data seed 0, seeds 22/23 | — | 0.247 / 0.234 [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}] | 0.108 / 0.094 [\texttt{f-20261013-051807-f3f1}] | 0.040 / 0.033 [\texttt{f-20261013-063603-95b5}] |
| Data seed 1, seeds 20/21 | — | 0.209 / 0.209 [\texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}] | 0.137 / 0.145 [\texttt{f-20261013-054542-23e1}] | 0.072 / 0.039 [\texttt{f-20261013-063631-c4ed}] |
| Data seed 0, seeds 20/21 | 0.236 / 0.275 [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}] | — | — | 0.113 / 0.003 (rerun) [\texttt{f-20261013-050513-2c7a}] |

\textbf{$N = 16$ and $N = 64$ give the same force level} [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}, \texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}].

\textbf{From $N = 64$ to $N = 128$, the drop comes from the trained model.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Seeds 24–26.} Trained force $R^2$ fell by 0.098, 0.106 and 0.128, while the controls moved by only +0.002 to +0.009. \texttt{distance} $R^2$ did not fall [\texttt{f-20261013-075824-35f0}].
\item \textbf{Seeds 22/23.} The same pattern holds [\texttt{f-20261013-051807-f3f1}].
\item \textbf{Data seed 1.} The same pattern holds, but the drop is smaller (0.064–0.072) [\texttt{f-20261013-054542-23e1}].
\item \textbf{12000 steps.} The $N = 64$ margin still sat 0.062 and 0.069 above $N = 128$ [\texttt{f-20261013-051738-2a37}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{From $N = 128$ to $N = 256$, the cause of the drop depends on the dataset.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Data seed 1.} Trained force $R^2$ did not fall (about 0.66 at $N = 128$, 0.671–0.672 at $N = 256$). The controls rose from 0.52 to 0.60–0.63, so the whole drop is a control effect [\texttt{f-20261013-063631-c4ed}].
\item \textbf{Data seed 0, seeds 24–26.} Trained force $R^2$ fell by 0.054–0.076, 0.054–0.062 and 0.023–0.028. The controls account for roughly 35–50\% of the margin loss, which refuted the hypothesis that the drop comes from the controls alone [\texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}].
\item \textbf{Data seed 0, seeds 22/23.} The split is intermediate: trained $R^2$ fell by 0.044 and 0.021 [\texttt{f-20261013-063603-95b5}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The $N = 256$ shortfall is not just slower convergence on data seed 0.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{1000 more training steps.} Training seeds 24 and 25 to 4000 steps left trained force $R^2$ at 0.557 and 0.569. That is still 0.082 and 0.053 below the same seeds' $N = 128$ values, after a gain of only about 0.011 and 0.014 between steps 3000 and 4000. Force still failed its control in both seeds [\texttt{f-20261013-083108-2efd}].
\item \textbf{Matched on \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio}.} We compared each seed's $N = 256$ checkpoint at step 4000 with the first $N = 128$ checkpoint that had an equal or lower ratio. At that matched point, $N = 128$ still decoded force better: 0.641 vs 0.557 (+0.084) for seed 24 and 0.630 vs 0.569 (+0.061) for seed 25. The lead also holds at the checkpoint just before the match. At step 4000, $N = 128$ force beats its control (margins 0.145 and 0.169) [\texttt{f-20261013-093210-2e03}].
\end{itemize}

Two caveats apply to this matched comparison. First, it covers two seeds on data seed 0 only, and on data seed 1 the $N = 128 \to 256$ drop was entirely a control effect. Second, the baseline behind \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} itself changes with $N$ [\texttt{f-20261013-093210-2e03}]. Force was also still gaining about 0.005–0.007 margin per 500 steps at $N = 256$, so a fixed ceiling has not been shown [\texttt{f-20261013-083108-2efd}].

!\href{figure:heatmap/run-20261013-092046-2617/force}{Force probe heatmap, N = 128, seed 24, 4000 steps}

\textbf{The normalised \texttt{accel\_step} margin is flat across $N$:}

\begin{itemize}
\item 0.68–0.69 at $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}];
\item 0.68–0.71 at $N = 64$ [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}, \texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}], and 0.725–0.740 on data seed 1 [\texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}];
\item 0.63–0.72 at $N = 128$ [\texttt{f-20261013-051807-f3f1}, \texttt{f-20261013-075824-35f0}, \texttt{f-20261013-054542-23e1}, \texttt{f-20261013-093210-2e03}];
\item 0.66–0.75 at $N = 256$ [\texttt{f-20261013-063603-95b5}, \texttt{f-20261013-063631-c4ed}, \texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}, \texttt{f-20261013-083108-2efd}].
\end{itemize}

So the per-snapshot acceleration code is equally readable from $N = 16$ to $N = 256$. Linear force decodability, by contrast, falls with $N$ in every paired seed.

\textbf{The original $N = 256$ reference did not reproduce, but reruns are deterministic.} An exact rerun of the original config gave force margins of 0.113 and 0.003, against 0.033–0.040 originally. At step 3000 the rerun matched the 6000- and 12000-step runs, which rules out the learning-rate schedule horizon as the cause [\texttt{f-20261013-050513-2c7a}]. A second rerun matched the first to 3 decimals at every checkpoint [\texttt{f-20261013-054603-d6cc}]. Why the original runs differ is still unexplained.

\subsection{Eccentricity sets the control level}

Widening the training eccentricity range lowers both the control and the trained \texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$:

| $e$ range | control $R^2$ | trained $R^2$ | normalised margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\le 0.5$ | 0.724–0.746 | 0.890–0.902 | — |
| $\le 0.7$ | 0.543–0.559 | 0.80–0.81 | ≈0.55 |
| $\le 0.75$ | 0.463–0.487 | 0.746–0.753 | 0.51–0.54 |
| $\le 0.8$ | 0.372–0.396 | 0.670–0.672 | 0.455–0.475 |
| $\le 0.85$ | 0.248–0.333 | 0.507–0.594 | 0.34–0.40 |

Sources: [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}, \texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}, \texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}, \texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}, \texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}].

The decline steepens above $e \le 0.75$ [\texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}]. Force stays much weaker at every ceiling, with normalised margins of 0.16–0.22 [\texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}, \texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}, \texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}].

In a 6-layer, $d_{\text{model}} = 128$ model at $e \le 0.85$, linear \texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ levelled off at about 0.70–0.74. Over the same training, \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} fell a further 5.5–6.8×, so linear decodability stops tracking prediction quality [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}].

\subsection{Out-of-distribution transfer}

\textbf{Trained on $e \le 0.5$:}

\begin{itemize}
\item Error on high-$e$ systems was 16–18× the in-distribution error. This fails the pre-registered 10× criterion, although the model still beats the baseline on those systems.
\item Transfer to low mass ratios held at about 3.6× [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Trained on $e \in [0, 0.8]$:}

\begin{itemize}
\item The high-$e$ gap closed to about 1.0×.
\item The budget stop cut both runs short at step 2500 (completion 0.867 and 0.9), so their probes describe the step-2500 weights.
\item Force probe $R^2$ on held-out high-$e$ systems stayed at 0.366–0.377, against 0.729–0.802 on low-$e$ systems. These out-of-distribution probes have no random controls [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Symbolic regression has not recovered $r^{-2}$}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Strong probes} ($R^2$ 0.831 and 0.881). The \texttt{accel\_step} direction fits as $a^2/r - c\,a$ with $c \approx 0.55$, not as the true $a^3/r^2$. Force fits as about $0.8/r$ [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}].
\item \textbf{Weak probes at $e \le 0.9$.} The fits were also $r^{-1}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{Open check.} The fitter has not been run on the true \texttt{accel\_step} target, so a fitting artefact cannot be ruled out.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Null and negative results}

Twelve hypotheses were refuted:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A wider $m_2$ range would make force fall behind \texttt{accel\_step} on margin.} Force kept the larger raw margin [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item \textbf{A model trained on $e \le 0.5$ would transfer to high $e$ within 10×.} The gap was 16–18× [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}].
\item \textbf{Fixing $m_2 = 0.5$ would lift force $R^2$ to 0.85.} It reached 0.750 and 0.732, and both runs were cut short by the budget stop [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}].
\item \textbf{Wide-$e$ training would give an $r^{-2}$ symbolic fit.} The fits were $r^{-1}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{\texttt{accel\_step} would clear a 0.15 bar and the early force spike would vanish under best-of-5 controls.} Neither held [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].
\item \textbf{Formation would scale in proportion to the learning rate.} The shift was sub-proportional [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}].
\item \textbf{$N = 64$ formation would fall in a narrow pre-registered window.} Only 2 of 5 seeds did [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\item \textbf{$N = 16$ would raise the normalised \texttt{accel\_step} margin.} It stayed level with $N = 64$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}].
\item \textbf{Force would decline gradually from $N = 16$ to $N = 256$.} This was refuted because $N = 64$ matched $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}]. Later paired arms show a steady decline from $N = 64$ upward [\texttt{f-20261013-075824-35f0}, \texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}].
\item \textbf{An exact $N = 256$ rerun would reproduce the original margins within 0.02.} It did not [\texttt{f-20261013-050513-2c7a}].
\item \textbf{The $N = 128 \to 256$ force drop comes from the controls alone.} On data seed 0, trained $R^2$ also fell [\texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}].
\item \textbf{1000 more training steps at $N = 256$ would close the trained force $R^2$ gap to $N = 128$.} The gap stayed at 0.053–0.082 [\texttt{f-20261013-083108-2efd}].
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Not yet tested, and limits}

\textbf{Not yet reported:}

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{velocity} probe results;
\item activation patching, or any other causal test of whether the decoded codes are used;
\item a force probe with \texttt{distance} and \texttt{accel\_step} regressed out;
\item a random-control pool shared across $N$;
\item symbolic regression on the true \texttt{accel\_step} target;
\item a matched-loss $N = 128$ vs $N = 256$ comparison on data seed 1;
\item an explanation of why the original $N = 256$ runs differ.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Limits throughout:}

\begin{itemize}
\item All probes are linear.
\item \texttt{formed\_at\_step} sometimes lags the trajectory by one checkpoint [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}, \texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}].
\item Several paired comparisons use 3-decimal trajectory values. The trajectory's $R^2$ field may be a maximum over layers rather than the $R^2$ at the best-margin layer [\texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}, \texttt{f-20261013-083108-2efd}].
\item Most results come from one 4-layer, $d_{\text{model}} = 64$ architecture.
\item Five findings use data seed 1 [\texttt{f-20261013-020028-8792}, \texttt{f-20261013-025812-b9ee}, \texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}, \texttt{f-20261013-054542-23e1}, \texttt{f-20261013-063631-c4ed}]. All others use data seed 0.
\end{itemize}

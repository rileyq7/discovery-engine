\section{Abstract}

We ask whether small transformers trained on simulated orbits ($G = M = 1$) encode the physical law or shortcuts that happen to work, and when in training any such structure forms. We fit linear probes on the residual stream for force $m_2/r^2$, the per-snapshot acceleration \texttt{accel\_step} $= (2\pi/N)^2 a^3/((1+m_2)r^2)$, distance and energy. A probe result counts only if it beats random-initialisation controls. Across 15 replicated findings (10 supported, 5 refuted), \texttt{accel\_step} beats its control in every evidence run. In runs with dense checkpoints it forms at steps 60–140 [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-030203-1224}, \texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}]. Its timing relative to beating the constant-velocity baseline depends on $N$. At $N = 64$ it forms before the model beats the baseline, and at $N = 16$ it forms after [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}]. At $N = 256$ it forms even though the model never beats the baseline (\texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} 2.03–2.37) [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}]. Physical force usually fails its control in short runs, with final margins of 0.000–0.08 at $N = 64$ [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-030203-1224}]. In a 6-layer model, linear \texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ levels off at 0.70–0.74 while \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} keeps falling 5.5–6.8× [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}]. The main negative result is that symbolic fits to the probe directions give $r^{-1}$, not $r^{-2}$, in every seed [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}, \texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}]. The fitting pipeline has not yet been checked against the true target.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data generation}

All orbits are simulated with REBOUND in units where $G = 1$ and the primary mass is $M = 1$. The gravitational force on body 2 is therefore $|F| = m_2/r^2$, where $m_2$ is the mass ratio. Varying $M$ would add nothing. Positions are sampled at fixed phase intervals, so absolute mass and time scale cannot be identified from them.

Each orbit is sampled at a fixed number of snapshots per orbit, $N$ (\texttt{steps\_per\_orbit}). The time between snapshots is therefore $\Delta t = P/N$, which grows with orbit size ($\Delta t^2 \propto a^3$). The model never sees physical time.

Three-body systems are hierarchical: the third body sits at 3.5–6× the inner semi-major axis. They are used only as an evaluation set for models trained on two-body systems.

The configuration schema adds these fields to the specification:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{data.eccentricity\_range}
\item \texttt{data.data\_seed}
\item \texttt{probes.control\_margin}
\item \texttt{probes.patching}
\item \texttt{probes.symbolic}
\item \texttt{eval\_sets}, for out-of-distribution evaluation
\end{itemize}

Unknown fields are rejected. The training ranges differ between the cited experiments:

\begin{itemize}
\item Some runs train on the default range, which reaches $e \leq 0.5$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item Some runs widen the mass ratio to $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item Some runs widen eccentricity to $e \in [0, 0.8]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\item Some runs widen both, to $e \in [0, 0.9]$ and $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. Both of these runs share data seed 0.
\end{itemize}

The held-out evaluation sets are:

\begin{itemize}
\item low mass ratio, $m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$
\item high eccentricity, $e \in [0.5, 0.8]$
\item three-body systems [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]
\item held-out low-$e$ and high-$e$ splits, used for the wide-eccentricity runs [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tokens, model and prediction target}

Each token is one time step. It holds the $xy$ positions of up to three bodies (6 numbers) in the centre-of-mass frame, with absent bodies set to zero. Because every system uses the same token format, a model trained on two bodies can be evaluated on three-body sets.

The model predicts the normalised displacement to the next step. All evidence runs use one transformer architecture: $L = 4$ layers, width $d = 64$, and $N = 64$ snapshots per orbit [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Prediction quality is reported as \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio}. This is the model's MSE divided by the MSE of constant-velocity extrapolation, $\hat{x}_{t+1} = 2x_t - x_{t-1}$. Both are computed in physical position units over steps $t \geq 1$ on the same held-out systems. A value below 1 means the model beats the baseline.

\subsection{Training}

The lab's default first-experiment (E1) configuration trains for 5k steps at batch size 128 and learning rate $10^{-3}$. Training runs on a laptop GPU (MPS) and is kept CPU-friendly.

Checkpoint spacing varies between experiments. One formation experiment probes every 250 steps [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}]. A dense-checkpoint experiment probes every 25 steps early in training [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].

Every finding is replicated on two training seeds.

\textbf{Budget hard stop.} A runaway guard stops a run once it reaches 2× its seed's share of \texttt{budget.max\_compute\_hours}. The clock starts at the first training step, so dataset generation does not count. A stopped run is checkpointed and probed at its stop step. It records \texttt{steps\_completed} and \texttt{steps\_planned}, and its \texttt{completion} is the fraction of planned steps it trained. Runs below 80\% completion do not count toward replication.

Two of the 12 evidence runs were cut short by an earlier version of this guard. That version stopped at 1.2× the budget share and counted dataset generation against it. Both runs are in [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]: seed 9 stopped at completion 0.867 (step 2640 of 3000) and seed 8 at 0.9 (step 2714). Under that version the probes were taken at the last regular checkpoint, step 2500, not at the stop step. Their \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still falling when training stopped.

\subsection{Probe targets}

Linear probes read five quantities from the residual stream at every layer:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{distance}
\item relative \texttt{velocity}
\item \texttt{energy}, which is constant per system
\item physical \texttt{force}, $|F| = m_2/r^2$
\item the per-snapshot acceleration \texttt{accel\_step}:
\end{itemize}

\[|a_{\text{step}}| = \frac{F}{m_2}\,\Delta t^2 = \left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\right)^2 \frac{a^3}{(1+m_2)\,r^2}.\]

Next-step prediction needs \texttt{accel\_step}, not the physical force. A weak \texttt{force} probe on its own could therefore mean the model learned a per-snapshot version of gravity, not that it learned no gravity. For this reason \texttt{force} and \texttt{accel\_step} are always compared.

A unit test checks two properties of \texttt{accel\_step}:

\begin{itemize}
\item It equals the second difference of positions, to within 1\%.
\item It is not proportional to the physical force across orbit sizes.
\end{itemize}

\texttt{velocity} probe results do not appear in any cited finding.

\subsection{Probe protocol and random-model controls}

Probes run on 300 held-out systems, at every other time step from $t = 8$. Systems are split 70/30 into probe-train and probe-test sets, so rows from the same orbit never appear on both sides.

Each probe is a ridge regression (RidgeCV over $\alpha \in [10^{-3}, 10^{4}]$) on standardised inputs and outputs. $R^2$ is averaged across output dimensions.

Every probe has a control: the same probe fitted on a randomly initialised model of the same architecture. There is one control per layer, because a random model does not change across checkpoints. A probe result counts only if

\[R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}} \geq 0.1 \quad (\texttt{probes.control\_margin}).\]

For each target, the lab reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the best layer
\item its $R^2$ and control $R^2$
\item \texttt{formed\_at\_step}, the first checkpoint at which the margin reaches the threshold (null if it never does)
\end{itemize}

The control $R^2$ for \texttt{accel\_step} is high under the default ranges (0.71–0.75). This caps the margin \texttt{accel\_step} can reach. One finding therefore also reports a post hoc, headroom-normalised score, $(R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}})/(1 - R^2_{\text{control}})$, but its pre-registered criterion uses the raw margin [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].

Probes on the out-of-distribution evaluation sets have no random controls. Their $R^2$ values therefore cannot be read as margins [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}, \texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Only the final checkpoint's activations are saved to disk, as float16. Activations for earlier checkpoints are recomputed from the saved weights when needed.

\subsection{Symbolic regression}

When \texttt{probes.symbolic} is enabled, PySR fits a closed-form expression to the best-layer probe direction for \texttt{force} and for \texttt{accel\_step}. The \texttt{accel\_step} fit uses the inputs $r$, $m_2$ and $a$. Each fit reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the equation
\item whether the true law was recovered (\texttt{law\_recovered})
\item \texttt{fit\_error\_vs\_true\_law}
\end{itemize}

Symbolic fits were run at the final checkpoint only, and only in the runs behind [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. These are the only evidence runs with fitted equations.

\subsection{Activation patching}

The pipeline also supports activation patching. Heads are ranked by a force probe on each head's contribution to the residual stream. The top three heads, plus the lowest-ranked head as a control, are each patched with that head's output on a different system, and the relative increase in next-step MSE is recorded. None of the cited findings report patching results.

\section{Results}

The lab has 15 citable findings, all replicated across seeds. Ten support their hypothesis and five refute it. They rest on 36 evidence runs. The budget hard stop cut four of those runs short, and this section notes it wherever their numbers appear. Every model is a transformer trained on two-body REBOUND orbits with $G = M = 1$. The force on body 2 is therefore $|F| = m_2/r^2$, and the per-snapshot acceleration the model needs for next-step prediction is $(2\pi/N)^2\,a^3/((1+m_2)\,r^2)$. All probes are linear (ridge regression). The first eight findings were recorded with a single random control per probe, so their evidence has no \texttt{control\_spread}. From [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}] onward, each probe is judged against the best of several random controls.

\subsection{Prediction quality}

Fully trained models beat constant-velocity extrapolation by a wide margin:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Default 4-layer, $d = 64$ runs:} final \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} of about 0.011 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}] and 0.0115–0.0157 in later configurations [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}, \texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}].
\item \textbf{6-layer, $d_{\text{model}} = 128$ model at $e \in [0, 0.85]$:} 0.0037 and 0.0032 [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}].
\item \textbf{Short 300-step dense-checkpoint runs at $N = 64$:} these end well above the long-run values but still below 1, at 0.43–0.53 [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}, \texttt{f-20261012-030203-1224}].
\item \textbf{$N = 16$:} the same 300 steps reach about 0.017 [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].
\item \textbf{$N = 256$ at $e \in [0, 0.85]$:} after 600 steps the model never beats the baseline, with final \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} 2.03–2.37 [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}]. The paired $N = 64$ arm reaches 0.13 and is still falling [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}].
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{At $N = 64$, \texttt{accel\_step} forms before the model beats the baseline.} This holds in every seed of every experiment. In the dense runs the lead is 40–90 steps [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}, \texttt{f-20261012-030203-1224}]. With 25-step checkpoints, the margin was 0.115 and 0.108 at step 100, while \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still 1.96 and 1.84 [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item \textbf{At $N = 16$ the order flips, but formation itself barely moves.} \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} drops below 1 at step 20, well before \texttt{accel\_step} forms at steps 75–90. Those formation steps are within 5–15 steps of the seed-matched $N = 64$ runs [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}]. The flip comes from the weaker constant-velocity baseline at $N = 16$. "Beats the baseline" is therefore a baseline-dependent marker and should not be used to time formation across $N$.
\item \textbf{At $N = 256$, formation happens even though the model never beats the baseline.} Formation steps were 60, 70 and 60, and the half-final-margin step was 70 in every seed. That is within about 10 steps of the paired $N = 64$ arm, which formed at steps 60–70 with half-final-margin steps of 70, 80 and 90 [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}, \texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}].
\item \textbf{Timing does not follow relative loss reduction.} At high $e$, formation happens at about 8–10\% of the step-0 validation MSE for $N = 64$ and about 15–18\% for $N = 256$ (\texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} 59–68), compared with about 2–3\% at $e \le 0.5$ [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}, \texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}]. Formation therefore tracks a window of training steps rather than a fixed fraction of the loss.
\item \textbf{\texttt{distance} and \texttt{energy} form first.} They are readable at steps 5–40 in the dense runs, when the model is still far worse than the baseline [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}, \texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}, \texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}]. With 250-step checkpoints, all four probes already beat their controls at the first checkpoint [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Refuted: the \texttt{accel\_step} margin clears a stricter 0.15 bar before the model beats the baseline, and the early force signal is a control artefact} [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{0.15 bar:} in two of three seeds the margin levelled off at 0.134–0.135 and never reached 0.15. Seed 48 reached 0.150 at step 150, only 5 steps before \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} fell below 1.
\item \textbf{Early force spike:} a force margin of 0.166–0.189 at step 10 survives best-of-5 controls. It decays to about 0.00–0.02 by steps 40–50.
\item \textbf{The spike replicates} at 0.152–0.158 on new seeds [\texttt{f-20261012-030203-1224}] and at 0.106–0.127 at $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Caveats on timing.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Thin margins at the crossing.} At the crossing the margin is often only just above 0.1, and seed 58 crosses at exactly 0.100. A bar 0.02 higher would move formation by about 20 steps [\texttt{f-20261012-030203-1224}]. At $N = 16$, no seed reaches a raw 0.15 bar [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].
\item \textbf{Threshold-free measures agree at $N = 64$.} Half of the final margin is reached at steps 50–65 [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].
\item \textbf{\texttt{distance} has not been ruled out.} Nobody has yet tested whether regressing \texttt{distance} out of \texttt{accel\_step} removes its margin [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}].
\item \textbf{This is correlation in time, not use.} Early formation does not show that the model uses this structure to predict [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Refuted: widening the mass range makes force fall behind \texttt{accel\_step}} [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Result:} with $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$, force kept the larger margin in both seeds.
\item \textbf{Measured as a fraction of headroom, \texttt{accel\_step} leads:} 0.70 vs 0.48 in seed 2 and 0.71 vs 0.42 in seed 3. The raw-margin criterion favours force, because \texttt{accel\_step}'s control $R^2$ of 0.71–0.75 caps the margin it can reach.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Refuted: fixing $m_2$ lifts the force probe} [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Prediction and result:} with $m_2 = 0.5$, force $R^2$ was predicted to reach 0.85. It reached only 0.750 and 0.732, while \texttt{distance} was decoded at 0.87–0.89. The force ceiling therefore comes from the $1/r^2$ nonlinearity or how it is encoded, not from missing $m_2$ information.
\item \textbf{Both runs were cut short by the budget hard stop.}
\item Seed 14 stopped at step 1500 (completion 0.55, \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} 0.063). It is below the 80\% bar and does not count as an agreeing seed.
\item Seed 15 stopped at step 2500 (completion 0.917). It was the better-trained model and had the slightly lower force $R^2$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Where each quantity is read out.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\texttt{accel\_step}} is best at the last layer in every evidence run: layer 4 in the 4-layer models and layer 6 in the 6-layer model [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}, \texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}].
\item \textbf{\texttt{distance}} is best at layer 1 or 2 [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}, \texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].
\item \textbf{\texttt{energy}} is usually best at layer 1 or 2, with one exception at layer 3 ($N = 256$, seed 60) [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}].
\item \textbf{Force} has no stable best layer. It peaks anywhere from layer 0 to layer 4 in the 4-layer models and drifts across layers 1–6 in the 6-layer model [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}, \texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}, \texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Linear decodability stops tracking prediction quality}

\textbf{Supported: in a larger model, the linear \texttt{accel\_step} code levels off while prediction keeps improving} [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Plateau:} in the 6-layer, $d_{\text{model}} = 128$ model, layer-6 \texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ rose only from 0.710 to 0.735 (seed 56) and from 0.683 to 0.705 (seed 57) after step 2000. It never reached 0.8.
\item \textbf{Prediction kept improving:} over the same steps \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} fell 6.8× and 5.5×.
\item \textbf{Limits:} $R^2$ still creeps up by about 0.011–0.012 per 1000 steps, and checkpoints are 500 steps apart. A nonlinear \texttt{accel\_step} code has not been tested.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Out-of-distribution transfer}

\textbf{Refuted: a model trained on restricted eccentricity transfers to high eccentricity within 10× of its in-distribution error} [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Low mass ratio ($m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$): passed.} \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was 0.057 and 0.055, about 3.6× in-distribution.
\item \textbf{High eccentricity ($e \in [0.5, 0.8]$): failed.} \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was 0.270 and 0.249, about 16–18× in-distribution, and force $R^2$ fell to 0.40 and 0.34.
\item \textbf{Every two-body ratio stayed below 1.} Three-body ratios of 72 and 70 are exploratory only.
\item \textbf{The out-of-distribution probes have no random controls.}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Supported: training on $e \in [0, 0.8]$ removes the prediction gap} [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Gap closed:} held-out high-$e$ \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was 0.0187 and 0.0189, about 1.05× and 1.01× in-distribution.
\item \textbf{Budget stop:} both runs were cut short at step 2500, with completion 0.867 (seed 9) and 0.9 (seed 8). Both are above the 80\% bar.
\item \textbf{Probe gap remained:} the force probe reached only 0.366–0.377 on held-out high-$e$ systems, against 0.729–0.802 on held-out low-$e$ systems.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Symbolic recovery of the law}

\textbf{Refuted: wide eccentricity coverage yields an $r^{-2}$ law from the probe directions} [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\texttt{accel\_step} fits at $e \in [0, 0.9]$:} $0.0103\,a^2/r$ (fit error 0.71) and $0.00277\,\big((a+0.685)^3/r - r\big)$ (fit error 0.62).
\item \textbf{Force fits:} $m_2/r + 0.40$ and $(m_2 + 0.60)/r$.
\item \textbf{Weak probes:} \texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ was only 0.395 and 0.503.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Supported: the linearly decodable gravity direction has an $r^{-1}$ orbit-scale form} [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Better probes, same exponent:} with \texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ of 0.831 and 0.881, the fits were $a^2/r - 0.55a + 0.076r$ (scaled by 0.0134, fit error 0.245) and $0.0136\,(a^2/r - 0.56a) + 0.0015$ (fit error 0.201).
\item \textbf{Force fits} were $0.81/r$ and $0.80/r$.
\item \textbf{Not yet checked:} the symbolic-regression pipeline has not been run on the true \texttt{accel\_step} target. If it also returns $r^{-1}$ there, the result describes the fitter, not the model.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Summary of null and negative results}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Five hypotheses were refuted:}
\item Wider $m_2$ did not make force fall behind \texttt{accel\_step} [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item Restricted-$e$ training did not transfer to high $e$ within 10× [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]. Later runs traced this to training coverage [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\item Fixing $m_2$ did not lift force $R^2$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}].
\item Wide-$e$ training did not yield an $r^{-2}$ fit [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item At $N = 64$, the \texttt{accel\_step} margin did not clear a 0.15 bar before the model beat the baseline, and the early force spike survived best-of-5 controls [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].
\item \textbf{No $1/r^2$ law has been extracted.} Every symbolic fit so far gives $r^{-1}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}, \texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}].
\item \textbf{A physical-force code is usually absent in short runs.} That covers every $N = 64$ dense run at $e \le 0.5$, every $N = 256$ run, two of three $N = 16$ runs and two of three high-$e$ $N = 64$ runs [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}, \texttt{f-20261012-030203-1224}, \texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}, \texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}, \texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}].
\item \textbf{Probe structure and prediction quality come apart.} This shows at high eccentricity [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}, \texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}], at $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}], at $N = 256$, where \texttt{accel\_step} forms but the model never beats the baseline [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}], and in the 6-layer model [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}].
\item \textbf{Not yet reported:} \texttt{velocity} probe results and activation-patching results.
\item \textbf{Limits throughout:}
\item All probes are linear.
\item Formation steps depend on the 0.1 threshold, which sits close to the \texttt{accel\_step} ceiling at $e \le 0.5$, where control $R^2$ is 0.71–0.79.
\item The budget hard stop cut four evidence runs short. Seed 14 (completion 0.55) is below the replication bar [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}, \texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}].
\end{itemize}

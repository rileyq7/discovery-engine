\section{Abstract}

When a small transformer predicts orbits well, does its residual stream hold the physical law $|F| = m_2/r^2$ or a step-based shortcut, and when does that structure form? We train transformers on simulated two-body orbits sampled at $N$ snapshots per orbit. We then fit linear probes for force, the per-snapshot acceleration $(F/m_2)\,dt^2$ (\texttt{accel\_step}), distance and energy, and judge each probe against best-of-several random-model controls. The lab has 18 replicated findings (11 supported, 7 refuted), based on 47 evidence runs; the budget hard stop cut 4 of these runs short. At $N = 64$, \texttt{accel\_step} reached R² 0.890–0.902 against controls of 0.724–0.746, while force never beat its control [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}]. The \texttt{accel\_step} code forms in a narrow window of training steps, 60–70 at $e \le 0.85$ for both $N = 64$ and $N = 256$, even though the $N = 256$ model never beats constant-velocity extrapolation [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}, \texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}]. Halving the learning rate delayed formation by only 10–30 steps [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}]. The main negative result is that symbolic fits to the \texttt{accel\_step} direction give $r^{-1}$, not the true $r^{-2}$, even at probe R² of 0.831 and 0.881 [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}]. We cannot yet rule out that this comes from the fitting method rather than the model.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data generation}

All orbits are simulated with REBOUND in units where $G = 1$ and the primary mass is $M = 1$. The gravitational force on body 2 is therefore $|F| = m_2/r^2$, where $m_2$ is the mass ratio. Varying $M$ would add nothing. Positions are sampled at fixed phase intervals, so absolute mass and time scale cannot be identified from them.

Each orbit is sampled at a fixed number of snapshots per orbit, $N$ (\texttt{steps\_per\_orbit}). The time between snapshots is therefore $\Delta t = P/N$, which grows with orbit size ($\Delta t^2 \propto a^3$). The model never sees physical time.

Three-body systems are hierarchical: the third body sits at 3.5–6× the inner semi-major axis. They are used only as an evaluation set for models trained on two-body systems.

The configuration schema adds these fields to the specification:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{data.eccentricity\_range}
\item \texttt{data.data\_seed}
\item \texttt{probes.control\_margin}
\item \texttt{probes.patching}
\item \texttt{probes.symbolic}
\item \texttt{eval\_sets}, for out-of-distribution evaluation
\end{itemize}

Unknown fields are rejected. The training ranges differ between the cited experiments:

\begin{itemize}
\item Some runs train on the default range, which reaches $e \leq 0.5$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item Some runs widen the mass ratio to $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item Some runs widen eccentricity to $e \in [0, 0.8]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\item Some runs widen both, to $e \in [0, 0.9]$ and $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. Both of these runs share data seed 0.
\end{itemize}

The held-out evaluation sets are:

\begin{itemize}
\item low mass ratio, $m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$
\item high eccentricity, $e \in [0.5, 0.8]$
\item three-body systems [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]
\item held-out low-$e$ and high-$e$ splits, used for the wide-eccentricity runs [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tokens, model and prediction target}

Each token is one time step. It holds the $xy$ positions of up to three bodies (6 numbers) in the centre-of-mass frame, with absent bodies set to zero. Because every system uses the same token format, a model trained on two bodies can be evaluated on three-body sets.

The model predicts the normalised displacement to the next step. All evidence runs use one transformer architecture: $L = 4$ layers, width $d = 64$, and $N = 64$ snapshots per orbit [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Prediction quality is reported as \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio}. This is the model's MSE divided by the MSE of constant-velocity extrapolation, $\hat{x}_{t+1} = 2x_t - x_{t-1}$. Both are computed in physical position units over steps $t \geq 1$ on the same held-out systems. A value below 1 means the model beats the baseline.

\subsection{Training}

The lab's default first-experiment (E1) configuration trains for 5k steps at batch size 128 and learning rate $10^{-3}$. Training runs on a laptop GPU (MPS) and is kept CPU-friendly.

Checkpoint spacing varies between experiments. One formation experiment probes every 250 steps [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}]. A dense-checkpoint experiment probes every 25 steps early in training [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].

Every finding is replicated on two training seeds.

\textbf{Budget hard stop.} A runaway guard stops a run once it reaches 2× its seed's share of \texttt{budget.max\_compute\_hours}. The clock starts at the first training step, so dataset generation does not count. A stopped run is checkpointed and probed at its stop step. It records \texttt{steps\_completed} and \texttt{steps\_planned}, and its \texttt{completion} is the fraction of planned steps it trained. Runs below 80\% completion do not count toward replication.

Two of the 12 evidence runs were cut short by an earlier version of this guard. That version stopped at 1.2× the budget share and counted dataset generation against it. Both runs are in [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]: seed 9 stopped at completion 0.867 (step 2640 of 3000) and seed 8 at 0.9 (step 2714). Under that version the probes were taken at the last regular checkpoint, step 2500, not at the stop step. Their \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still falling when training stopped.

\subsection{Probe targets}

Linear probes read five quantities from the residual stream at every layer:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{distance}
\item relative \texttt{velocity}
\item \texttt{energy}, which is constant per system
\item physical \texttt{force}, $|F| = m_2/r^2$
\item the per-snapshot acceleration \texttt{accel\_step}:
\end{itemize}

\[|a_{\text{step}}| = \frac{F}{m_2}\,\Delta t^2 = \left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\right)^2 \frac{a^3}{(1+m_2)\,r^2}.\]

Next-step prediction needs \texttt{accel\_step}, not the physical force. A weak \texttt{force} probe on its own could therefore mean the model learned a per-snapshot version of gravity, not that it learned no gravity. For this reason \texttt{force} and \texttt{accel\_step} are always compared.

A unit test checks two properties of \texttt{accel\_step}:

\begin{itemize}
\item It equals the second difference of positions, to within 1\%.
\item It is not proportional to the physical force across orbit sizes.
\end{itemize}

\texttt{velocity} probe results do not appear in any cited finding.

\subsection{Probe protocol and random-model controls}

Probes run on 300 held-out systems, at every other time step from $t = 8$. Systems are split 70/30 into probe-train and probe-test sets, so rows from the same orbit never appear on both sides.

Each probe is a ridge regression (RidgeCV over $\alpha \in [10^{-3}, 10^{4}]$) on standardised inputs and outputs. $R^2$ is averaged across output dimensions.

Every probe has a control: the same probe fitted on a randomly initialised model of the same architecture. There is one control per layer, because a random model does not change across checkpoints. A probe result counts only if

\[R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}} \geq 0.1 \quad (\texttt{probes.control\_margin}).\]

For each target, the lab reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the best layer
\item its $R^2$ and control $R^2$
\item \texttt{formed\_at\_step}, the first checkpoint at which the margin reaches the threshold (null if it never does)
\end{itemize}

The control $R^2$ for \texttt{accel\_step} is high under the default ranges (0.71–0.75). This caps the margin \texttt{accel\_step} can reach. One finding therefore also reports a post hoc, headroom-normalised score, $(R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}})/(1 - R^2_{\text{control}})$, but its pre-registered criterion uses the raw margin [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].

Probes on the out-of-distribution evaluation sets have no random controls. Their $R^2$ values therefore cannot be read as margins [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}, \texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Only the final checkpoint's activations are saved to disk, as float16. Activations for earlier checkpoints are recomputed from the saved weights when needed.

\subsection{Symbolic regression}

When \texttt{probes.symbolic} is enabled, PySR fits a closed-form expression to the best-layer probe direction for \texttt{force} and for \texttt{accel\_step}. The \texttt{accel\_step} fit uses the inputs $r$, $m_2$ and $a$. Each fit reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the equation
\item whether the true law was recovered (\texttt{law\_recovered})
\item \texttt{fit\_error\_vs\_true\_law}
\end{itemize}

Symbolic fits were run at the final checkpoint only, and only in the runs behind [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. These are the only evidence runs with fitted equations.

\subsection{Activation patching}

The pipeline also supports activation patching. Heads are ranked by a force probe on each head's contribution to the residual stream. The top three heads, plus the lowest-ranked head as a control, are each patched with that head's output on a different system, and the relative increase in next-step MSE is recorded. None of the cited findings report patching results.

\section{Results}

The lab has 18 citable findings, all replicated across seeds. Of these, 11 are supported and 7 are refuted. They rest on 47 evidence runs. The budget hard stop cut 4 of those runs short, and we flag them wherever their numbers are used.

\subsection{Per-snapshot acceleration is decodable; physical force mostly is not}

The \texttt{accel\_step} probe beat its random-model control in every evidence run. This target is the per-snapshot acceleration $(F/m_2)\,dt^2 = (2\pi/N)^2 a^3/((1+m_2) r^2)$. The physical-force probe, $|F| = m_2/r^2$, behaved differently: it beat its control reliably only in longer runs. In short runs with dense checkpoints it usually failed. \begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Short runs.} In the short runs, force beat its control in only a minority of seeds.
\item At $N = 64$ with seeds 62–64, force never beat its control, while \texttt{accel\_step} R² reached 0.890–0.902 against controls of 0.724–0.746 [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}].
\item The two seeds that did clear the bar in the newest $N = 64$ replication did so only just: margins of 0.103 and 0.105 against a 0.1 bar, with control spread 0.034–0.048 [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\item \textbf{Early force transient.} A brief force signal appears early in training and then fades:
\item 0.166–0.189 at step 10 under best-of-5 controls [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}];
\item 0.148–0.159 at step 10 in seeds 62–64 [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}];
\item 0.106–0.127 at $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}];
\item 0.12–0.19 at steps 10–25 in seeds 65–69, falling to 0.002–0.10 by steps 40–55 [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\item \textbf{Possible confound.} Force and \texttt{accel\_step} are collinear through $1/r^2$. Part of the force signal in longer runs may therefore be distance information [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Where each quantity is read out:}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\texttt{accel\_step}} is best at the last layer in almost every run: layer 4 in 4-layer models and layer 6 in the 6-layer model [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}, \texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}]. The one exception is seed 67, where it peaked at layer 2 [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\item \textbf{\texttt{distance}} is best at layer 1 or 2 [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}, \texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}].
\item \textbf{Force} has no stable best layer. It ranges from layer 0 to layer 4 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}, \texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}], and drifts across layers 1–6 in the 6-layer model [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{When the \texttt{accel\_step} code forms}

The \texttt{accel\_step} code forms within a fairly narrow window of training steps. The model's loss at that point varies a lot between settings.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Early runs.} The code was present at the first checkpoint (step 250). That is 1250 steps before \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} fell below 0.1 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}]. With 25-step checkpoints, it formed at step 100 in both seeds, while \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still 1.96 and 1.84 [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item \textbf{$N = 64$, $e \le 0.5$.} The persistent 0.1-bar crossing came at these steps:
\item steps 140, 140 and 100 [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}];
\item steps 85–90 [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}];
\item steps 90, 100 and 100, with the half-final-margin step at 80, 60 and 90 [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}].
\end{itemize}

  In each of these, the crossing came 40–90 steps before the model beat constant-velocity extrapolation.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Changing $N$.} Changing $N$ barely moves the formation step:
\item At $N = 16$, formation came at steps 90, 75 and 80. That is within 5–15 steps of the seed-matched $N = 64$ runs. Yet \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} dropped below 1 already at step 20, so the "forms before beating the baseline" ordering reverses [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].
\item At $N = 256$ and $e \in [0, 0.85]$, formation came at steps 60, 70 and 60, although the model never beat the baseline (final \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} 2.03–2.37) [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}].
\item The matched $N = 64$ arm formed at steps 60, 60 and 70 [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}].
\item At $e \in [0, 0.7]$, formation came at steps 60, 60 and 70 [\texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}].
\item \textbf{Implication.} Comparing formation with "beats the baseline" depends on how strong the baseline is at each $N$. It should not be used to time formation across $N$ [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].
\item \textbf{The loss level at formation is not fixed.} Formation came at:
\item 2.0–2.9\% of the step-0 value at $N = 64$, $e \le 0.5$ [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}];
\item 8–10\% at $e \in [0, 0.85]$ [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}];
\item about 15–18\% at $N = 256$ [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}].
\end{itemize}

  Formation timing therefore follows a window of training steps, not a fixed fraction of the loss reduction.

![Formation timeline, N = 256, e ∈ [0, 0.85], seed 59](figure:formation/\texttt{run-20261012-033409-533d})

Three timing hypotheses were refuted:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Stricter bar at $N = 64$} [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}]. In 2 of 3 seeds the \texttt{accel\_step} margin never reached a 0.15 bar. It levelled off at 0.134–0.135. The early force spike also survived best-of-5 controls.
\item \textbf{Halving the learning rate} to 0.0005 [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}]. Formation moved from steps 60/60/70 to 70/80/100. That is +10/+20/+30 steps, not the roughly 2× delay that proportional scaling predicts.
\item \textbf{Pre-registered $N = 64$ window} [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}]. Only 2 of 5 fresh seeds (65–69) fell inside the window, and at least 4 of 5 were required.
\item Seeds 67 and 68 formed for good at step 65. Seeds 65, 66 and 69 formed at step 90. Half-final steps ranged from 40 to 70.
\item These runs used 5-step checkpoints, whereas the earlier ones used 10-step checkpoints. So part of the shift to earlier steps may come from the finer resolution.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Eccentricity sets the control level, not decoding quality}

The \texttt{accel\_step} random-control R² falls as the eccentricity range widens:

\begin{itemize}
\item 0.71–0.77 at $e \le 0.5$;
\item 0.543, 0.553 and 0.559 at $e \le 0.7$;
\item 0.25–0.35 at $e \le 0.85$ [\texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}, \texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}].
\end{itemize}

Raw \texttt{accel\_step} R² falls with it. It is 0.80–0.81 at $e \le 0.7$ [\texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}] and only 0.51–0.59 at $e \le 0.85$ [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}]. The larger margins at high $e$ therefore reflect more headroom, not a stronger representation. These reference points come from separate experiments and were not matched on seeds or mass range [\texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}].

\subsection{Refuted hypotheses about the force probe}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A wider mass range did not make force fall behind \texttt{accel\_step} on raw margin} [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}]. On a headroom-normalised margin, $(R^2 - \text{control})/(1 - \text{control})$, \texttt{accel\_step} led: 0.70 vs 0.48 in seed 2 and 0.71 vs 0.42 in seed 3.
\item \textbf{Fixing $m_2 = 0.5$ did not lift force R² to the predicted 0.85} [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}].
\item Force R² was 0.750 and 0.732. Over the same runs, \texttt{distance} was decoded at 0.87–0.89.
\item Both runs were cut short by the budget hard stop. Seed 14 reached completion 0.55, which is below the 80\% replication bar. Seed 15 reached 0.917.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Linear decodability stops tracking prediction quality}

The 6-layer, $d_{\text{model}} = 128$ model was trained at $e \in [0, 0.85]$ [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}].

\begin{itemize}
\item After step 2000, layer-6 \texttt{accel\_step} R² rose only from 0.710 to 0.735 (seed 56) and from 0.683 to 0.705 (seed 57).
\item Over the same steps, \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} fell 6.8× and 5.5×, to 0.0037 and 0.0032.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Out-of-distribution transfer}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Restricted-$e$ training did not transfer to high $e$ within 10× of in-distribution error} [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}].
\item On high-$e$ systems, \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was 0.270 and 0.249, about 16–18× the in-distribution value.
\item Transfer to low mass ratios held: about 3.6× in-distribution.
\item Three-body ratios of 72 and 70 are exploratory only.
\item \textbf{Training on $e \in [0, 0.8]$ closed the prediction gap} [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\item The high-$e$ to in-distribution ratio was about 1.05× and 1.01×.
\item Both runs were cut short by the budget stop at step 2500, with completion 0.867 and 0.9. \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still falling when they stopped.
\item A probe gap remained: force R² on held-out high-$e$ systems was 0.366–0.377, against 0.729–0.802 on low-$e$ systems. The out-of-distribution probes have no random controls.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Symbolic regression has not recovered $1/r^2$}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{At $e \in [0, 0.9]$} [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]: the fits to the \texttt{accel\_step} direction were $0.0103\,a^2/r$ and $0.00277\,((a+0.685)^3/r - r)$, with fit errors 0.71 and 0.62. These probes were weak, with R² of 0.395 and 0.503.
\item \textbf{With stronger probes} [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}]: at R² 0.831 and 0.881, the fits were still $r^{-1}$, of the form $a^2/r - c\,a$ with $c \approx 0.55$ (fit errors 0.245 and 0.201). The force fits were $0.81/r$ and $0.80/r$.
\item \textbf{Open check.} The fitting pipeline has not yet been run on the true \texttt{accel\_step} target. Until it has, an artefact of the fitter cannot be excluded.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Null and negative results, and open gaps}

\textbf{Seven hypotheses were refuted:}

\begin{itemize}
\item wider $m_2$ separating force from \texttt{accel\_step} [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}];
\item restricted-$e$ transfer to high $e$ [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}];
\item fixed $m_2$ lifting force [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}];
\item wide-$e$ training yielding $r^{-2}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}];
\item the 0.15-bar ordering and a spurious force spike at $N = 64$ [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}];
\item learning-rate-proportional formation [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}];
\item the narrow $N = 64$ formation window [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Not yet reported:}

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{velocity} probe results;
\item activation-patching results;
\item any test of whether the \texttt{accel\_step} code is causally used.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Limits throughout:}

\begin{itemize}
\item All probes are linear.
\item At $e \le 0.5$, the \texttt{accel\_step} margins sit close to the 0.1 bar because the controls are high.
\item Most results share one data seed and one 4-layer architecture.
\end{itemize}

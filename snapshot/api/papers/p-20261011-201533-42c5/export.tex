\section{Abstract}

We ask whether small transformers trained to predict simulated orbits (REBOUND, $G = M = 1$) encode the physical force $|F| = m_2/r^2$ or a shortcut, and when any structure forms. We fit linear probes to the residual stream and compare each against randomly initialised controls. We compare force with \texttt{accel\_step} $\propto a^3/r^2$, the per-snapshot acceleration that next-step prediction requires. The lab has 45 replicated findings (32 supported, 13 refuted) from 107 evidence runs. \texttt{accel\_step} is consistently decodable. At $N = 64$ snapshots per orbit it forms by steps 85–140, before the model beats constant-velocity extrapolation [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}]. Its headroom-normalised margin stays near 0.63–0.79 across $N$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}, \texttt{f-20261013-063603-95b5}]. Force does not hold up: its margin falls from 0.209–0.275 at $N = 64$ to 0.003–0.113 at $N = 256$ [\texttt{f-20261013-050513-2c7a}, \texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}]. From $N = 64$ to $N = 128$ the loss is in trained $R^2$, not in the controls [\texttt{f-20261013-075824-35f0}, \texttt{f-20261013-224908-8b46}], even with $m_2$ fixed [\texttt{f-20261013-234552-f8e8}]. The main negative result is that symbolic regression on the probe directions returns $r^{-1}$ forms such as $a^2/r - c\,a$ ($c \approx 0.55$), never the true $r^{-2}$ law [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}, \texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. The fitter itself has not yet been checked on the true target.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data generation}

All orbits are simulated with REBOUND in units where $G = 1$ and the primary mass is $M = 1$. The gravitational force on body 2 is therefore $|F| = m_2/r^2$, where $m_2$ is the mass ratio. Varying $M$ would add nothing. Positions are sampled at fixed phase intervals, so absolute mass and time scale cannot be identified from them.

Each orbit is sampled at a fixed number of snapshots per orbit, $N$ (\texttt{steps\_per\_orbit}). The time between snapshots is therefore $\Delta t = P/N$, which grows with orbit size ($\Delta t^2 \propto a^3$). The model never sees physical time.

Three-body systems are hierarchical: the third body sits at 3.5–6× the inner semi-major axis. They are used only as an evaluation set for models trained on two-body systems.

The configuration schema adds these fields to the specification:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{data.eccentricity\_range}
\item \texttt{data.data\_seed}
\item \texttt{probes.control\_margin}
\item \texttt{probes.patching}
\item \texttt{probes.symbolic}
\item \texttt{eval\_sets}, for out-of-distribution evaluation
\end{itemize}

Unknown fields are rejected. The training ranges differ between the cited experiments:

\begin{itemize}
\item Some runs train on the default range, which reaches $e \leq 0.5$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item Some runs widen the mass ratio to $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item Some runs widen eccentricity to $e \in [0, 0.8]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\item Some runs widen both, to $e \in [0, 0.9]$ and $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. Both of these runs share data seed 0.
\end{itemize}

The held-out evaluation sets are:

\begin{itemize}
\item low mass ratio, $m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$
\item high eccentricity, $e \in [0.5, 0.8]$
\item three-body systems [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]
\item held-out low-$e$ and high-$e$ splits, used for the wide-eccentricity runs [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tokens, model and prediction target}

Each token is one time step. It holds the $xy$ positions of up to three bodies (6 numbers) in the centre-of-mass frame, with absent bodies set to zero. Because every system uses the same token format, a model trained on two bodies can be evaluated on three-body sets.

The model predicts the normalised displacement to the next step. All evidence runs use one transformer architecture: $L = 4$ layers, width $d = 64$, and $N = 64$ snapshots per orbit [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Prediction quality is reported as \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio}. This is the model's MSE divided by the MSE of constant-velocity extrapolation, $\hat{x}_{t+1} = 2x_t - x_{t-1}$. Both are computed in physical position units over steps $t \geq 1$ on the same held-out systems. A value below 1 means the model beats the baseline.

\subsection{Training}

The lab's default first-experiment (E1) configuration trains for 5k steps at batch size 128 and learning rate $10^{-3}$. Training runs on a laptop GPU (MPS) and is kept CPU-friendly.

Checkpoint spacing varies between experiments. One formation experiment probes every 250 steps [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}]. A dense-checkpoint experiment probes every 25 steps early in training [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].

Every finding is replicated on two training seeds.

\textbf{Budget hard stop.} A runaway guard stops a run once it reaches 2× its seed's share of \texttt{budget.max\_compute\_hours}. The clock starts at the first training step, so dataset generation does not count. A stopped run is checkpointed and probed at its stop step. It records \texttt{steps\_completed} and \texttt{steps\_planned}, and its \texttt{completion} is the fraction of planned steps it trained. Runs below 80\% completion do not count toward replication.

Two of the 12 evidence runs were cut short by an earlier version of this guard. That version stopped at 1.2× the budget share and counted dataset generation against it. Both runs are in [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]: seed 9 stopped at completion 0.867 (step 2640 of 3000) and seed 8 at 0.9 (step 2714). Under that version the probes were taken at the last regular checkpoint, step 2500, not at the stop step. Their \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still falling when training stopped.

\subsection{Probe targets}

Linear probes read five quantities from the residual stream at every layer:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{distance}
\item relative \texttt{velocity}
\item \texttt{energy}, which is constant per system
\item physical \texttt{force}, $|F| = m_2/r^2$
\item the per-snapshot acceleration \texttt{accel\_step}:
\end{itemize}

\[|a_{\text{step}}| = \frac{F}{m_2}\,\Delta t^2 = \left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\right)^2 \frac{a^3}{(1+m_2)\,r^2}.\]

Next-step prediction needs \texttt{accel\_step}, not the physical force. A weak \texttt{force} probe on its own could therefore mean the model learned a per-snapshot version of gravity, not that it learned no gravity. For this reason \texttt{force} and \texttt{accel\_step} are always compared.

A unit test checks two properties of \texttt{accel\_step}:

\begin{itemize}
\item It equals the second difference of positions, to within 1\%.
\item It is not proportional to the physical force across orbit sizes.
\end{itemize}

\texttt{velocity} probe results do not appear in any cited finding.

\subsection{Probe protocol and random-model controls}

Probes run on 300 held-out systems, at every other time step from $t = 8$. Systems are split 70/30 into probe-train and probe-test sets, so rows from the same orbit never appear on both sides.

Each probe is a ridge regression (RidgeCV over $\alpha \in [10^{-3}, 10^{4}]$) on standardised inputs and outputs. $R^2$ is averaged across output dimensions.

Every probe has a control: the same probe fitted on a randomly initialised model of the same architecture. There is one control per layer, because a random model does not change across checkpoints. A probe result counts only if

\[R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}} \geq 0.1 \quad (\texttt{probes.control\_margin}).\]

For each target, the lab reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the best layer
\item its $R^2$ and control $R^2$
\item \texttt{formed\_at\_step}, the first checkpoint at which the margin reaches the threshold (null if it never does)
\end{itemize}

The control $R^2$ for \texttt{accel\_step} is high under the default ranges (0.71–0.75). This caps the margin \texttt{accel\_step} can reach. One finding therefore also reports a post hoc, headroom-normalised score, $(R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}})/(1 - R^2_{\text{control}})$, but its pre-registered criterion uses the raw margin [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].

Probes on the out-of-distribution evaluation sets have no random controls. Their $R^2$ values therefore cannot be read as margins [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}, \texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Only the final checkpoint's activations are saved to disk, as float16. Activations for earlier checkpoints are recomputed from the saved weights when needed.

\subsection{Symbolic regression}

When \texttt{probes.symbolic} is enabled, PySR fits a closed-form expression to the best-layer probe direction for \texttt{force} and for \texttt{accel\_step}. The \texttt{accel\_step} fit uses the inputs $r$, $m_2$ and $a$. Each fit reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the equation
\item whether the true law was recovered (\texttt{law\_recovered})
\item \texttt{fit\_error\_vs\_true\_law}
\end{itemize}

Symbolic fits were run at the final checkpoint only, and only in the runs behind [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. These are the only evidence runs with fitted equations.

\subsection{Activation patching}

The pipeline also supports activation patching. Heads are ranked by a force probe on each head's contribution to the residual stream. The top three heads, plus the lowest-ranked head as a control, are each patched with that head's output on a different system, and the relative increase in next-step MSE is recorded. None of the cited findings report patching results.

\section{Results}

The lab has 45 citable findings, all with status \texttt{replicated}. Of these, 32 support their hypothesis and 13 refute it. They rest on 107 evidence runs. The budget hard stop cut 4 of those runs short, and we flag each one below where its numbers are used. Unless stated otherwise, models have 4 layers and $d_\text{model} = 64$, use $N = 64$ snapshots per orbit and $e \le 0.5$, and are probed with linear (ridge) probes against best-of-$k$ random-init controls. Throughout, we report two kinds of margin. The \emph{raw margin} is $R^2 - R^2_\text{control}$. The \emph{normalised margin} is $(R^2 - R^2_\text{control}) / (1 - R^2_\text{control})$.

\subsection{Prediction quality}

The transformers predict orbits far better than constant-velocity extrapolation.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Default runs ($N = 64$).} Final \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} is about 0.011 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}], and 0.014–0.016 after 3000 steps [\texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}].
\item \textbf{Coarse sampling.} At $N = 16$ it falls to 0.0007, because the baseline is weaker there [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}].
\item \textbf{Larger model.} With 6 layers and $d_\text{model} = 128$ it reaches 0.0037 / 0.0032 [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}].
\item \textbf{Fine sampling.} $N = 256$ is the exception. After 600 steps at $e \le 0.85$ it never beats the baseline (2.03–2.37) [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}]. At $e \le 0.5$ it drops below 1 only around step 2000 and ends at 0.24–0.39 after 3000 steps [\texttt{f-20261013-063603-95b5}, \texttt{f-20261013-063631-c4ed}, \texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What is decodable: per-snapshot acceleration, not physical force}

Two codes are reliably decodable in every configuration tested:

\begin{itemize}
\item a per-snapshot acceleration code, \texttt{accel\_step} $\propto a^3/((1+m_2)r^2)$, which is best decoded at the last layer;
\item a \texttt{distance} code at layer 1.
\end{itemize}

The physical force $|F| = m_2/r^2$ is weaker, and whether it is decodable depends on the configuration.

\textbf{Short runs at $N = 64$ (300 steps).} Force fails its control at the end in every seed. Final margins are 0.000–0.062 [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}] and 0.033–0.080 [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}]. \texttt{accel\_step} beats its control, at R² 0.884–0.905 against controls of 0.72–0.79.

\textbf{Longer training.} Force eventually forms, but late and mostly at the \texttt{distance} layer:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{1500 steps, data seed 1.} Force first holds its 0.1 margin at steps 400 / 450 / 600, ending at 0.196 / 0.193 / 0.167. It crosses the bar only after its best layer moves from 4 to 1. Layer-4 force never exceeds about 0.085 [\texttt{f-20261013-025812-b9ee}].
\item \textbf{$d_\text{model} = 128$, 5000 steps.} Force forms at steps 400 / 350, against step 100 for \texttt{accel\_step}. From step 500 to 5000, force gains +0.074 / +0.081 while \texttt{accel\_step} gains only +0.025 / +0.022 [\texttt{f-20261013-020028-8792}].
\item \textbf{3000 steps, $N = 64$.} Force margins are 0.215–0.261 on data seed 0 [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}, \texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}] and 0.209 on data seed 1 [\texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}].
\item \textbf{Two layers, $d_\text{model} = 32$.} Force margins are 0.221 / 0.255 [\texttt{f-20261013-000220-ab2d}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Which code leads depends on the metric.}

\begin{itemize}
\item On raw margin, force usually leads after long training.
\item On normalised margin, \texttt{accel\_step} leads in almost every case. For example, it is 0.69–0.71 against force's 0.43–0.48 [\texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}], and 0.86 / 0.83 against 0.49 / 0.53 at $d_\text{model} = 128$ [\texttt{f-20261013-020028-8792}].
\item The one exception is $L = 2$, $d_\text{model} = 32$, seed 2, where force leads on both measures [\texttt{f-20261013-000220-ab2d}].
\end{itemize}

The reason for the split is that \texttt{accel\_step}'s random control is high (about 0.65–0.81 at $e \le 0.5$), which caps its raw margin.

\textbf{Early-run findings (single controls).} In the earliest runs, force also kept a larger raw margin than \texttt{accel\_step} when $m_2$ was widened to $[0.01, 1]$; the hypothesis that it would fall behind was refuted [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].

| 250-step checkpoints | 250 (margins 0.158 / 0.127) | \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} < 0.1 at step 1500 | [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}] |
| 25-step checkpoints | 100 (margins 0.115 / 0.108) | \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} < 1 first reported at step 175 | [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}] |
| 10-step checkpoints, $N = 64$ | 140 / 140 / 100 | step 180–190 | [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}] |
| Best-of-5 controls | 85–90 | step 135–165 | [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}] |
| Seeds 62–64 | 90–100 | step 140–160 | [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}] |

The two earliest rows used a single random control.

\textbf{\texttt{distance} and \texttt{energy} form earlier still}, at steps 5–40, while the model is far worse than the baseline [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}, \texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}, \texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}].

\textbf{Formation timing depends little on sampling density or learning rate.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$N = 16$.} \texttt{accel\_step} forms at steps 75–90, within 5–15 steps of the seed-matched $N = 64$ runs. The model now beats the baseline first, at step 20, because the baseline is weaker at coarse sampling. So "forms before beating the baseline" is a baseline-dependent marker [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].
\item \textbf{$N = 256$ at $e \le 0.85$.} Formation (steps 60–70) matches $N = 64$ (60–70) even though the $N = 256$ models never beat the baseline [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}, \texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}].
\item \textbf{Halving the learning rate.} Formation moves from 60 / 60 / 70 to 70 / 80 / 100. The hypothesis that formation scales in proportion to the learning rate was refuted [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}].
\item \textbf{5-step checkpoints.} Persistent formation spans steps 65–90. A pre-registered $N = 64$ window held in only 2 of 5 seeds (refuted) [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Formation timing is sensitive to the threshold.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{accel\_step} margins at $e \le 0.5$ level off just above the 0.1 bar.
\item Under best-of-5 controls, a 0.15 bar was not reached in 2 of 3 seeds (refuted) [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].
\item At $N = 16$, the 0.15 bar was never reached [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Force shows an early transient that does not last.}

\begin{itemize}
\item A margin of 0.106–0.19 appears around steps 10–25 and collapses to about 0 by steps 20–55 [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}, \texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}, \texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}, \texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\item It survives best-of-5 controls, so it is not a control artefact [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{From $N = 64$ to $N = 128$, force falls in every paired seed, and the loss is in trained $R^2$, not in the controls.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Data seed 0.} For seeds 24–26, trained force $R^2$ fell by 0.098–0.128 while controls moved by +0.002 to +0.009 [\texttt{f-20261013-075824-35f0}].

\item \textbf{Fixed $m_2$.} The drop persists with $m_2 = 0.5$; the hypothesis that fixing $m_2$ removes it was refuted. \item \textbf{Interpretation.} The model loses the ability to read out $1/r^2$; the loss is not in the $m_2$ factor.
\item \textbf{Long training.} After 12000 steps, $N = 64$ is still 0.062 / 0.069 above $N = 128$ [\texttt{f-20261013-051738-2a37}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{From $N = 128$ to $N = 256$, what drives the drop depends on the dataset.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Data seed 1.} The drop is entirely a control effect: trained $R^2$ stays at about 0.66–0.67 while controls rise to 0.60–0.63 [\texttt{f-20261013-063631-c4ed}].

\end{itemize}

\textbf{On data seed 0, the $N = 256$ shortfall is not just slow convergence.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{1000 extra steps.} These added only 0.011–0.014 of trained force $R^2$ (refuted) [\texttt{f-20261013-083108-2efd}].
\item \textbf{Matched on \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio}.} $N = 128$ still decodes force better: 0.641 vs 0.557, and 0.630 vs 0.569 [\texttt{f-20261013-093210-2e03}].
\item \textbf{No ceiling yet.} Force was still gaining 0.005–0.007 of margin per 500 steps, so a fixed ceiling has not been shown [\texttt{f-20261013-083108-2efd}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Fixing the mass ratio}

\textbf{Fixing $m_2 = 0.5$ does not lift force to the hypothesised $R^2 \ge 0.85$ (refuted).} In early runs, force reached 0.750 / 0.732 while \texttt{accel\_step} reached 0.924 / 0.946 [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}]. The budget stop cut both of those runs short:

\begin{itemize}
\item seed 14 at step 1500, completion 0.55, which is below the 80\% needed to count toward replication;
\item seed 15 at step 2500, completion 0.917.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{In complete 3000-step runs at $N = 64$}, trained force $R^2$ is:

\begin{itemize}
\item 0.763–0.789 across four seeds on data seed 0 [\texttt{f-20261013-234439-d851}, \texttt{f-20261014-004505-d52c}];
\item 0.795 / 0.790 on data seed 1 [\texttt{f-20261014-015644-a80b}].
\end{itemize}

Trained $R^2$ is higher than in wide-$m_2$ runs, but the margins are smaller, because fixing $m_2$ raises the force control to about 0.54–0.64.

\subsection{Eccentricity sets the control level}

Widening the training range of $e$ lowers both the \texttt{accel\_step} control and its trained $R^2$. All rows are $N = 64$:

| $e$ range | \texttt{accel\_step} control $R^2$ | trained $R^2$ | normalised margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\le 0.5$ [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}] | 0.724–0.746 | 0.890–0.902 | — |
| $\le 0.7$ [\texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}] | 0.543–0.559 | 0.80–0.81 | ≈0.55 |
| $\le 0.75$ [\texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}] | 0.463–0.487 | 0.746–0.753 | 0.51–0.54 |
| $\le 0.8$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}] | 0.372–0.396 | 0.670–0.672 | 0.455–0.475 |
| $\le 0.85$ [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}] | 0.248–0.333 | 0.507–0.594 | 0.34–0.40 |

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The decline steepens above $e \le 0.75$} [\texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}].
\item \textbf{Force is weaker at every ceiling.} Its normalised margins are 0.11–0.22, and it forms late, at steps 250–590 [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}, \texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}, \texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}, \texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}].
\item \textbf{Larger model at $e \le 0.85$} (6 layers, $d_\text{model} = 128$). Linear \texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ levels off at about 0.70–0.74, gaining only 0.022–0.025 after step 2000. Over the same steps, \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} keeps falling 5.5–6.8× [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}]. So linear decodability of \texttt{accel\_step} stops tracking prediction quality.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Out-of-distribution transfer}

\textbf{Trained on $e \le 0.5$} [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Low mass ratios} ($m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$). Error is about 3.6× the in-distribution error, and force $R^2$ holds at 0.75–0.77.
\item \textbf{High eccentricity} ($e \in [0.5, 0.8]$). Error is 16–18× the in-distribution error, which fails the pre-registered 10× criterion (refuted). The models still beat the baseline, and force $R^2$ falls to 0.40 / 0.34.
\item \textbf{Three-body sets.} Ratios of 72 and 70 are exploratory only.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Trained on $e \in [0, 0.8]$} [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The high-$e$ gap closes.} High-$e$ error falls to 1.01–1.05× the in-distribution error.
\item \textbf{Truncated runs.} The budget stop cut both runs short at step 2500 (completion 0.867 and 0.9), so their probes describe the step-2500 weights.
\item \textbf{The probes still differ.} With prediction error matched, force $R^2$ on held-out high-$e$ systems is 0.366–0.377, against 0.729–0.802 on low-$e$ systems. These out-of-distribution probes have no random controls.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Symbolic regression has not recovered $r^{-2}$}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Strong probes} (\texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ 0.831 / 0.881). The fitted direction has the form $a^2/r - c\,a$ with $c \approx 0.55$, not $a^3/r^2$. Fit errors are 0.245 / 0.201. Force fits as about $0.8/r$ [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}].
\item \textbf{Weaker probes, training at $e \le 0.9$} (\texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ 0.40–0.50). These also fit as $r^{-1}$; the expected $r^{-2}$ did not appear (refuted) [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{Possible fitter artefact.} The fitter has not been run on the true \texttt{accel\_step} target, so we cannot yet rule out that it produces the $r^{-1}$ form on its own.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reproducibility}

\textbf{The original $N = 256$ reference runs did not reproduce.} An exact rerun gave force margins of 0.113 / 0.003. At step 3000 it agreed with 6000- and 12000-step runs to within about 0.025, which rules out the learning-rate schedule horizon as the cause (refuted) [\texttt{f-20261013-050513-2c7a}].

\textbf{The current pipeline is deterministic for a fixed seed.} A second identical rerun matched the first to 3 decimals at every checkpoint [\texttt{f-20261013-054603-d6cc}]. It follows that:

\begin{itemize}
\item the 0.11 force gap between seeds 20 and 21 at $N = 256$ is genuine seed variance;
\item the cause of the original discrepancy is still unexplained.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Null and negative results}

The 13 refuted hypotheses are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A wider $m_2$ range would put force behind \texttt{accel\_step}.} It did not [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item \textbf{A model trained on $e \le 0.5$ would transfer to high $e$ within 10×.} The error was 16–18× [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}].
\item \textbf{Fixing $m_2$ would lift force $R^2$ to 0.85.} It did not [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}].
\item \textbf{Wide-$e$ training would yield an $r^{-2}$ fit.} The fits were $r^{-1}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{\texttt{accel\_step} would clear a 0.15 bar, and the early force spike would vanish under best-of-5 controls.} Neither held [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].
\item \textbf{Formation would scale in proportion to the learning rate.} The shift was sub-proportional [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}].
\item \textbf{$N = 64$ formation would fall in a narrow window.} Only 2 of 5 seeds did [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\item \textbf{$N = 16$ would raise the normalised \texttt{accel\_step} margin.} It was level with $N = 64$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}].
\item \textbf{Force would decline gradually from $N = 16$.} $N = 64$ matched $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}].
\item \textbf{An exact rerun would reproduce the original $N = 256$ margins.} It did not [\texttt{f-20261013-050513-2c7a}].
\item \textbf{The $N = 128 \to 256$ force drop is all control effect.} On data seed 0, trained $R^2$ also fell [\texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}].
\item \textbf{1000 extra steps would close the $N = 256$ gap.} They did not [\texttt{f-20261013-083108-2efd}].
\item \textbf{Fixing $m_2$ would remove the $N = 64 \to 128$ drop.} The drop persisted [\texttt{f-20261013-234552-f8e8}].
\end{enumerate}

Several supported findings are also nulls for force, where force fails its control:

\begin{itemize}
\item short $N = 64$ runs [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}];
\item $N = 256$ in most seeds [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}, \texttt{f-20261013-063603-95b5}, \texttt{f-20261013-063631-c4ed}];
\item $m_2$-fixed runs at $N = 128$ [\texttt{f-20261014-004426-68d6}, \texttt{f-20261014-015607-930a}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Not yet tested, and limits}

The lab has not yet reported:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{velocity} probe results;
\item activation patching, or any other causal test of whether the decoded codes are used;
\item a force probe with \texttt{distance} and \texttt{accel\_step} regressed out;
\item a random-control pool shared across $N$;
\item nonlinear or log-target probes;
\item symbolic regression on the true \texttt{accel\_step} target;
\item matched-loss comparisons for data seed 1 and for the $m_2$-fixed arms.
\end{itemize}

Several limits apply throughout:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Probes.} All probes are linear.
\item \textbf{Single controls in early runs.} The earliest findings used a single random control [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}, \texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}, \texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item \textbf{Formation steps.} \texttt{formed\_at\_step} sometimes lags the trajectory by one checkpoint [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}, \texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}, \texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}].
\item \textbf{Rounded values.} Several paired comparisons rely on 3-decimal trajectory values whose $R^2$ field may be a maximum over layers [\texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}, \texttt{f-20261014-004426-68d6}, \texttt{f-20261014-015607-930a}].
\item \textbf{Training length.} Force was still rising at the end of most 3000-step runs.
\item \textbf{Datasets.} Only two data seeds have been tested.
\item \textbf{Architecture.} Most results come from one architecture (4 layers, $d_\text{model} = 64$).
\end{itemize}

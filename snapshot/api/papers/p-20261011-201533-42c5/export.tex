\section{Abstract}

We ask whether small transformers trained to predict two-body orbits (REBOUND, $G = M = 1$) encode the gravitational law or quantities specific to the task, and when that structure forms. We fit linear probes on each layer for physical force $m_2/r^2$, per-snapshot acceleration \texttt{accel\_step} $\propto a^3/r^2$, \texttt{distance} and \texttt{energy}. A probe counts only if it beats the best of several random-init controls by a required margin. The lab has 49 replicated findings (36 supported, 13 refuted) from 115 evidence runs, 4 of them cut short by the budget stop. \texttt{accel\_step} is decodable in every setting. Its headroom-normalised margin stays at about 0.63–0.80 across $N = 16$–$256$ snapshots per orbit [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}, \texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}, \texttt{f-20261014-051311-3e2e}]. At $N = 64$ it forms at steps 100–140, 40–90 steps before the model beats constant-velocity extrapolation [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}]. Force is weaker and depends on sampling. Its margin is 0.22–0.26 at $N = 64$, falls to 0.08–0.14 at $N = 128$, and fails its control in most $N = 256$ runs [\texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}, \texttt{f-20261013-075824-35f0}, \texttt{f-20261013-063603-95b5}]. The $N = 64 \to 128$ gap survives fixing $m_2$ and 12000 training steps [\texttt{f-20261014-051311-3e2e}]. The main negative result is that symbolic fits to the probe directions give $r^{-1}$, not $r^{-2}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}, \texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. A fitter artefact is not yet excluded.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data generation}

All orbits are simulated with REBOUND in units where $G = 1$ and the primary mass is $M = 1$. The gravitational force on body 2 is therefore $|F| = m_2/r^2$, where $m_2$ is the mass ratio. Varying $M$ would add nothing. Positions are sampled at fixed phase intervals, so absolute mass and time scale cannot be identified from them.

Each orbit is sampled at a fixed number of snapshots per orbit, $N$ (\texttt{steps\_per\_orbit}). The time between snapshots is therefore $\Delta t = P/N$, which grows with orbit size ($\Delta t^2 \propto a^3$). The model never sees physical time.

Three-body systems are hierarchical: the third body sits at 3.5–6× the inner semi-major axis. They are used only as an evaluation set for models trained on two-body systems.

The configuration schema adds these fields to the specification:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{data.eccentricity\_range}
\item \texttt{data.data\_seed}
\item \texttt{probes.control\_margin}
\item \texttt{probes.patching}
\item \texttt{probes.symbolic}
\item \texttt{eval\_sets}, for out-of-distribution evaluation
\end{itemize}

Unknown fields are rejected. The training ranges differ between the cited experiments:

\begin{itemize}
\item Some runs train on the default range, which reaches $e \leq 0.5$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item Some runs widen the mass ratio to $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item Some runs widen eccentricity to $e \in [0, 0.8]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\item Some runs widen both, to $e \in [0, 0.9]$ and $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. Both of these runs share data seed 0.
\end{itemize}

The held-out evaluation sets are:

\begin{itemize}
\item low mass ratio, $m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$
\item high eccentricity, $e \in [0.5, 0.8]$
\item three-body systems [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]
\item held-out low-$e$ and high-$e$ splits, used for the wide-eccentricity runs [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tokens, model and prediction target}

Each token is one time step. It holds the $xy$ positions of up to three bodies (6 numbers) in the centre-of-mass frame, with absent bodies set to zero. Because every system uses the same token format, a model trained on two bodies can be evaluated on three-body sets.

The model predicts the normalised displacement to the next step. All evidence runs use one transformer architecture: $L = 4$ layers, width $d = 64$, and $N = 64$ snapshots per orbit [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Prediction quality is reported as \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio}. This is the model's MSE divided by the MSE of constant-velocity extrapolation, $\hat{x}_{t+1} = 2x_t - x_{t-1}$. Both are computed in physical position units over steps $t \geq 1$ on the same held-out systems. A value below 1 means the model beats the baseline.

\subsection{Training}

The lab's default first-experiment (E1) configuration trains for 5k steps at batch size 128 and learning rate $10^{-3}$. Training runs on a laptop GPU (MPS) and is kept CPU-friendly.

Checkpoint spacing varies between experiments. One formation experiment probes every 250 steps [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}]. A dense-checkpoint experiment probes every 25 steps early in training [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].

Every finding is replicated on two training seeds.

\textbf{Budget hard stop.} A runaway guard stops a run once it reaches 2× its seed's share of \texttt{budget.max\_compute\_hours}. The clock starts at the first training step, so dataset generation does not count. A stopped run is checkpointed and probed at its stop step. It records \texttt{steps\_completed} and \texttt{steps\_planned}, and its \texttt{completion} is the fraction of planned steps it trained. Runs below 80\% completion do not count toward replication.

Two of the 12 evidence runs were cut short by an earlier version of this guard. That version stopped at 1.2× the budget share and counted dataset generation against it. Both runs are in [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]: seed 9 stopped at completion 0.867 (step 2640 of 3000) and seed 8 at 0.9 (step 2714). Under that version the probes were taken at the last regular checkpoint, step 2500, not at the stop step. Their \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still falling when training stopped.

\subsection{Probe targets}

Linear probes read five quantities from the residual stream at every layer:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{distance}
\item relative \texttt{velocity}
\item \texttt{energy}, which is constant per system
\item physical \texttt{force}, $|F| = m_2/r^2$
\item the per-snapshot acceleration \texttt{accel\_step}:
\end{itemize}

\[|a_{\text{step}}| = \frac{F}{m_2}\,\Delta t^2 = \left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\right)^2 \frac{a^3}{(1+m_2)\,r^2}.\]

Next-step prediction needs \texttt{accel\_step}, not the physical force. A weak \texttt{force} probe on its own could therefore mean the model learned a per-snapshot version of gravity, not that it learned no gravity. For this reason \texttt{force} and \texttt{accel\_step} are always compared.

A unit test checks two properties of \texttt{accel\_step}:

\begin{itemize}
\item It equals the second difference of positions, to within 1\%.
\item It is not proportional to the physical force across orbit sizes.
\end{itemize}

\texttt{velocity} probe results do not appear in any cited finding.

\subsection{Probe protocol and random-model controls}

Probes run on 300 held-out systems, at every other time step from $t = 8$. Systems are split 70/30 into probe-train and probe-test sets, so rows from the same orbit never appear on both sides.

Each probe is a ridge regression (RidgeCV over $\alpha \in [10^{-3}, 10^{4}]$) on standardised inputs and outputs. $R^2$ is averaged across output dimensions.

Every probe has a control: the same probe fitted on a randomly initialised model of the same architecture. There is one control per layer, because a random model does not change across checkpoints. A probe result counts only if

\[R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}} \geq 0.1 \quad (\texttt{probes.control\_margin}).\]

For each target, the lab reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the best layer
\item its $R^2$ and control $R^2$
\item \texttt{formed\_at\_step}, the first checkpoint at which the margin reaches the threshold (null if it never does)
\end{itemize}

The control $R^2$ for \texttt{accel\_step} is high under the default ranges (0.71–0.75). This caps the margin \texttt{accel\_step} can reach. One finding therefore also reports a post hoc, headroom-normalised score, $(R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}})/(1 - R^2_{\text{control}})$, but its pre-registered criterion uses the raw margin [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].

Probes on the out-of-distribution evaluation sets have no random controls. Their $R^2$ values therefore cannot be read as margins [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}, \texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Only the final checkpoint's activations are saved to disk, as float16. Activations for earlier checkpoints are recomputed from the saved weights when needed.

\subsection{Symbolic regression}

When \texttt{probes.symbolic} is enabled, PySR fits a closed-form expression to the best-layer probe direction for \texttt{force} and for \texttt{accel\_step}. The \texttt{accel\_step} fit uses the inputs $r$, $m_2$ and $a$. Each fit reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the equation
\item whether the true law was recovered (\texttt{law\_recovered})
\item \texttt{fit\_error\_vs\_true\_law}
\end{itemize}

Symbolic fits were run at the final checkpoint only, and only in the runs behind [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. These are the only evidence runs with fitted equations.

\subsection{Activation patching}

The pipeline also supports activation patching. Heads are ranked by a force probe on each head's contribution to the residual stream. The top three heads, plus the lowest-ranked head as a control, are each patched with that head's output on a different system, and the relative increase in next-step MSE is recorded. None of the cited findings report patching results.

\section{Results}

The lab's records contain 49 citable findings, all with status \texttt{replicated}. Of these, 36 are supported and 13 are refuted. Together they rest on 115 evidence runs. The budget hard stop cut 4 of those runs short, and we flag each one where its numbers are used. Unless stated otherwise, models have 4 layers and $d_\text{model} = 64$, all probes are linear, and a probe counts as beating its control only if $R^2 - \text{control } R^2 \ge \max(0.1, \text{control spread})$. Where we use the headroom-normalised margin, it is $(R^2 - \text{control})/(1 - \text{control})$. This puts \texttt{accel\_step}, whose random controls are high, on a fair footing with \texttt{force}.

\subsection{1. The models predict well, and probe structure is present}

Every trained configuration below $N = 256$ beats constant-velocity extrapolation:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Default $e \le 0.5$ runs} end at \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} ≈ 0.011 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}].
\item \textbf{3000-step $N = 64$ runs} end at 0.014–0.018 [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}, \texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}, \texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}].
\item \textbf{The 6-layer, $d_\text{model} = 128$ model} reaches 0.0032–0.0037 [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}].
\end{itemize}

\texttt{distance}, \texttt{energy} and \texttt{accel\_step} beat their random controls in every completed run reported here. \texttt{force} is the only one of these targets that often fails its control (Section 3).

\subsection{2. When structure forms}

\textbf{Gravity-relevant structure forms early, in a fixed window of training steps.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Single controls, seeds 2–3.} \texttt{accel\_step} already beats its control at the first checkpoint (step 250), with margins 0.158 / 0.127. That is 1250 steps before \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} first drops below 0.1 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}].
\item \textbf{Denser 25-step checkpoints, seeds 4–5.} \texttt{accel\_step} crosses the 0.1 margin at step 100. At that point \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} is still 1.96 / 1.84, and it first falls below 1 at step 175 [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item \textbf{10-step checkpoints, three further seeds at $N = 64$.} Crossings come at steps 140, 140 and 100, which is 40–90 steps before the model beats the baseline [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}].
\item \textbf{Best-of-5 controls.} Crossings come at steps 85–90 in all three seeds [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].
\item \textbf{Seeds 62–64.} Crossings come at steps 90–100, with the baseline beaten at steps 140–160 [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}].
\end{itemize}

In every case \texttt{distance} and \texttt{energy} form first (steps 0–40), then \texttt{accel\_step}, while \texttt{force} forms late or not at all.

!\href{figure:formation/run-20261011-234250-a7ca}{Formation trajectory at N = 64 with 10-step checkpoints, seed 46}

\textbf{"Forms before beating the baseline" is a property of the baseline, not of formation.}

\begin{itemize}
\item At $N = 16$, \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} drops below 1 by step 20. \texttt{accel\_step} still forms at steps 75–90, within 5–15 steps of the seed-matched $N = 64$ runs, so the ordering flips while the formation step barely moves [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].
\item At $N = 256$ with $e \le 0.85$, \texttt{accel\_step} forms at steps 60–70, the same window as $N = 64$ [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}]. Yet these models never beat constant-velocity extrapolation (final ratio 2.03–2.37) [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}].
\item Halving the learning rate delays formation by only +10, +20 and +30 steps, not the roughly 2× that cumulative $\text{lr} \times \text{steps}$ would predict (refuted) [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The formation window is less precise than first claimed.}

\begin{itemize}
\item A pre-registered window for $N = 64$ was met by only 2 of 5 seeds. With 5-step checkpoints, persistent formation came at steps 65–90 (refuted) [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\item In 2 of 3 seeds the \texttt{accel\_step} margin never reached a 0.15 bar (refuted) [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{A brief early force signal appears and then disappears.} At $N = 64$ and $N = 16$, \texttt{force} reaches a margin of 0.106–0.189 at step 10 and decays to about 0 by steps 20–50. This survives best-of-5 controls [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}, \texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}, \texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}].

\textbf{A lasting force code forms only with longer training.}

\begin{itemize}
\item Training to 1500 steps on data seed 1 gives persistent force formation at steps 400–600, with final margins of 0.167–0.196. The best layer for force moves to layer 1, the \texttt{distance} layer [\texttt{f-20261013-025812-b9ee}].
\item At $d_\text{model} = 128$ over 5000 steps, force forms 250–300 steps after \texttt{accel\_step} and keeps strengthening late (+0.074 / +0.081 from step 500 to 5000). Over the same span \texttt{accel\_step} gains only +0.025 / +0.022 [\texttt{f-20261013-020028-8792}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{3. Per-snapshot acceleration vs physical force}

\textbf{Short runs at $N = 64$ hold an \texttt{accel\_step} code but no \texttt{force} code.} \texttt{force} fails its control in all 3 seeds of each of two experiments (final margins 0.000–0.080) [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}].

\textbf{After full-length training both targets beat their controls, and which one leads depends on the metric.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Raw margin favours \texttt{force}} in several settings:
\item 3000-step runs at $N = 64$ [\texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}];
\item a smaller 2-layer, $d_\text{model} = 32$ model (force 0.221 / 0.255 vs \texttt{accel\_step} 0.146 / 0.128) [\texttt{f-20261013-000220-ab2d}];
\item a wider $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ range. Here the hypothesis that force would fall behind \texttt{accel\_step} was refuted [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item \textbf{Headroom-normalised margin favours \texttt{accel\_step}} in almost every case: about 0.65–0.79, against about 0.35–0.59 for \texttt{force} [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}, \texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}, \texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}, \texttt{f-20261014-040037-f9bb}]. The one exception is seed 2 of the 2-layer model [\texttt{f-20261013-000220-ab2d}].
\end{itemize}

The \texttt{force} signal usually peaks at layer 1, where \texttt{distance} is decoded at $R^2$ ≈ 0.85–0.93. No finding has yet regressed \texttt{distance} out of the force target.

!\href{figure:heatmap/run-20261013-074422-d66a/accel_step}{accel\_step probe heatmap, N = 64, seed 24}

\textbf{Linear decodability stops tracking prediction quality in a larger model.} In the 6-layer, $d_\text{model} = 128$ model at $e \le 0.85$, linear \texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ levels off at about 0.70–0.74 after step 2000. Over the same steps, \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} falls a further 5.5–6.8× [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}].

\subsection{5. Fixing the mass ratio at $m_2 = 0.5$}

\begin{itemize}
\item seed 14 stopped at step 1500 (completion 0.55), below the 80\% needed to count as an agreeing seed;
\item seed 15 stopped at step 2500 (completion 0.917).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What the table shows:}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The deficit is not in the $m_2$ factor.} The hypothesis that fixing $m_2$ removes the drop was refuted [\texttt{f-20261013-234552-f8e8}]. Controls stay nearly flat in every pairing, so what is lost at $N = 128$ appears to be the readout of $1/r^2$.
\item \textbf{It persists through 12000 steps.} At $N = 128$, force gained only 0.008 / 0.029 between steps 6000 and 12000, while \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} fell about 6–10× [\texttt{f-20261014-050044-cd2f}].
\item \textbf{It holds when matched on loss.} Read at matched \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio}, the gaps are about 0.13 and 0.07 [\texttt{f-20261014-051311-3e2e}].
\item \textbf{It holds on data seed 1, with smaller gaps.} The data-seed-1 gaps compare layer 1 with layer 1. They are smaller than on data seed 0 but keep the same seed ranking [\texttt{f-20261014-060114-ee01}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Caveats:}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Cross-layer comparison.} Seed 22's data-seed-0 gaps compare different layers.
\item \textbf{Possible slow convergence.} Seed 23's same-layer gap on data seed 0 narrowed from about 0.096 to 0.074 between 6000 and 12000 steps, so slower convergence is not ruled out for that seed [\texttt{f-20261014-051311-3e2e}].
\item \textbf{Wide spread across seeds.} At 6000 steps on data seed 0, per-seed gaps span 0.011–0.143 [\texttt{f-20261014-040037-f9bb}].
\item \textbf{\texttt{accel\_step} is unaffected.} Across these runs its normalised margin stays at 0.72–0.80 [\texttt{f-20261014-050044-cd2f}, \texttt{f-20261014-051311-3e2e}, \texttt{f-20261014-060043-9a03}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{6. Eccentricity sets the control level}

\texttt{accel\_step} at $N = 64$, 600 steps:

| Training $e$ range | Control $R^2$ | Trained $R^2$ | Normalised margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\le 0.5$ [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}] | 0.724–0.746 | 0.890–0.902 | — |
| $\le 0.7$ [\texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}] | 0.543–0.559 | 0.80–0.81 | ≈0.55 |
| $\le 0.75$ [\texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}] | 0.463–0.487 | 0.746–0.753 | 0.51–0.54 |
| $\le 0.8$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}] | 0.372–0.396 | 0.670–0.672 | 0.455–0.475 |
| $\le 0.85$ [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}] | 0.248–0.333 | 0.507–0.594 | 0.34–0.40 |

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Wider eccentricity lowers both the control and linear decodability}, and the decline steepens above $e \le 0.75$ [\texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}].
\item \textbf{Force is weaker at every eccentricity ceiling.} Its normalised margins are about 0.11–0.22, and it forms late, at steps 250–590 [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}, \texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}, \texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}, \texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{7. Out-of-distribution transfer}

\textbf{Trained on $e \le 0.5$} [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Low mass ratios} ($m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$): error is about 3.6× the in-distribution error.
\item \textbf{High eccentricity} ($e \in [0.5, 0.8]$): error is 16–18× in-distribution, which fails the pre-registered 10× criterion (refuted). The models still beat the baseline, and force $R^2$ falls to 0.40 / 0.34.
\item \textbf{Three-body sets:} ratios of 72 and 70 are exploratory only.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Trained on $e \in [0, 0.8]$, the high-$e$ prediction gap closes} to 1.01–1.05× in-distribution [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

\begin{itemize}
\item The budget hard stop ended both runs at step 2500 (completion 0.867 and 0.9), so their probes describe the step-2500 weights.
\item Even with matched prediction error, force $R^2$ on held-out high-$e$ systems is only 0.366–0.377, against 0.729–0.802 on low-$e$ systems. These out-of-distribution probes have no random controls.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{8. Symbolic regression has not recovered $r^{-2}$}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Strong \texttt{accel\_step} probes} ($R^2$ 0.831 / 0.881): the fitted direction has the form $a^2/r - c\,a$ with $c \approx 0.55$, not the true $a^3/r^2$. Fit errors are 0.245 / 0.201, and force fits as about $0.8/r$ [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}].
\item \textbf{Training at $e \le 0.9$:} the fits are again $r^{-1}$ (refuted) [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{Possible fitter artefact:} the fitter has not yet been run on the true \texttt{accel\_step} target, so we cannot rule out that the $r^{-1}$ form comes from the fitter rather than the model.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{9. Reproducibility}

\textbf{The original $N = 256$ reference margins did not reproduce on an exact rerun (refuted).} The rerun gave force margins of 0.113 / 0.003 [\texttt{f-20261013-050513-2c7a}].

\textbf{The current pipeline is deterministic for a fixed seed.} A second identical rerun matched the first to 3 decimals at every checkpoint [\texttt{f-20261013-054603-d6cc}]. Two consequences follow:

\begin{itemize}
\item The force gap between seeds 20 and 21 at $N = 256$ is genuine seed variance.
\item Why the original runs differ is still unexplained.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{10. Null and negative results}

The 13 refuted hypotheses:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Widening $m_2$ would put force behind \texttt{accel\_step}.} It did not [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item \textbf{$e \le 0.5$ training would transfer to high $e$ within 10×.} The error was 16–18× [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}].
\item \textbf{Fixing $m_2$ would lift force $R^2$ to 0.85.} It did not [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}].
\item \textbf{Wide-$e$ training would yield an $r^{-2}$ fit.} The fits were $r^{-1}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{\texttt{accel\_step} would clear a 0.15 margin, and the early force spike would vanish under stricter controls.} Neither happened [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].
\item \textbf{Formation would scale in proportion to the learning rate.} The shift was sub-proportional [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}].
\item \textbf{$N = 64$ formation would fall in a narrow window.} Only 2 of 5 seeds did [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\item \textbf{$N = 16$ would raise the normalised \texttt{accel\_step} margin.} It was level with $N = 64$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}].
\item \textbf{Force would decline gradually from $N = 16$.} $N = 64$ matched $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}].
\item \textbf{An exact rerun would reproduce the original $N = 256$ margins.} It did not [\texttt{f-20261013-050513-2c7a}].
\item \textbf{The $N = 128 \to 256$ force drop is entirely a control effect.} On data seed 0, trained $R^2$ also fell [\texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}].
\item \textbf{1000 extra steps would close the $N = 256$ gap.} They did not [\texttt{f-20261013-083108-2efd}].
\item \textbf{Fixing $m_2$ would remove the $N = 64 \to 128$ drop.} The drop persisted [\texttt{f-20261013-234552-f8e8}].
\end{enumerate}

Several supported findings are also nulls for force, in that force fails its control:

\begin{itemize}
\item short $N = 64$ runs [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}];
\item most $N = 256$ runs [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}, \texttt{f-20261013-063603-95b5}, \texttt{f-20261013-063631-c4ed}];
\item $m_2$-fixed $N = 128$ runs [\texttt{f-20261014-004426-68d6}, \texttt{f-20261014-015607-930a}, \texttt{f-20261014-050044-cd2f}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{11. Not yet established}

No finding yet reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{velocity} probe results;
\item activation patching or any other causal test;
\item a force probe with \texttt{distance} and \texttt{accel\_step} regressed out;
\item a random-control pool shared across $N$;
\item nonlinear or log-target probes;
\item symbolic regression on the true \texttt{accel\_step} target.
\end{itemize}

Limits that apply throughout:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Single controls.} The earliest findings used a single random control [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}, \texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}, \texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item \textbf{Rounded values.} Several paired comparisons use 3-decimal trajectory values whose $R^2$ field may be a maximum over layers [\texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}, \texttt{f-20261014-004426-68d6}, \texttt{f-20261014-015607-930a}, \texttt{f-20261014-060114-ee01}].
\item \textbf{Training length.} Force was still rising at the end of most runs.
\item \textbf{Datasets and architecture.} Only two data seeds have been tested, and most results come from one architecture.
\end{itemize}

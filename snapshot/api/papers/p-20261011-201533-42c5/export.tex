\section{Abstract}

We ask whether small transformers trained to predict simulated two-body orbits ($G = M = 1$) encode the gravitational law or a step-based surrogate, and when that structure forms. Linear probes on the residual stream target force ($m_2/r^2$), per-snapshot acceleration \texttt{accel\_step} ($\propto a^3/r^2$), distance and energy. A probe result counts only if it beats several random-init controls. The lab has 51 replicated findings (38 supported, 13 refuted) from 120 evidence runs, 4 of them cut short by the budget hard stop. \texttt{accel\_step} is consistently decodable. At $N = 64$ it forms at steps 85–140, before the model beats constant-velocity extrapolation [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}]. Its normalised margin stays at roughly 0.63–0.75 from $N = 16$ to $N = 256$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}, \texttt{f-20261013-051807-f3f1}, \texttt{f-20261013-063603-95b5}]. Force is weaker and depends on sampling. From $N = 64$ to $N = 128$, trained force $R^2$ falls 0.098–0.128 while controls stay flat [\texttt{f-20261013-075824-35f0}]. This gap survives fixing $m_2$ and training for 12000 steps [\texttt{f-20261013-234552-f8e8}, \texttt{f-20261014-051311-3e2e}], and force fails its control at $N = 256$ [\texttt{f-20261013-063603-95b5}]. The main negative result is that symbolic fits to the decoded directions give $r^{-1}$, not $r^{-2}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}, \texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. Whether that comes from the fitter is untested.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data generation}

All orbits are simulated with REBOUND in units where $G = 1$ and the primary mass is $M = 1$. The gravitational force on body 2 is therefore $|F| = m_2/r^2$, where $m_2$ is the mass ratio. Varying $M$ would add nothing. Positions are sampled at fixed phase intervals, so absolute mass and time scale cannot be identified from them.

Each orbit is sampled at a fixed number of snapshots per orbit, $N$ (\texttt{steps\_per\_orbit}). The time between snapshots is therefore $\Delta t = P/N$, which grows with orbit size ($\Delta t^2 \propto a^3$). The model never sees physical time.

Three-body systems are hierarchical: the third body sits at 3.5–6× the inner semi-major axis. They are used only as an evaluation set for models trained on two-body systems.

The configuration schema adds these fields to the specification:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{data.eccentricity\_range}
\item \texttt{data.data\_seed}
\item \texttt{probes.control\_margin}
\item \texttt{probes.patching}
\item \texttt{probes.symbolic}
\item \texttt{eval\_sets}, for out-of-distribution evaluation
\end{itemize}

Unknown fields are rejected. The training ranges differ between the cited experiments:

\begin{itemize}
\item Some runs train on the default range, which reaches $e \leq 0.5$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item Some runs widen the mass ratio to $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item Some runs widen eccentricity to $e \in [0, 0.8]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\item Some runs widen both, to $e \in [0, 0.9]$ and $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. Both of these runs share data seed 0.
\end{itemize}

The held-out evaluation sets are:

\begin{itemize}
\item low mass ratio, $m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$
\item high eccentricity, $e \in [0.5, 0.8]$
\item three-body systems [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]
\item held-out low-$e$ and high-$e$ splits, used for the wide-eccentricity runs [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tokens, model and prediction target}

Each token is one time step. It holds the $xy$ positions of up to three bodies (6 numbers) in the centre-of-mass frame, with absent bodies set to zero. Because every system uses the same token format, a model trained on two bodies can be evaluated on three-body sets.

The model predicts the normalised displacement to the next step. All evidence runs use one transformer architecture: $L = 4$ layers, width $d = 64$, and $N = 64$ snapshots per orbit [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Prediction quality is reported as \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio}. This is the model's MSE divided by the MSE of constant-velocity extrapolation, $\hat{x}_{t+1} = 2x_t - x_{t-1}$. Both are computed in physical position units over steps $t \geq 1$ on the same held-out systems. A value below 1 means the model beats the baseline.

\subsection{Training}

The lab's default first-experiment (E1) configuration trains for 5k steps at batch size 128 and learning rate $10^{-3}$. Training runs on a laptop GPU (MPS) and is kept CPU-friendly.

Checkpoint spacing varies between experiments. One formation experiment probes every 250 steps [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}]. A dense-checkpoint experiment probes every 25 steps early in training [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].

Every finding is replicated on two training seeds.

\textbf{Budget hard stop.} A runaway guard stops a run once it reaches 2× its seed's share of \texttt{budget.max\_compute\_hours}. The clock starts at the first training step, so dataset generation does not count. A stopped run is checkpointed and probed at its stop step. It records \texttt{steps\_completed} and \texttt{steps\_planned}, and its \texttt{completion} is the fraction of planned steps it trained. Runs below 80\% completion do not count toward replication.

Two of the 12 evidence runs were cut short by an earlier version of this guard. That version stopped at 1.2× the budget share and counted dataset generation against it. Both runs are in [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]: seed 9 stopped at completion 0.867 (step 2640 of 3000) and seed 8 at 0.9 (step 2714). Under that version the probes were taken at the last regular checkpoint, step 2500, not at the stop step. Their \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still falling when training stopped.

\subsection{Probe targets}

Linear probes read five quantities from the residual stream at every layer:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{distance}
\item relative \texttt{velocity}
\item \texttt{energy}, which is constant per system
\item physical \texttt{force}, $|F| = m_2/r^2$
\item the per-snapshot acceleration \texttt{accel\_step}:
\end{itemize}

\[|a_{\text{step}}| = \frac{F}{m_2}\,\Delta t^2 = \left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\right)^2 \frac{a^3}{(1+m_2)\,r^2}.\]

Next-step prediction needs \texttt{accel\_step}, not the physical force. A weak \texttt{force} probe on its own could therefore mean the model learned a per-snapshot version of gravity, not that it learned no gravity. For this reason \texttt{force} and \texttt{accel\_step} are always compared.

A unit test checks two properties of \texttt{accel\_step}:

\begin{itemize}
\item It equals the second difference of positions, to within 1\%.
\item It is not proportional to the physical force across orbit sizes.
\end{itemize}

\texttt{velocity} probe results do not appear in any cited finding.

\subsection{Probe protocol and random-model controls}

Probes run on 300 held-out systems, at every other time step from $t = 8$. Systems are split 70/30 into probe-train and probe-test sets, so rows from the same orbit never appear on both sides.

Each probe is a ridge regression (RidgeCV over $\alpha \in [10^{-3}, 10^{4}]$) on standardised inputs and outputs. $R^2$ is averaged across output dimensions.

Every probe has a control: the same probe fitted on a randomly initialised model of the same architecture. There is one control per layer, because a random model does not change across checkpoints. A probe result counts only if

\[R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}} \geq 0.1 \quad (\texttt{probes.control\_margin}).\]

For each target, the lab reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the best layer
\item its $R^2$ and control $R^2$
\item \texttt{formed\_at\_step}, the first checkpoint at which the margin reaches the threshold (null if it never does)
\end{itemize}

The control $R^2$ for \texttt{accel\_step} is high under the default ranges (0.71–0.75). This caps the margin \texttt{accel\_step} can reach. One finding therefore also reports a post hoc, headroom-normalised score, $(R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}})/(1 - R^2_{\text{control}})$, but its pre-registered criterion uses the raw margin [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].

Probes on the out-of-distribution evaluation sets have no random controls. Their $R^2$ values therefore cannot be read as margins [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}, \texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Only the final checkpoint's activations are saved to disk, as float16. Activations for earlier checkpoints are recomputed from the saved weights when needed.

\subsection{Symbolic regression}

When \texttt{probes.symbolic} is enabled, PySR fits a closed-form expression to the best-layer probe direction for \texttt{force} and for \texttt{accel\_step}. The \texttt{accel\_step} fit uses the inputs $r$, $m_2$ and $a$. Each fit reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the equation
\item whether the true law was recovered (\texttt{law\_recovered})
\item \texttt{fit\_error\_vs\_true\_law}
\end{itemize}

Symbolic fits were run at the final checkpoint only, and only in the runs behind [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. These are the only evidence runs with fitted equations.

\subsection{Activation patching}

The pipeline also supports activation patching. Heads are ranked by a force probe on each head's contribution to the residual stream. The top three heads, plus the lowest-ranked head as a control, are each patched with that head's output on a different system, and the relative increase in next-step MSE is recorded. None of the cited findings report patching results.

\section{Results}

The lab has 51 citable findings. All 51 are replicated: 38 support their hypothesis and 13 refute it. Together they rest on 120 evidence runs. The budget hard stop cut 4 of those runs short, and we flag each one where its numbers appear. Unless stated otherwise, models have 4 layers and $d_{\text{model}} = 64$. A probe result counts only if it clears its random-model control by the required margin, $\max(0.1, \text{control\_spread})$. The earliest findings used a single control, and we say so where they are cited.

Across these findings, four things hold. Next-step prediction beats constant-velocity extrapolation by a wide margin. The residual stream linearly encodes the per-snapshot acceleration \texttt{accel\_step} $\propto a^3/((1+m_2)r^2)$ above control in every run that reports it. It forms early, at a training step that is stable across seeds, learning rates and sampling densities. Physical force $|F| = m_2/r^2$ is weaker, forms later and depends on the setup. It fades as sampling gets finer, and fixing $m_2$ does not rescue it. Symbolic fits to the probe directions have not recovered the $r^{-2}$ law.

\subsection{1. Prediction quality}

Every completed two-body model that reports a final \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} beats the constant-velocity baseline, with one exception: $N = 256$ runs trained for only 600 steps, which end at 2.03–2.37 [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Typical 3000–4000-step runs at $N = 64$, $e \le 0.5$:} final ratios of about 0.011–0.016 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}, \texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}, \texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}].
\item \textbf{Longer or wider runs:} 0.0030–0.0031 at 12000 steps [\texttt{f-20261013-051738-2a37}], and 0.0037 / 0.0032 for a 6-layer, $d_{\text{model}} = 128$ model [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}].
\item \textbf{Coarse sampling:} at $N = 16$ the constant-velocity baseline is weak, and the ratio reaches 0.0007 [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{2. When structure forms}

\textbf{\texttt{accel\_step} forms before the model beats the baseline at $N = 64$.}

\begin{itemize}
\item With 25-step checkpoints, the \texttt{accel\_step} margin first reached 0.1 at step 100 in both seeds. At that point \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still 1.96 / 1.84, and it first fell below 1 by step 175 (single control) [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item With 10-step checkpoints and best-of-several controls, formation came at steps 140 / 140 / 100. That is 40–90 steps before \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} < 1 (steps 180–190) [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}].
\item A second set of seeds formed at steps 85–90, 45–75 steps before the baseline crossing [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].
\item Persistent formation at steps 90–100 came 40–60 steps before the baseline crossing [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}].
\item In the earliest 250-step-resolution runs, gravity-related probes already beat control at step 250. That is 1250 steps before \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} first fell below 0.1 (single control) [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}].
\end{itemize}

!\href{figure:formation/run-20261012-043022-144a}{Probe formation, N = 64, e ≤ 0.5, seed 64}

\textbf{Formation follows the training step, not the loss.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$N = 16$:} \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} falls below 1 at step 20, but \texttt{accel\_step} forms at steps 75–90, within 5–15 steps of the seed-matched $N = 64$ runs. The ordering against the baseline flips while the formation step barely moves. "Beats the baseline" is therefore a baseline-dependent marker [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}].
\item \textbf{$e \le 0.85$:} \texttt{accel\_step} forms at steps 60–70 at $N = 64$ [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}]. At $N = 256$ it forms at steps 60–70 too, even though those models never beat the baseline [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}].
\item \textbf{Learning rate:} halving it delays formation by only +10 / +20 / +30 steps. Proportional scaling would predict roughly 2× (refuted) [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}].
\item \textbf{A narrow window was too narrow:} a pre-registered $N = 64$ window held for only 2 of 5 seeds. Persistent formation spanned steps 65–90, and part of the shift may come from the finer 5-step checkpoints (refuted) [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Order of formation.} \texttt{distance} and \texttt{energy} form first (steps 5–50 at fine resolution). \texttt{accel\_step} follows. Force forms late or not at all [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}, \texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}].

\textbf{The early force transient survives best-of-5 controls.} It reaches a force margin of 0.166–0.189 at step 10 and decays to about 0.00–0.02 by steps 40–50 [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}]. A transient of 0.106–0.127 at step 10 also appears at $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261012-015955-fc2b}]. The same finding refuted the claim that \texttt{accel\_step} would clear a 0.15 margin: it plateaued at 0.134–0.135 in 2 of 3 seeds.

\textbf{Force can form later with longer training.}

\begin{itemize}
\item On data seed 1, 1500-step runs make force beat control persistently in all 3 seeds, at steps 400–600. This happens only after force's best layer moves to layer 1, the \texttt{distance} layer. At step 600, force was already at 0.104–0.138, compared with 0.085–0.099 in 600-step runs [\texttt{f-20261013-025812-b9ee}].
\item At $d_{\text{model}} = 128$, force forms at steps 350 / 400, against step 100 for \texttt{accel\_step}. It keeps gaining through step 5000 (+0.074 / +0.081, against +0.025 / +0.022 for \texttt{accel\_step}) [\texttt{f-20261013-020028-8792}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{3. \texttt{accel\_step} versus force}

\textbf{Raw margin and headroom-normalised margin rank the two probes differently.} \texttt{accel\_step} controls are high (often 0.71–0.81 at $e \le 0.5$), which caps its raw margin. Force often has the larger raw margin, but \texttt{accel\_step} leads on headroom-normalised margin, $(R^2 - c)/(1 - c)$.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Wider $m_2$ range:} widening $m_2$ to $[0.01, 1]$ did not put force behind \texttt{accel\_step} on raw margin (refuted). On normalised margin, \texttt{accel\_step} leads 0.70 vs 0.48 and 0.71 vs 0.42 [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item \textbf{Same pattern elsewhere:} the identical split appears at $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}], at $N = 64$ on both data seeds [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}, \texttt{f-20261013-054512-1b08}, \texttt{f-20261013-075855-f75f}], and in a 2-layer, $d_{\text{model}} = 32$ cell [\texttt{f-20261013-000220-ab2d}].
\item \textbf{Linear decodability can stop tracking loss.} For the 6-layer, $d_{\text{model}} = 128$ model at $e \le 0.85$:
\item \texttt{accel\_step} $R^2$ plateaus at about 0.70–0.74 and rises only +0.025 / +0.022 after step 2000, while \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} falls a further 6.8× / 5.5×.
\item Its normalised margin is 0.66 / 0.61, against 0.41 for force [\texttt{f-20261012-030233-ab28}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{$N = 128$ to $N = 256$:} the cause depends on the dataset.

\begin{itemize}
\item On data seed 1, the drop is entirely a control effect [\texttt{f-20261013-063631-c4ed}].

\end{itemize}

\textbf{The $N = 256$ shortfall is not just slow convergence.}

\begin{itemize}
\item 1000 extra steps added only about 0.011–0.014 of trained force $R^2$ (refuted) [\texttt{f-20261013-083108-2efd}].

\end{itemize}

\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item seed 14 stopped at step 1500 (completion 0.55), below the 80\% needed to count toward replication;
\item seed 15 stopped at step 2500 (completion 0.917).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Caveats:}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Cross-layer comparisons.} Several pairings compare different layers, notably seed 22 on data seed 0.
\item \textbf{Possible slow convergence.} Seed 23's same-layer gap on data seed 0 narrowed from about 0.096 to 0.074 between 6000 and 12000 steps, so slower convergence is not ruled out for that seed [\texttt{f-20261014-051311-3e2e}].
\item \textbf{Wide seed spread.} At 6000 steps on data seed 0, per-seed gaps span 0.011–0.143 [\texttt{f-20261014-040037-f9bb}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{6. Eccentricity sets the control level}

\texttt{accel\_step} at $N = 64$, 600 steps:

| Training $e$ range | Control $R^2$ | Trained $R^2$ | Normalised margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\le 0.5$ [\texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}] | 0.724–0.746 | 0.890–0.902 | — |
| $\le 0.7$ [\texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}] | 0.543–0.559 | 0.80–0.81 | ≈0.55 |
| $\le 0.75$ [\texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}] | 0.463–0.487 | 0.746–0.753 | 0.51–0.54 |
| $\le 0.8$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}] | 0.372–0.396 | 0.670–0.672 | 0.455–0.475 |
| $\le 0.85$ [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}] | 0.248–0.333 | 0.507–0.594 | 0.34–0.40 |
| $\le 0.9$ [\texttt{f-20261016-012952-f2b5}] | 0.150–0.170 | 0.3927–0.4361 | 0.284–0.320 |

\textbf{Wider eccentricity lowers both the control and linear decodability, and the decline steepens.}

\begin{itemize}
\item Above $e \le 0.75$ the drop accelerates [\texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}].
\item From $e \le 0.85$ to $e \le 0.9$, raw $R^2$ fell about 0.13–0.16 on seeds 70–72, relative to $e \le 0.85$ values of 0.557 / 0.541 / 0.570 from a separate experiment.
\item Even at $e \le 0.9$, \texttt{accel\_step} still beats its control by 0.240–0.266 and forms at step 100 [\texttt{f-20261016-012952-f2b5}].
\end{itemize}

These 600-step runs had not converged.

!\href{figure:heatmap/run-20261016-005901-b03a/accel_step}{accel\_step probe heatmap, e ≤ 0.9, seed 70}

\textbf{Force is weaker at every eccentricity ceiling.} Its normalised margins are about 0.09–0.22, it forms late (steps 250–590), and it fails its control in some seeds [\texttt{f-20261012-034018-5526}, \texttt{f-20261012-055051-b178}, \texttt{f-20261013-000359-37e2}, \texttt{f-20261013-005923-526c}, \texttt{f-20261016-012952-f2b5}].

\subsection{7. Out-of-distribution transfer}

\textbf{Models trained on $e \le 0.5$} [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Low mass ratios} ($m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$): error is about 3.6× the in-distribution error.
\item \textbf{High eccentricity} ($e \in [0.5, 0.8]$): error is 16–18× in-distribution, failing the pre-registered 10× criterion (refuted). The models still beat the baseline, and best force $R^2$ falls to 0.40 / 0.34.
\item \textbf{Three-body sets:} ratios of 72 and 70 are exploratory only.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Training on $e \in [0, 0.8]$ closes the high-$e$ prediction gap} to 1.01–1.05× in-distribution [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

\begin{itemize}
\item The budget hard stop ended both runs at step 2500 (completion 0.867 and 0.9), so their probes describe the step-2500 weights.
\item Force $R^2$ on held-out high-$e$ systems is still only 0.366–0.377, against 0.729–0.802 on low-$e$ systems. These out-of-distribution probes have no random controls.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{8. Symbolic regression has not recovered $r^{-2}$}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Strong \texttt{accel\_step} probes} ($R^2$ 0.831 / 0.881): the fitted direction has the form $a^2/r - c\,a$ with $c \approx 0.55$, not $a^3/r^2$. Fit errors are 0.245 / 0.201, and force fits as about $0.8/r$ [\texttt{f-20261011-231118-7a37}].
\item \textbf{Training at $e \le 0.9$:} the fits are again $r^{-1}$ (refuted) [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{Possible fitter artefact:} the fitter has not yet been run on the true \texttt{accel\_step} target, so the $r^{-1}$ form may come from the fitter rather than the model.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{9. Reproducibility}

\textbf{The original $N = 256$ reference margins did not reproduce on an exact rerun (refuted).} The rerun gave force margins of 0.113 / 0.003 [\texttt{f-20261013-050513-2c7a}].

\textbf{The current pipeline is deterministic for a fixed seed.} A second identical rerun matched the first to 3 decimals at every checkpoint [\texttt{f-20261013-054603-d6cc}]. So the force gap between seeds 20 and 21 at $N = 256$ is genuine seed variance. Why the original runs differ is still unexplained.

\subsection{10. Null and negative results}

The 13 refuted hypotheses:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Widening $m_2$ would put force behind \texttt{accel\_step} on raw margin.} It did not [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item \textbf{$e \le 0.5$ training would transfer to high $e$ within 10×.} The error was 16–18× [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}].
\item \textbf{Fixing $m_2$ would lift force $R^2$ to 0.85.} It did not [\texttt{f-20261011-223000-b5f9}].
\item \textbf{Wide-$e$ training would yield an $r^{-2}$ fit.} The fits were $r^{-1}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{\texttt{accel\_step} would clear a 0.15 margin, and the early force spike would vanish under stricter controls.} Neither happened [\texttt{f-20261012-005950-42a7}].
\item \textbf{Formation would scale in proportion to the learning rate.} The shift was sub-proportional [\texttt{f-20261012-044258-ab88}].
\item \textbf{$N = 64$ formation would fall in a narrow window.} Only 2 of 5 seeds did [\texttt{f-20261012-054929-0067}].
\item \textbf{$N = 16$ would raise the normalised \texttt{accel\_step} margin.} It was level with $N = 64$ [\texttt{f-20261013-000329-a86b}].
\item \textbf{Force would decline gradually from $N = 16$.} $N = 64$ matched $N = 16$ [\texttt{f-20261013-015914-3f49}].
\item \textbf{An exact rerun would reproduce the original $N = 256$ margins.} It did not [\texttt{f-20261013-050513-2c7a}].
\item \textbf{The $N = 128 \to 256$ force drop is entirely a control effect.} On data seed 0, trained $R^2$ also fell [\texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}].
\item \textbf{1000 extra steps would close the $N = 256$ gap.} They did not [\texttt{f-20261013-083108-2efd}].
\item \textbf{Fixing $m_2$ would remove the $N = 64 \to 128$ drop.} The drop persisted [\texttt{f-20261013-234552-f8e8}].
\end{enumerate}

Several supported findings are also nulls for force, in that force fails its control:

\begin{itemize}
\item short $N = 64$ runs [\texttt{f-20261012-002713-8d93}, \texttt{f-20261012-044142-d490}];
\item most $N = 256$ runs [\texttt{f-20261012-034134-5e82}, \texttt{f-20261013-063603-95b5}, \texttt{f-20261013-063631-c4ed}];
\item $m_2$-fixed $N = 128$ runs [\texttt{f-20261014-004426-68d6}, \texttt{f-20261014-015607-930a}, \texttt{f-20261014-050044-cd2f}];
\item one of three seeds at $e \le 0.9$ [\texttt{f-20261016-012952-f2b5}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{11. Not yet established}

No finding yet reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{velocity} probe results;
\item activation patching or any other causal test;
\item a force probe with \texttt{distance} and \texttt{accel\_step} regressed out;
\item a random-control pool shared across $N$;
\item nonlinear or log-target probes;
\item symbolic regression on the true \texttt{accel\_step} target.
\end{itemize}

Limits that apply throughout:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Single controls.} The earliest findings used a single random control [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}, \texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}, \texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item \textbf{Rounded values.} Several paired comparisons use 3-decimal trajectory values whose $R^2$ field may be a maximum over layers [\texttt{f-20261013-075749-3d2c}, \texttt{f-20261014-004426-68d6}, \texttt{f-20261014-015607-930a}, \texttt{f-20261014-060114-ee01}].
\item \textbf{Training length.} Force was still rising at the end of most runs.
\item \textbf{Datasets and architecture.} Only two data seeds have been tested, and most results come from the 4-layer, $d_{\text{model}} = 64$ architecture.
\end{itemize}

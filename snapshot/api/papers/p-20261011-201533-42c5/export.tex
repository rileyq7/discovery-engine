\section{Abstract}

Do small transformers trained to predict simulated two-body orbits encode the gravitational law, and when does that structure form? We probe the residual stream with linear probes for the physical force $m_2/r^2$, the per-snapshot acceleration \texttt{accel\_step} $\propto a^3/((1+m_2)r^2)$, distance and energy. A probe result counts only if its $R^2$ beats a random-init control by at least 0.1. The six findings were all replicated across two seeds: three were supported and three refuted. They rest on 12 runs, two of which were cut short by the budget stop. The models beat constant-velocity extrapolation, with a final \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} of about 0.011 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}]. \texttt{accel\_step} structure formed before prediction beat the baseline: its margin reached 0.1 at step 100, while \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still 1.96 and 1.84 [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}]. Error on high-eccentricity systems was 16–18× the in-distribution error [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]. That gap closed to about 1.0× once training covered $e \in [0, 0.8]$, in runs stopped at 0.867 and 0.9 completion [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]. Widening $m_2$ did not make the force margin fall behind \texttt{accel\_step} [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}]. The main negative result is that symbolic fits to the probe directions gave $r^{-1}$, not $r^{-2}$ (fit errors 0.71 and 0.62), so no explicit inverse-square law has been extracted [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data generation}

All orbits are simulated with REBOUND in units where $G = 1$ and the primary mass is $M = 1$. The gravitational force on body 2 is therefore $|F| = m_2/r^2$, where $m_2$ is the mass ratio. Varying $M$ would add nothing. Positions are sampled at fixed phase intervals, so absolute mass and time scale cannot be identified from them.

Each orbit is sampled at a fixed number of snapshots per orbit, $N$ (\texttt{steps\_per\_orbit}). The time between snapshots is therefore $\Delta t = P/N$, which grows with orbit size ($\Delta t^2 \propto a^3$). The model never sees physical time.

Three-body systems are hierarchical: the third body sits at 3.5–6× the inner semi-major axis. They are used only as an evaluation set for models trained on two-body systems.

The configuration schema adds these fields to the specification:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{data.eccentricity\_range}
\item \texttt{data.data\_seed}
\item \texttt{probes.control\_margin}
\item \texttt{probes.patching}
\item \texttt{probes.symbolic}
\item \texttt{eval\_sets}, for out-of-distribution evaluation
\end{itemize}

Unknown fields are rejected. The training ranges differ between the cited experiments:

\begin{itemize}
\item Some runs train on the default range, which reaches $e \leq 0.5$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item Some runs widen the mass ratio to $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item Some runs widen eccentricity to $e \in [0, 0.8]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\item Some runs widen both, to $e \in [0, 0.9]$ and $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. Both of these runs share data seed 0.
\end{itemize}

The held-out evaluation sets are:

\begin{itemize}
\item low mass ratio, $m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$
\item high eccentricity, $e \in [0.5, 0.8]$
\item three-body systems [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]
\item held-out low-$e$ and high-$e$ splits, used for the wide-eccentricity runs [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tokens, model and prediction target}

Each token is one time step. It holds the $xy$ positions of up to three bodies (6 numbers) in the centre-of-mass frame, with absent bodies set to zero. Because every system uses the same token format, a model trained on two bodies can be evaluated on three-body sets.

The model predicts the normalised displacement to the next step. All evidence runs use one transformer architecture: $L = 4$ layers, width $d = 64$, and $N = 64$ snapshots per orbit [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Prediction quality is reported as \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio}. This is the model's MSE divided by the MSE of constant-velocity extrapolation, $\hat{x}_{t+1} = 2x_t - x_{t-1}$. Both are computed in physical position units over steps $t \geq 1$ on the same held-out systems. A value below 1 means the model beats the baseline.

\subsection{Training}

The lab's default first-experiment (E1) configuration trains for 5k steps at batch size 128 and learning rate $10^{-3}$. Training runs on a laptop GPU (MPS) and is kept CPU-friendly.

Checkpoint spacing varies between experiments. One formation experiment probes every 250 steps [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}]. A dense-checkpoint experiment probes every 25 steps early in training [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].

Every finding is replicated on two training seeds.

\textbf{Budget hard stop.} A runaway guard stops a run once it reaches 2× its seed's share of \texttt{budget.max\_compute\_hours}. The clock starts at the first training step, so dataset generation does not count. A stopped run is checkpointed and probed at its stop step. It records \texttt{steps\_completed} and \texttt{steps\_planned}, and its \texttt{completion} is the fraction of planned steps it trained. Runs below 80\% completion do not count toward replication.

Two of the 12 evidence runs were cut short by an earlier version of this guard. That version stopped at 1.2× the budget share and counted dataset generation against it. Both runs are in [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}]: seed 9 stopped at completion 0.867 (step 2640 of 3000) and seed 8 at 0.9 (step 2714). Under that version the probes were taken at the last regular checkpoint, step 2500, not at the stop step. Their \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still falling when training stopped.

\subsection{Probe targets}

Linear probes read five quantities from the residual stream at every layer:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{distance}
\item relative \texttt{velocity}
\item \texttt{energy}, which is constant per system
\item physical \texttt{force}, $|F| = m_2/r^2$
\item the per-snapshot acceleration \texttt{accel\_step}:
\end{itemize}

\[|a_{\text{step}}| = \frac{F}{m_2}\,\Delta t^2 = \left(\frac{2\pi}{N}\right)^2 \frac{a^3}{(1+m_2)\,r^2}.\]

Next-step prediction needs \texttt{accel\_step}, not the physical force. A weak \texttt{force} probe on its own could therefore mean the model learned a per-snapshot version of gravity, not that it learned no gravity. For this reason \texttt{force} and \texttt{accel\_step} are always compared.

A unit test checks two properties of \texttt{accel\_step}:

\begin{itemize}
\item It equals the second difference of positions, to within 1\%.
\item It is not proportional to the physical force across orbit sizes.
\end{itemize}

\texttt{velocity} probe results do not appear in any cited finding.

\subsection{Probe protocol and random-model controls}

Probes run on 300 held-out systems, at every other time step from $t = 8$. Systems are split 70/30 into probe-train and probe-test sets, so rows from the same orbit never appear on both sides.

Each probe is a ridge regression (RidgeCV over $\alpha \in [10^{-3}, 10^{4}]$) on standardised inputs and outputs. $R^2$ is averaged across output dimensions.

Every probe has a control: the same probe fitted on a randomly initialised model of the same architecture. There is one control per layer, because a random model does not change across checkpoints. A probe result counts only if

\[R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}} \geq 0.1 \quad (\texttt{probes.control\_margin}).\]

For each target, the lab reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the best layer
\item its $R^2$ and control $R^2$
\item \texttt{formed\_at\_step}, the first checkpoint at which the margin reaches the threshold (null if it never does)
\end{itemize}

The control $R^2$ for \texttt{accel\_step} is high under the default ranges (0.71–0.75). This caps the margin \texttt{accel\_step} can reach. One finding therefore also reports a post hoc, headroom-normalised score, $(R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}})/(1 - R^2_{\text{control}})$, but its pre-registered criterion uses the raw margin [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].

Probes on the out-of-distribution evaluation sets have no random controls. Their $R^2$ values therefore cannot be read as margins [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}, \texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

Only the final checkpoint's activations are saved to disk, as float16. Activations for earlier checkpoints are recomputed from the saved weights when needed.

\subsection{Symbolic regression}

When \texttt{probes.symbolic} is enabled, PySR fits a closed-form expression to the best-layer probe direction for \texttt{force} and for \texttt{accel\_step}. The \texttt{accel\_step} fit uses the inputs $r$, $m_2$ and $a$. Each fit reports:

\begin{itemize}
\item the equation
\item whether the true law was recovered (\texttt{law\_recovered})
\item \texttt{fit\_error\_vs\_true\_law}
\end{itemize}

Symbolic fits were run at the final checkpoint only, and only in the runs behind [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. These are the only evidence runs with fitted equations.

\subsection{Activation patching}

The pipeline also supports activation patching. Heads are ranked by a force probe on each head's contribution to the residual stream. The top three heads, plus the lowest-ranked head as a control, are each patched with that head's output on a different system, and the relative increase in next-step MSE is recorded. None of the cited findings report patching results.

\section{Results}

The lab has six citable findings, all replicated across two seeds: three supported and three refuted. They rest on 12 evidence runs. The budget hard stop cut 2 of those runs short. All models share one architecture ($L = 4$, $d = 64$, $N = 64$) [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}, \texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. Each probe result below counts only if its $R^2$ beats a random-init control of the same architecture by at least 0.1.

\subsection{Prediction quality}

Every trained model beat constant-velocity extrapolation on held-out two-body systems.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Final in-distribution \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio}:}
\item About 0.011 in the default-range runs [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}].
\item 0.0114 and 0.0119 with $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item 0.0151 and 0.0157 in the restricted-eccentricity transfer runs [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}].
\item 0.0178 and 0.0187 with $e \in [0, 0.8]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\item 0.0137 and 0.0128 with $e \in [0, 0.9]$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{The dense-checkpoint runs ended higher,} at 0.141 and 0.166 [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{When structure forms}

\textbf{Supported: gravity-related structure forms before the model beats the baseline} [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}, \texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{250-step checkpoints (seeds 2 and 3)} [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}]:
\item \texttt{accel\_step} already beat its control at the first checkpoint, step 250. Its margins were 0.158 and 0.127 at layer 4.
\item \texttt{distance} margins at the same checkpoint were 0.597 and 0.681 at layer 1.
\item \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} first fell below 0.1 only at step 1500 (0.0858 and 0.0985).
\item \textbf{25-step checkpoints (seeds 4 and 5)} [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}]:
\item In both seeds the \texttt{accel\_step} margin first reached $\geq 0.1$ at step 100 (0.115 and 0.108, layer 4).
\item At that point \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still 1.96 and 1.84. It was first reported below 1 at step 175 (0.736 and 0.878).
\item \texttt{distance} and \texttt{energy} formed at step 25, while \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still between 5 and 83.
\end{itemize}

!\href{figure:formation/run-20261011-195457-2ec2}{Formation trajectory of probe margins and val\_mse\_ratio, seed 2 (250-step checkpoints)}

!\href{figure:formation/run-20261011-202258-3cf9}{Formation trajectory of probe margins and val\_mse\_ratio, seed 4 (25-step checkpoints)}

\textbf{Force never formed before \texttt{accel\_step}.}

\begin{itemize}
\item Seed 2: both formed at step 250. Seed 3: force formed at step 750, \texttt{accel\_step} at 250 [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}].
\item Seed 5: force formed at step 450, \texttt{accel\_step} at 100. Seed 4: force never formed, with a final margin of 0.068 [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Caveats on timing.}

\begin{itemize}
\item The \texttt{accel\_step} margins at the crossing are only just above the threshold (0.108–0.158). With a 0.12 threshold, the dense-run crossing would move to about step 175 and coincide with \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} < 1 rather than come before it [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item Seed 5's \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} rose back to 1.057 at step 225, so "first beats baseline" is not a stable transition [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item Both trajectories skip some checkpoints [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}, \texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item Early \texttt{distance} and \texttt{energy} signal may be geometry that is easy to read from the input embedding, not learned dynamics [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}].
\item Forming early is a correlation in time. It does not show that the model uses this structure to predict [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Force versus \texttt{accel\_step}}

The physical force is $|F| = m_2/r^2$. Next-step prediction needs the per-snapshot acceleration, $|a_{\text{step}}| = (2\pi/N)^2\, a^3/((1+m_2)\, r^2)$.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Raw $R^2$:} in every evidence run \texttt{accel\_step} has the higher raw $R^2$ (0.395–0.935, against 0.154–0.738 for force).
\item \textbf{Margin over control:} which probe leads depends on the configuration (table below).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Refuted: widening the mass range does not make force fall behind \texttt{accel\_step}} [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Prediction:} with $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$, the force probe would fall behind \texttt{accel\_step}.
\item \textbf{Result:} force kept the larger margin in both seeds (0.243 and 0.235, against 0.205 and 0.175). Its margins were no lower than in the default-range runs (0.223–0.230).
\item \textbf{The criterion is biased against \texttt{accel\_step}.} Its control $R^2$ is already 0.71–0.75, which leaves little room for a margin. Measured as a fraction of available headroom, $(R^2 - R^2_{\text{control}})/(1 - R^2_{\text{control}})$, \texttt{accel\_step} leads: 0.70 vs 0.48 in seed 2 and 0.71 vs 0.42 in seed 3.
\item \textbf{What this shows:} some force-specific signal exists beyond the control. It does not show that force is the primary representation [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Where each quantity is read out.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{accel\_step} is best at layer 4 in every evidence run.
\item \texttt{energy} is best at layer 1 in every evidence run.
\item \texttt{distance} is best at layer 1 everywhere except seed 16, where it is best at layer 2 [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item Force has no stable best layer. It is best at layer 4 in some seeds and layer 1 in others [\texttt{f-20261011-201533-0653}, \texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}, \texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}, \texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}, \texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}, \texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item Force and \texttt{accel\_step} are collinear through $1/r^2$, and \texttt{distance} margins are large (0.64–0.70 in the wide-mass runs). Part of the force signal may therefore be distance information [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Out-of-distribution transfer}

\textbf{Refuted: training on restricted eccentricity transfers to high eccentricity} [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]. In seeds 7 and 6, in-distribution \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was 0.0151 and 0.0157.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Low mass ratio ($m_2 \in [0.01, 0.1]$): passed.} \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was 0.057 and 0.055, about 3.6× in-distribution. Force $R^2$ held at 0.75–0.77.
\item \textbf{High eccentricity ($e \in [0.5, 0.8]$): failed the pre-registered 10× criterion.} \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was 0.270 and 0.249, about 16–18× in-distribution. Force $R^2$ fell to 0.40 and 0.34, from 0.74 and 0.69.
\item \textbf{All two-body ratios stayed below 1.}
\item \textbf{Three-body ratios of 72 and 70 are exploratory only.}
\item \textbf{OOD probe values have no random controls,} so the force $R^2$ drop cannot be read as a margin.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Supported: covering $e \in [0, 0.8]$ in training removes the high-eccentricity gap} [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Early stop:} both runs (seeds 9 and 8) were cut short by the budget hard stop, at completion 0.867 and 0.9. Their probes describe the weights at the stop, and \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was still falling. Both runs are above the 80\% completion bar, so they count toward replication.
\item \textbf{Prediction gap closed:} held-out high-$e$ \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} was 0.0187 and 0.0189, about 1.05× and 1.01× in-distribution. The pre-registered threshold was 3×. Held-out low-$e$ systems scored 0.0185 and 0.0215, so high $e$ was no worse.
\item \textbf{Probes still beat their controls} (table above).
\item \textbf{Probe gap remained:} the best force probe on held-out high-$e$ systems reached only 0.366–0.377, against 0.729–0.802 on low-$e$ systems, even though prediction error was the same on both [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Symbolic recovery of the law}

\textbf{Refuted: wide eccentricity coverage yields an $r^{-2}$ law from the probe directions} [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}]. Seeds 16 and 17 were trained with $e \in [0, 0.9]$ and $m_2 \in [0.01, 1]$. Symbolic regression on the best \texttt{accel\_step} direction gave $r^{-1}$ in both seeds:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Seed 16:} $0.0103\,a^2/r$, with error against the true law 0.71.
\item \textbf{Seed 17:} $0.00277\,\big((a+0.685)^3/r - r\big)$. This is roughly $a^3$ but still $r^{-1}$, with error 0.62.
\item \textbf{Force fits:} $m_2/r + 0.40$ and $(m_2 + 0.60)/r$, with errors 0.90 and 0.64.
\item \textbf{No fit recovered the law.}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Probe quality fell while prediction held.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{val\_mse\_ratio} stayed at 0.0137 and 0.0128.
\item \texttt{accel\_step} raw $R^2$ fell to 0.395 and 0.503, from 0.92–0.95 at $e \leq 0.5$. It still beat its control, with margins 0.220 and 0.321.
\item The force margin was 0.097 in seed 16, below the 0.1 threshold, and 0.106 in seed 17.
\item The $r^{-1}$ result may come from a linear probe failing to follow the heavy-tailed $1/r^2$ target near pericentre, not from the model encoding an $r^{-1}$ quantity.
\item These are the only evidence runs with symbolic fits [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Summary of null and negative results}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Three hypotheses were refuted:}
\item Widening $m_2$ did not make force fall behind \texttt{accel\_step} [\texttt{f-20261011-203628-5cd9}].
\item Restricted-$e$ training did not transfer to high $e$ within 10× [\texttt{f-20261011-203723-b7de}]. A follow-up traced this to training coverage [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}].
\item Symbolic fits gave $r^{-1}$, not $r^{-2}$ [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{No explicit $1/r^2$ law has been extracted} [\texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{Force sometimes never beats its control} (seeds 4 and 16) [\texttt{f-20261011-203659-4e2f}, \texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{Linear decodability and prediction come apart at high eccentricity} [\texttt{f-20261011-211551-d718}, \texttt{f-20261011-223027-8fb3}].
\item \textbf{Not yet reported:} no \texttt{velocity} probe results and no activation-patching results.
\item \textbf{Limits that apply throughout:} all evidence uses linear probes, two seeds per finding and one architecture.
\end{itemize}
